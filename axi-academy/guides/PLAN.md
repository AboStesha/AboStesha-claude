## Course 1: الفوركس من الصفر

### L01: ما هو الفوركس؟
**Video:** forex = foreign exchange; exchanging currencies; rates change as currencies strengthen or weaken; John's travel example; learn before trading.

**Guide:**
- **How the market is built.** It is OTC with no central exchange. The participants are central banks, commercial banks (the interbank market), funds and institutions, companies hedging, and retail traders through brokers and liquidity providers.
- **A short history.**
  - The gold standard.
  - Bretton Woods in 1944: fixed rates, with the USD convertible to gold.
  - The end of convertibility in 1971.
  - Floating rates from 1973.
  - Online retail trading from the late 1990s and 2000s.
- **ISO 4217 codes.** How the three letters are built. Tables of major and regional currencies: code, Arabic name, country and symbol. Include the Arab currencies: IQD, SAR, AED, KWD, QAR, BHD, OMR, JOD, EGP. Add market nicknames such as Greenback, Cable, Aussie, Kiwi, Loonie, Swissy and Fiber.
- **Exchange-rate regimes.**
  - Free float.
  - Managed float.
  - Pegs: SAR 3.75 to the USD since 1986, AED 3.6725, QAR 3.64, BHD 0.376, OMR 0.3845, JOD 0.709, and HKD in a band of 7.75–7.85.
  - KWD is tied to a basket.
  - IQD has an official rate set by the Central Bank of Iraq, and the market rate can differ. Give no figure.
- **What makes a currency stronger or weaker.** Keep it short: interest rates, inflation, growth, trade and capital flows, risk sentiment. The details belong to L16.
- **Exchanging money vs. trading forex.** A bank or exchange office converts real money. A broker lets you trade price changes, usually through CFDs with leverage.
- **Worked conversions.** Use rates written both ways (1 EUR = 1.10 USD, and its inverse), plus an exchange office's buy and sell rates. Use hypothetical numbers.

### L03: كيف تتداول الفوركس؟
**Video:** pairs, AUD/USD = 0.7000, buy if you expect the pair to rise, sell if you expect it to fall. The video says the pair names are "in the file below".

**Guide:**
- **Reading a quote precisely.** Base and quote currency. Direct and indirect quotes. Inverting a quote (1/price).
- **The full pair lists**, in tables with the symbol, the Arabic reading and the nickname:
  - **Majors (7):** EUR/USD (Fiber), USD/JPY, GBP/USD (Cable), USD/CHF (Swissy), AUD/USD (Aussie), USD/CAD (Loonie), NZD/USD (Kiwi).
  - **Crosses and minors:** EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY, GBP/JPY, EUR/CHF, AUD/JPY, EUR/AUD, GBP/CHF, AUD/NZD, CAD/JPY, NZD/JPY, EUR/CAD, GBP/AUD, AUD/CAD, CHF/JPY and others.
  - **Exotics:** USD/TRY, USD/ZAR, USD/MXN, USD/SGD, USD/HKD, USD/NOK, USD/SEK, USD/PLN, USD/CNH, EUR/TRY.
  - Which pairs are offered depends on the broker.
- **Why some quotes start with USD and others don't.** The priority convention: EUR > GBP > AUD > NZD > USD > CAD > CHF > JPY.
- **What buying a pair really means.** You buy the base currency and sell the quote currency at the same time. Profit or loss is counted in the quote currency. Worked buy and sell examples in money, with 10,000 units and exact arithmetic.
- **Cross rates.** Example: EUR/JPY = EUR/USD × USD/JPY.
- **Pairs that share a currency often move together or in opposite directions,** for example EUR/USD and USD/CHF. Keep this short.

### L07: الرافعة المالية (Leverage)
**Video:** borrowing; the car example at 1:10; a $1,000 balance at 1:100; leverage on its own doesn't change the result of a trade; higher leverage needs less margin; losses grow too; be careful.

**Guide:**
- **Notional value vs. margin.** Effective leverage = total position value ÷ equity, compared with the account's maximum leverage. This is the key idea the video doesn't cover.
- **Table of leverage ratio ↔ margin %:** 1:10 = 10%, 1:20 = 5%, 1:30 = 3.33%, 1:50 = 2%, 1:100 = 1%, 1:200 = 0.5%, 1:500 = 0.2%, 1:1000 = 0.1%.
- **Retail limits under ESMA, FCA and ASIC** (source and year):
  - 30:1 major FX
  - 20:1 non-major FX, gold and major indices
  - 10:1 other commodities and non-major indices
  - 5:1 shares
  - 2:1 crypto
  - Other regulators differ, and the limit depends on the entity.
- **How a price move turns into an account move.**
  - A table of price move % × effective leverage = account move %.
  - The adverse move that wipes out the equity ≈ 1 ÷ effective leverage.
- **Choosing leverage sensibly.** Keep the effective leverage low, combine it with a stop loss and position sizing (L15), and know that negative balance protection depends on the regulator.
- **Worked examples:**
  - a $1,000 account with $50,000 of positions
  - the same trade at three effective leverages
  - how far the price can move before a margin call (rough)

### L08: الهامش (Margin)
**Video:** margin = a deposit; margin = trade size ÷ leverage; base and quote currency; 0.1 lot of EUR/USD at 1:100 = $100; margin call; stop out; margin level = equity ÷ used margin × 100, with the example 1,000/500 = 200%.

**Guide:**
- **Every account figure precisely.** Balance; equity = balance + floating P/L; used margin; free margin = equity − used margin; margin level; and how each one moves while trades are open.
- **Margin-call level vs. stop-out level.** Typical examples are 100% and 50%, and they depend on the broker. The stop out usually closes the largest losing position first.
- **Margin for other instruments.** Gold: lots × 100 × price ÷ leverage. Indices and shares: a margin %. When the account currency is different from the quote currency, convert.
- **Worked examples:**
  - EUR/USD 0.5 lot at 1.0850 and 1:100 → $542.50
  - USD/JPY 1 lot at 1:100 → $1,000
  - an account with a floating loss: balance $2,000, used $542.50, P/L −$300, which gives equity $1,700, free margin $1,157.50 and a margin level of about 313%
  - how far the price can fall before the stop out
- **Habits that protect your margin.** Check the margin level before adding trades, and don't use all the free margin.

### L09: جني الأرباح (Take Profit)
**Video:** a TP closes the trade automatically at the target; the Facebook example 150 → 160.

**Guide:**
- **Ways to choose a TP:** a fixed number of pips, support and resistance, earlier highs and lows, round numbers, a multiple of the risk (1:2, 1:3), ATR. Taking part of the profit at several levels. The trailing stop as a moving exit.
- **The maths of risk:reward and win rate.** The win rate you need to break even = 1 ÷ (1 + R). Table: R=1 → 50%, 1.5 → 40%, 2 → 33.3%, 3 → 25%.
- **Which price triggers a TP.**
  - A buy closes at the Bid, so its TP triggers when the Bid reaches it.
  - A sell closes at the Ask, so the spread matters.
  - A TP usually fills at the level or better; this depends on the broker's execution.
- **Setting and moving a TP on MetaTrader.** The order window, and dragging the line on the chart.
- **The psychology.** Moving the TP out of greed, and closing too early out of fear.
- **Worked examples.** A stop of 30 pips and R=2 → a target of 60 pips, and its money value with the pip value from L13. A sell with a TP below the entry. A partial exit.

### L10: وقف الخسارة (Stop Loss)
**Video:** SL definition; the Facebook example 150 → 140, a $10 loss; it protects you in volatility; always use one.

**Guide:**
- **Types of stop loss:** fixed, technical (beyond a support or resistance level or a swing point), volatility-based (ATR), time-based, trailing, and moving the stop to breakeven.
- **Placing the stop.** For a buy, below the swing low. For a sell, above the swing high. Too tight vs. too wide, and why obvious round numbers are a risk.
- **What actually happens when a stop triggers.**
  - It becomes a market order, so slippage and weekend gaps can fill it at a worse price.
  - A guaranteed stop-loss order exists at some brokers, for a fee.
  - A buy's stop triggers on the Bid and a sell's stop on the Ask, so a wider spread can hit a sell's stop.
- **The stop sets the risk.** Risk = pips to the stop × pip value × lots. Details in L15.
- **Common mistakes:** moving the stop further away, trading without a stop, placing it too close, removing it before news.
- **Worked examples.** A technical stop under a swing low, with its distance in pips. A gap filled through the stop (hypothetical). Moving the stop to breakeven.

### L11: سعر الشراء والبيع
**Video:** two prices; Ask (العرض) = the price you buy at and Bid (الطلب) = the price you sell at; decide whether you expect a rise or a fall; the Apple example of setting a price and getting filled when the market reaches it.

**Guide:**
- **Why there are two prices.** Market makers and liquidity providers. The spread as the cost, and what makes it wider or narrower.
- **What the chart shows.** A MetaTrader chart shows the Bid by default. You can show the Ask line in chart properties.
- **Which price triggers what:**
  - a Buy Limit or Buy Stop triggers on the Ask
  - a Sell Limit or Sell Stop triggers on the Bid
  - a buy position's SL and TP trigger on the Bid
  - a sell position's SL and TP trigger on the Ask
  - Use a table.
- **Points vs. pips on 5-digit quotes, and the spread in money.** Spread in pips × pip value × lots.
- **Execution.** Market execution vs. instant execution. Requotes. Slippage, which can go either way. Depth of market in MT5.
- **Worked examples.** A spread of 1.2 pips on 1 lot in money. Why a sell position starts with a loss equal to the spread. A Sell Limit that triggers when the Bid reaches it.

### L12: رسوم التبييت (Swap)
**Video:** the swap is interest for holding a trade overnight, to cover the broker lending you money; charged at 17:00 New York; none if you close before then; none at weekends, hence triple swap on Wednesday; it can be positive; it varies by instrument; check the platform.

**Guide:**
- **Where the swap comes from.** The interest-rate difference between the two currencies, plus the broker's markup, which is why long and short swaps differ. A simplified idea of the calculation.
- **The carry trade.** The idea and its risks.
- **Reading the specification.** Swap type (points, money or %), swap long, swap short, the 3-day swap day.
- **Converting swap points to money.** Swap points × point value × lots × nights. Worked example: −5.2 points on EUR/USD 1 lot → −$5.20 a night, and −$15.60 on the triple day.
- **Rollover time in Baghdad.** 00:00 when the US is on summer time and 01:00 in winter.
- **Broker-specific details.** Some instruments charge the triple day on Friday. Holidays.
- **Swap-free accounts.** Their conditions and fees, and that they vary by broker. Add «للأسئلة الشرعية راجع أهل الاختصاص».
- **Worked examples.** A position held from Monday to Thursday. Comparing the cost of a long and a short.

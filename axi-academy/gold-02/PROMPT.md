The second gold lesson uses a **different style** from lesson 01. Lesson 01 was glass, floods and chat. This one follows the grammar of the reference *Prompt_Motion_3* (the LismCSS 1.0 release film):
- a light documentation page;
- a small section label top-left;
- crisp flat diagrams that morph into one another;

What ties it together from the first frame to the last is **one gold thread**. The logo shrinks into its seed, and the thread then runs through every frame. It is:

## Information check

Every claim in the narration was checked:
- **2025 records:** the LBMA PM gold price set **53 new all-time highs in 2025** (World Gold Council, *Gold Demand Trends: Full Year 2025*).
- **March 2020:** WGC noted that gold was sold, together with other assets, to raise cash and cover losses and margin calls in the March-2020 «dash for cash». Gold still ended March roughly flat.
- **The dollar:** gold is priced in USD worldwide.
  - A stronger dollar tends to weigh on gold, and a weaker one tends to support it. This is a tendency, not a fixed rule.
  - Hypothetical example: if USD rises 5% against another currency, an ounce at an unchanged $4,000 costs 4,000 → 4,200 units of that currency (+5%).
- **Rates:** the Fed sets the short-term policy rate, and the market sets real yields (nominal yield minus expected inflation). Gold pays no interest.
  - Higher real yields raise the opportunity cost of holding gold and often weigh on it.
  - WGC has noted that the relationship sometimes breaks down, as it did in 2022–2024.
- **2022:** US inflation was high (CPI peaked at 9.1% y/y in June). Yet gold ended 2022 roughly where it began in USD, about $1,800/oz (WGC). Real yields and the dollar rose that year.
- **Central banks (WGC estimates):** net purchases were above 1,000 t in each of 2022, 2023 and 2024, and about 863 t in 2025.
- **Investment:** bars, coins and ETFs together reached a record 2,175 t in 2025, beating the 1,805 t record of 2020 (WGC).
- **Mine supply:** mine supply is about 3,600 t a year against roughly 216,000 t of gold above ground — under 2% a year.
- **Calendar:**
  - US CPI and the Employment Situation (jobs) report are monthly releases from BLS.
  - The FOMC holds eight scheduled meetings a year.
  - Dates can change, so check the official Fed and BLS calendars.
- **1980 → 2008:** gold peaked at $850 (London PM fix, January 1980) and fell to about $253 in 1999, a drop of about 70%. It did not trade above $850 again until January 2008, more than 20 years later, in nominal terms (without adjusting for inflation).
- **Illustrative shapes:** the on-screen paths for 2025, March 2020, 2022 and 1975–2010 are stylised through these real landmarks. They carry no price axis.

At 08:12 PM the agenda says “Official Project Reveal — cinematic reveal video with immersive light & sound.” We propose to make that video the most talked-about five minutes of the night.

What it is. A film plays on the wall. A performer on stage has rehearsed to it frame by frame, so the screen appears to answer their hands: a line follows a pen, water pours into a picture, a cloth reveals what is behind it.

Why it works. The audience cannot tell where the performer ends and the screen begins. That gap is where the applause, and the phones, come out.

What it must be. Unmistakably Budoor Signature: black, gold and green, a signature, a piano, and a city growing out of nature.

02 — What we learned from the referencesWhy the world believed it

House of Experience
Hermès × Les French Twins

A line-drawn horse and carriage. The performer pulls a real silk scarf out of the drawing, then the drawing becomes a real horse walking out of a split in the screen.

  • Line art hides the seam. A drawn world forgives a 3-frame miss; photoreal does not.
  • Something leaves the screen. The scarf is the proof that it is “real”.
  • The impossible thing comes last. One trick you cannot explain, saved for the end.
Darvis × Autumn Lake

A red cloth repaints a car, balls are thrown “into” the screen, a man steps out of a screen doorway, and rain falls on real umbrellas.

  • The hand leads, the screen follows. Every effect starts a few frames after the touch.
  • One trick, three sizes. Small, medium, then the whole wall.
  • End on the client. The last frame is always the logo, and the room is on its feet.

03 — Making it read as liveThe sync playbook

House of Experience
Technical rehearsal: the wall shows the numbered touch-point grid, the floor carries the matching marks, the show caller runs everything from one timecode.
D-15Storyboard and animatic approved; performer starts rehearsing to a 1:1 projection. D-7Final render on the LED pixel map; cue sheet, click track and lighting cues locked. D-1Technical test and full dress rehearsal on the real wall, as in the event timeline.
1

One clock

Video, lighting, fog, confetti and the click all run from one SMPTE timecode on the media server. Nothing is fired by hand.

2

The hand leads

Every effect starts 6 to 10 frames after the touch, never before. Late is invisible; early breaks the spell.

3

Nine marks

Touch points are taped on the floor and mapped to the pixel map of the wall, so “where the hand is” is a number, not a guess.

4

Hold frames

Each trick has a loopable hold the operator can park on if the performer is a second late. The audience never sees it.

5

The camera decides

Every move is blocked for the hero angle: centre, 12 m back, phone height. A 9:16 crop of the master is cut for social the same night.

6

Rehearse to the frame

Fifteen days of content and training, two days on the real wall. The performer counts music, not seconds.

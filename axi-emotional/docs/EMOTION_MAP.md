# Axi Iraq · Emotional brand ad · Emotion map (round 2, 60.000 s)

- **Why:** client feedback on draft 1 (`docs/SCRIPT.md`). It had only one positive feeling. The client wants the trader's whole emotional life, from the very darkest feelings to the very brightest, so that every trader thinks "this is me, I really felt this" and shares it. «الغربة» and «الوحدة» are out. The film may run longer: **60.000 s**.
- **Music:** the 60 s edit `src/music/ad_music_60.wav` and its grid `src/music/grid_60.json` (128 BPM, 32 bars; see MUSIC.md §60 s edit). Every time below is a grid time: the stabs, the breath, the LIFT at 30.000, crash 1, the F-major step-up, crash 2, the D-minor phrase, G, the fill and the C-major arrival at 52.500.
- **Fixed rules:** these still apply.
  - Copy: white MSA with no diacritics, at most 5 words per line and 2 lines per beat, Western digits, Latin on its own line.
  - Claims: nothing that promises profit, guarantees anything, implies Axi trades or decides for you, or removes risk or emotion [FACTS 9.1–9.4]. Never «لن تمشي وحدك».
  - Risk strip: in the last 3 s at least, verbatim from `axi-iraq-copytrading/SCRIPT.md` §4.
  - Cast: the five approved faces only, one per shot.

## 1. The legend the client asked for, and what the writers agree on

| Who | Why | On screen? |
|---|---|---|
| **Mark Douglas**, *The Disciplined Trader* (1990) and *Trading in the Zone* (2000) | The most-cited writer on what traders feel. He nearly lost everything in his first year as a broker. His framework has four primary fears (being wrong, losing, missing out, leaving money on the table), the market as a neutral mirror, and the "zone": acting without hesitation and at peace with any outcome | **No.** In copyright, and naming him would imply he endorses Axi. Ideas only, paraphrased |
| **Jesse Livermore**, through *Reminiscences of a Stock Operator* (Edwin Lefèvre, 1923) | The legend. The most-quoted trading book in the world. It has the most direct emotional language: "sick, nervous, upset", "ashamed to go out", "the damage to the pocketbook and to the soul", "the real joy… at last on the right track" | **Yes, one line** (§4). It is the only book of these in the public domain. Credit the book, never Livermore by name |
| **Van K. Tharp**, *Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom* and *Super Trader* | It all happens inside you. A mistake means breaking your own rules, repeating it is self-sabotage, and you trade well only from acceptance or higher | No. Ideas only. Never echo "financial freedom" [9.11] |
| Supporting voices: Ed Seykota and the traders in Jack Schwager's *Market Wizards* (Paul Tudor Jones, Kovner, McKay, Eckhardt), Brett Steenbarger, Denise Shull, Nassim Taleb, Alexander Elder | Real accounts of panic, near-quitting and euphoria, the clinical vocabulary of self-talk and "fear of future regret", and the beginner who feels "brilliant and invincible" | No. Research only |

**What they all agree on, and how it shapes the film:**
- The darkest feelings come from fear and from the trader's own hand: hope that keeps a losing trade open, rage, shame, self-betrayal.
- The brightest feeling is **not the high of winning.** Douglas treats euphoria as being as dangerous as fear. The true peak is calm.
- Axi's own psychology pages say the same [FACTS 3.4–3.10], so the honest arc is also the compliant one. The bright side is about **how the trader stands**, never about money.

**How the quotes were checked:** every Lefèvre line below was checked word for word against Project Gutenberg #60979 (the 1923 George H. Doran text), and each chapter was read off the text's own chapter markers. The three research notes disagreed on several chapter numbers; the numbers here are the checked ones.

## 2. The shape: a false peak, a fall, a hinge, a climb

**The order of the film:** a false peak (the high) → the fall through 7 dark feelings to the darkest → silence → the hinge on the LIFT → a climb through 6 bright feelings to the brightest → the legend's line → Axi.

That gives **14 feelings: 7 dark, 7 bright**, and one of the bright ones is the dangerous high. On the 128 BPM grid, the 16 bars of darkness (0–30) are exactly mirrored by the light after the lift.

**Each persona gets a small arc of their own**, with a dark beat and its bright twin:

| Persona | Dark → bright | The rhyme |

| **Saeed** | the drop (E02) → rage (E05) → acceptance (E09) | He stops dead in E02 and steps forward in E09 |
| **Noor** | hope that hurts (E03) → self-doubt (E07) → the quiet (E14) | The same Tigris composition, at night and then at sunrise |

| **Aesha** | shame (E06) → quiet pride (E11) → self-trust (E13) | The smile she puts on for others becomes a smile just for herself |

**Camera grammar (from draft 1):**
- Before 30.0 the camera never moves *with* a trader. It is locked off, or it drifts or retreats on a long lens.
- From the LIFT it moves forward and then walks beside each trader.
- The first look into the lens is Aesha's in E11, when she shares her private pride with the viewer.

**E01 · The high: "I thought I'd figured it out"** · felt as +7, and it is a trap
- **Time:** 0.000–4.688. E drone into the A-minor pad. The hook runs to 2.813, then the line.
- **Persona and plate:** Sarmad · `Sarmad/sa_165949.png` (night river with towers). Paint out the green chart lines on the towers and in their reflections. Keep him a few steps back from the water.
- **Moment:** He reads the red phone (screen turned away) and laughs quietly to himself, head tipped back to the towers, as if the city were his. Hold one beat too long. On 4.688 one tower light in the reflection goes out, and the haptic buzz cuts us into E02.
- **Lines:** «ظننت أنك فهمت السوق / كله» · «أجمل شعور / وأخطره»
- **Grounding:**
  - Lefèvre, ch. XIII: "A great many smashes by brilliant men can be traced directly to the swelled head--an expensive disease everywhere to everybody, but particularly in Wall Street."
  - Douglas: euphoria after a run of wins is as dangerous as fear, because it wipes out the sense of risk.
  - Research only: Elder's beginner who feels "brilliant and invincible"; Eckhardt (in Schwager): what feels good is often the wrong thing to do.
- **Rule:** the high never appears without its danger. The very next frame is the drop. No money, P/L, green or celebration.

**E02 · The drop: one notification and everything stops** · −6
- **Time:** 4.688–7.500. The pad continues.
- **Persona and plate:** Saeed · `Saeed/se_155202.png` (glass towers in fog).
- **Moment:** He walks toward the camera reading the phone. The buzz. He stops dead mid-stride and stops breathing. His free hand goes flat to his chest while the fog keeps drifting past him. The sound drops to one heartbeat.
- **Lines:** «إشعار واحد / ويتوقف كل شيء» · «سقط السعر / وسقط قلبك معه»
- **Grounding:**
  - Lefèvre, ch. IX, on the 1907 crash: the men who, "dreading the pain of a small loss at the beginning, were now about to suffer total amputation--without anaesthetics".
  - Douglas: the mind reads neutral market information as a threat, so the body reacts before thought. This is his "fear of losing".
  - Axi [3.6]: "the fear of loss and making mistakes".

**E03 · Hope that hurts: "it will come back tomorrow"** · −7

  - Douglas: the mind blocks information that threatens it, so the trader doesn't see an exit that is plainly there.
  - Tharp: the classic failure is not taking the loss at the exit you set in advance.
  - The beat also carries the sleepless night. Lefèvre, ch. X, retells Dickson G. Watts: "I can't sleep… It is wearing me out."
- **Rule:** shown as pain, never as advice. No stop-loss UI.

**Why this line:**
- It names the two poles of the whole film in one sentence.
- A 100-year-old voice tells every trader "this is human".
- It agrees with Axi's own "It is impossible to eliminate emotions in trading" [3.4], so it strengthens the "acknowledge, never cure" rule.
- It sets up Axi's answer, «نعرف هذا الشعور».

**Why the closing card, not the opening:**
- Frame 0 must be a moving face, the hook and the lockup.
- The line works as the conclusion of everything before it.
- It keeps the book far from the darkest beats. Livermore went broke several times and took his own life in 1940, and commenters may bring that up.

**Risks for Legal and the brand manager:**
- Is any quote acceptable in an ad? Crediting a book, not a person, avoids implied endorsement.

## 8. Copy and compliance check

- **Length:** every line is at most 5 words, with at most 2 lines per beat. The Latin credit and the brand Latin sit on their own lines, as in draft 1's B16. There are no diacritics and no digits, apart from "1923" in the Latin credit.
- **Gender:**
  - The lines over the women are past tense, nouns, or forms that read as "she" as well as "you" when written without diacritics: تقول، تكن، تضحك، تدخلها، تثق، تسمع.
  - The one generic-masculine line is E05's «تريد أن تنتقم», and it sits over Saeed.
  - Draft 1 claimed that present-tense lines are gender-neutral. That is not strictly true, so this map prefers the past tense.
- **Grammar to proofread:**
  - «السوق لم يتغير» uses the masculine «سوق», as draft 1 did. «لم تتغير» is also correct.
  - Check that the client reads «أخلفت وعدك» as plain MSA. The alternative is «ثم كسرت وعدك».
- **No profit words:** no «ربح» and no money shown. «المال» appears only as something lost (E07, option 2).
- **No cure claim:** no «لا خوف» [9.4].
- **No advice:** no line tells anyone to trade, wait, exit or size [9.3]. Revenge and hope are shown as pain. No Axi line answers a loss with a recovery.
- **Euphoria** is always followed by its danger, the drop.
- **Douglas and Tharp** are never named or quoted on screen. Douglas's "95% of errors" figure is unverified and not used.
- **Self-harm:** no reading in any dark beat. Check every take for edges, water and heights.

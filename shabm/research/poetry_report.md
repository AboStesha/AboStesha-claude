## 1. THE WORD شَبِم / شَبَم — ROOT, MEANING, FORMS

### 1.1 Root and core sense

**Root: ش‑ب‑م (sh‑b‑m).**

The core, classical, *narrow* meaning is **the coldness of water** — not cold in general.

**Lisān al-ʿArab** (Ibn Manẓūr, d. 711 AH / 1311 CE), مادة «شبم» — retrieved text:

> **«الشَّبَمُ: بَرْدُ الماءِ. وماءٌ شَبِمٌ: بارِدٌ.»**
>
> **«يُقالُ: ماءٌ شَبِمٌ، ومَطَرٌ شَبِمٌ، وغَداةٌ ذاتُ شَبَمٍ. وقد شَبِمَ الماءُ، بالكَسْرِ، فهو شَبِمٌ.»**

Attestations quoted inside the same entry:

> **«وفي حَديثِ جَريرٍ: خَيْرُ الماءِ الشَّبِمُ، أيِ البارِدُ.»**
>
> **«وفي حَديثِ زَواجِ فاطِمَةَ عليها السلام: دَخَلَ عليها النبيُّ ﷺ في غَداةٍ شَبِمَةٍ.»**
>
> **«قيلَ لابْنَةِ الخُسِّ: ما أَطْيَبُ الأَشْياءِ؟ قالَتْ: لَحْمُ جَزورٍ سَنِمَةٍ، في غَداةٍ شَبِمَةٍ، بِشِفارٍ خَذِمَةٍ، في قُدورٍ هَزِمَةٍ.» — أرادَتْ: في غَداةٍ بارِدَةٍ.**

And — **this one is brand-critical, read it twice**:

> **«والعَرَبُ تُسَمِّي السَّمَّ شَبِماً، والمَوْتَ شَبِماً، لِبَرْدِهِ.»**
> *"The Arabs call poison* shabim *and death* shabim*, because of its coldness."*

Sources for the Lisān text: [Lisān al-ʿArab, حرف الشين، شبم — IslamWeb library, vol. 8](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/122/4117/%D8%B4%D8%A8%D9%85) · [Lisān al-ʿArab «شبم» — Dorar al-Iraq wiki mirror](https://wiki.dorar-aliraq.net/lisan-alarab/%D8%B4%D8%A8%D9%85) · [al-Maʿānī al-Jāmiʿ, aggregated lexicon entry «شبم»](https://www.almaany.com/ar/dict/ar-ar/%D8%B4%D8%A8%D9%85/) · [al-Bāḥith al-ʿArabī «شبم»](https://www.baheth.info/find/web/%D8%B4%D8%A8%D9%85)

### 1.2 al-Qāmūs al-Muḥīṭ and Tāj al-ʿArūs

**al-Qāmūs al-Muḥīṭ** (al-Fīrūzābādī, d. 817 AH / 1414 CE):

> **«الشَّبَمُ، مُحَرَّكَةً: البَرْدُ.»**

**Ibn Sīdah** (al-Muḥkam, d. 458 AH), narrowing it:

> **«الشَّبَمُ: بَرْدُ الماءِ.»**

**Abū ʿAmr (al-Shaybānī)**, a secondary sense:

> **«الشَّبِمُ: الذي يَجِدُ البَرْدَ مَعَ الجُوعِ.»**
> *"The* shabim *is one who feels cold together with hunger."*

**Tāj al-ʿArūs** (al-Zabīdī, d. 1205 AH / 1790 CE) reproduces the Qāmūs headword and adds the Azharī/Ibn Sīdah material. Sources: [Hawramani Arabic Lexicon «شبم» — aggregates Lisān, Qāmūs, Tāj, Ṣiḥāḥ, Lane](https://arabiclexicon.hawramani.com/%D8%B4%D8%A8%D9%85/) · [Tāj al-ʿArūs — Shamela](https://shamela.ws/book/7030) · [al-Qāmūs al-Muḥīṭ — Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/QamusMuhit)

### 1.5 How شَبِم differs from its neighbours — the actual brand argument

This table *is* your brand story. شَبِم is not a synonym for "cold." It is the one Arabic word that means **the specific coldness that belongs to water.**

| Word | Vocalised | Precise classical sense | Register / feel | Fit for SHABM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **شَبِم** | شَبِمٌ | **cold specifically of water / rain / a morning.** «الشَّبَمُ: بَرْدُ الماءِ» | High classical, poetic, essentially unused in modern speech | ★★★★★ — the whole point |
| **بارِد** | بارِدٌ | cold — of *anything*: weather, food, a person, a joke, a war | Everyday, neutral, generic; also "flat/insipid" of a joke, "cold-hearted" of a person | ★☆☆☆☆ — generic to the point of invisibility |
| **قارِس** | قارِسٌ | **biting, punishing** cold — of weather | Harsh, hostile. (Note the widespread error **قارِص**, which means "sour/stinging"; the correct form is **قارِس**) | ★☆☆☆☆ — cold as *suffering*, wrong emotion for a treat |
| **صَرْد / صَرِد** | صَرْدٌ / صَرِدٌ | cold; also **pure, unmixed** («حُبٌّ صَرْدٌ» = pure love, «شَرابٌ صَرْدٌ»); صَرِد = someone who feels cold easily | Archaic, ambiguous, also a bird name (الصُّرَد, the shrike) | ★★☆☆☆ — the "purity" sense is nice, the ambiguity is not |
| **زُلال** | زُلالٌ | **«ماءٌ عَذْبٌ بارِدٌ صافٍ سَهْلُ المُرورِ في الحَلْقِ»** — sweet, cold, clear, easy down the throat; more broadly "the clear part of anything" | Beautiful, poetic, still semi-current; **but** in modern Arabic زُلال also = *albumin* (زلال البيض / زلال البول), a medical/urinalysis word | ★★★★☆ — excellent as a **product-line name** (e.g. the still/juice line), weaker as the master brand |
| **قُرّ / قَرّ** | القُرُّ | the cold of winter | Archaic, weather-bound | ★☆☆☆☆ |

### ✅ VERSE 1 — أبو الطيب المتنبي — **AUTHENTIC**

**(a) Authentic?** Yes. It is one of the ten most famous lines in the entire Arabic canon.
**(b) Attribution correct?** Yes — Abū al-Ṭayyib al-Mutanabbī (303–354 AH / 915–965 CE).
**(c) Correct wording** — exactly as the founder supplied it:

> **وا حَـرَّ قَلْبـاهُ مِمَّـنْ قَلْبُـهُ شَـبِمُ ❊ وَمَـنْ بِجِسْمـي وَحالـي عِنْـدَهُ سَـقَمُ**

**(d) The poem.** The opening (maṭlaʿ) of al-Mutanabbī's most celebrated qaṣīda of reproach to **Sayf al-Dawla al-Ḥamdānī**, written after the courtiers' slander drove a wedge between poet and prince — the poem immediately preceding his departure from Aleppo.
- **Metre:** البَسيط (al-basīṭ)
- **Rhyme letter (الرَّوِيّ):** **الميم** — a **ميمِيَّة**; rhyme is *muṭlaqa* (‑mu), of the *mutarākib* type
- **Length:** ~38 lines
- **Contains the even more famous:** «الخَيْلُ وَاللَّيْلُ وَالبَيْداءُ تَعْرِفُني ❊ وَالسَّيْفُ وَالرُّمْحُ وَالقِرْطاسُ وَالقَلَمُ»

**(e) Meaning.**
- عربي: **يَشْكو الشَّاعِرُ حُرْقَةَ قَلْبِهِ مِنْ مَحْبوبٍ قَلْبُهُ بارِدٌ لا يَعْبَأُ بِهِ، وَجِسْمُهُ سَقيمٌ مِنْ أَجْلِهِ وَهُوَ عِنْدَهُ هَيِّنٌ.**
- English: *"Oh the burning of my heart — for one whose own heart is cold; one for whom my body and my whole condition are mere sickness."*

**Brand read.** The most powerful possible line for an ice brand, because it turns the brand word into a pun the whole Arab world already knows by heart: هُوَ **شَبِم**، وَأَنْتَ تَحْتَرِق. Written on a frozen cup it is instantly witty. Its literary context is a *complaint*, but nobody reads it that way any more — it functions as an idiom for icy indifference. **Approved for packaging.**

Sources: [aldiwan.net — واحر قلباه ممن قلبه شبم، المتنبي](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem10521.html) · [بوابة الشعراء — المتنبي، واحر قلباه](https://poetsgate.com/poem.php?pm=415) · [Arabic Wikipedia — «وا حر قلباه»](https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%88%D8%A7_%D8%AD%D8%B1_%D9%82%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%87) · [مشروع الإحياء — شرح رائعة المتنبي كاملة](https://alehyaa.pages.dev/2019/06/blog-post_24) · [Mawdoo3 — تحليل قصيدة المتنبي (واحر قلباه)](https://mawdoo3.com/%D8%AA%D8%AD%D9%84%D9%8A%D9%84_%D9%82%D8%B5%D9%8A%D8%AF%D8%A9_%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%AA%D9%86%D8%A8%D9%8A_(%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AD%D8%B1_%D9%82%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%87)) · [شرح ديوان المتنبي، قافية الميم — Hindawi/Safaḥāt](https://www.safahat.org/books/82959503/21/)

### ❌ VERSE 2 — الشريف الرضي — **NOT ATTESTED**

> «وَإِنَّ شِفَاءَ النفسِ ماءٌ شَبِمٌ ❊ يُبَرِّدُ مِنْ لَوْعَاتِها وَيُبَلِّلُ»

**(a) Authentic?** No evidence. **(b) Attribution?** Unsupported. **(c) Correct wording?** N/A.

Exact-string searches on both hemistichs returned **zero** hits in any poetry corpus. al-Sharīf al-Raḍī (359–406 AH / 970–1015 CE) is exhaustively digitised (aldiwan.net, ʿĀlam al-Adab, Balāgha.net, Hindawi); a line this quotable would be everywhere.

**Additional structural tell:** the two hemistichs do not share a clean rawī with each other in the way a real bayt does — «شَبِمٌ» / «يُبَلِّلُ» is a lām rhyme paired against a mīm-final ṣadr, which is fine in principle, but the whole line reads as a modern paraphrase of the *idea* of Mutanabbī's شبم rather than a classical bayt.

**Verdict: DELETE.** Sources searched: [aldiwan.net — ديوان الشريف الرضي](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem30189.html) · [عالم الأدب — الشريف الرضي](https://adabworld.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D8%B6%D9%8A/) · [Balāgha.net — شعر الشريف الرضي](http://arabic.balaghah.net/content/%D8%B4%D8%B9%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D8%B6%D9%8A)

*(If you want a real al-Raḍī water line — he is a Baghdadi, which suits you — the genuinely famous one is* **«الماءُ في ناظِري وَالنارُ في كَبِدي ❊ إِنْ شِئْتِ فاغْتَرِفي أَوْ شِئْتِ فاقْتَبِسي»** *— see [ʿĀlam al-Adab](https://adabworld.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B8%D8%B1%D9%8A-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B1-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D9%83%D8%A8%D8%AF%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%81-%D8%A7/). But it is a lover's-tears line, not a cold-water line — I would not force it onto a cup.)*

### ❌ VERSE 3 — الأخطل — **NOT ATTESTED**

> «بِمَاءٍ شَبِمٍ مِنْ سَحابَةٍ مُعْصِرٍ ❊ يُعِلُّ بِهِ بَعْدَ المَنامِ الحَناجِرا»

**(a) Authentic?** No evidence — zero hits for `"بماء شبم من سحابة معصر"` and zero for `"يعل به بعد المنام الحناجرا"`.

**(c) What it is actually a corruption OF.** This is the most instructive of the seven fakes. The verb-phrase **«يُعَلُّ بِهِ»** is lifted from a genuinely famous Imruʾ al-Qays passage:

> كَأَنَّ المُدامَ وَصَوْبَ الغَمامِ ❊ وَريحَ الخُزامى وَذَوْبَ العَسَلْ
> **يُعَلُّ بِهِ بَرْدُ أَنْيابِها** ❊ إِذا النَّجْمُ وَسْطَ السَّماءِ اسْتَقَلّْ

*(variant second hemistichs circulate: «وَنَشْرَ القُطُرْ» / «إِذا طَرَّبَ الطائِرُ المُسْتَحِرْ»)*
— [aldiwan.net, امرؤ القيس، «كأن المدام وصوب الغمام»](https://www.aldiwan.net/poem97597.html)

So the fabricator took Imruʾ al-Qays's **يُعَلُّ بِهِ**, bolted on Kaʿb b. Zuhayr's **شَبِم** register, and signed it *al-Akhṭal*. **DELETE.**

*(Also note: al-Akhṭal (d. c. 92 AH/710 CE) was a Christian Taghlibī famous above all as a **wine poet**. Attaching your Muslim-market brand to his name carries an extra, separate reputational cost even if a real line existed.)*

### ❌ VERSE 4 — عنترة بن شداد — **NOT ATTESTED, AND THE «خمر vs خير» QUESTION IS MOOT**

> «وَسَلاَفَةٍ مِنْ خَمْرِ بَيْسَانَ عَتَّقَتْ ❊ بِمَاءٍ شَبِمٍ مِنْ سَحَابٍ مُصَوَّبِ»

You specifically asked me to check **«خَمْرِ بَيْسانَ» vs «خَيْرِ بَيْسانَ»**. Here is the complete answer.

**(a)/(b) The verse is not in ʿAntara's Muʿallaqa, and this is provable from prosody alone — no manuscript needed.**

ʿAntara b. Shaddād's Muʿallaqa («هَلْ غادَرَ الشُّعَراءُ مِنْ مُتَرَدَّمِ»), the so-called **الذَّهَبِيَّة**:
- **Metre:** الكامِل (al-kāmil)
- **Rhyme letter:** **الميم** (‑mi) — it is a **ميمِيَّة**
- **Length:** **79 lines**
— [Arabic Wikipedia, معلقة عنترة بن شداد](https://ar.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%85%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A9_%D8%B9%D9%86%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A9_%D8%A8%D9%86_%D8%B4%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AF) · [Arabic Wikisource](https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/%D9%85%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%82%D8%A9_%D8%B9%D9%86%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A9_%D8%A8%D9%86_%D8%B4%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AF) · [شرح المعلقات السبع للزوزني — Shamela](https://shamela.ws/book/11253/278)

The supplied line rhymes in **‑bi (مُصَوَّبِ)**. **A bāʾ rhyme cannot occur in a mīm qaṣīda.** It is categorically not from the Muʿallaqa. Full stop.

**(c) Nor is it attested anywhere else.** Zero hits for the full line, for `"وسلافة من خمر بيسان"`, for `"وسلافة من خير بيسان"`, and for `"من سحاب مصوب"` in ʿAntara's dīwān or elsewhere.

**What ʿAntara's Muʿallaqa actually says about wine** (the real passage, so you can see what got pastiched):
> وَلَقَدْ شَرِبْتُ مِنَ المُدامَةِ بَعْدَما ❊ رَكَدَ الهَواجِرُ بِالمَشوفِ المُعْلَمِ
> بِزُجاجَةٍ صَفْراءَ ذاتِ أَسِرَّةٍ ❊ قُرِنَتْ بِأَزْهَرَ في الشِّمالِ مُفَدَّمِ

There is **no شبم anywhere in the Muʿallaqa**.

**On «خمر بيسان» as a phrase:** it *is* a genuine classical collocation — a shāhid **«مِنْ خَمْرِ بَيْسانَ تَخَيَّرْتُها ❊ تَرْياقَةً تُوشِكُ فَتْرَ العِظامْ»** appears in *Sharḥ al-Shawāhid al-Shiʿriyya fī Ummahāt al-Kutub al-Naḥwiyya* ([Shamela, p. 197](https://shamela.ws/book/17712/1157)). But that is **a different verse by a different (unconfirmed) poet**, not ʿAntara's, and it too is explicitly about wine.

**🚩 BRAND FLAG — and it is bigger than the wording question.**

The «خمر / خير» debate is a distraction. **Both readings are unusable**, because:

1. The verse does not exist in ʿAntara's authenticated corpus at all;
2. **سُلافة** itself means *the first, purest pressing of wine*. Even reading «خَيْرِ بَيْسانَ» ("the best of Baysān"), the noun the line hangs on is still a wine-term. You cannot launder a wine verse by swapping one word;
3. Baysān (Beit Sheʾan) wine is famous in the *khamriyyāt* tradition specifically. The association is fixed.

For a Baghdad-launched, Saudi/UAE/Kuwait-franchising, Muslim-market F&B brand, printing a *sulāfa* verse on a cup is a self-inflicted wound: a single screenshot with the caption «شركة تكتب بيت خمر على الكاسة» is all it takes in the Gulf. **DELETE, and do not look for a "clean variant." There isn't one.**

### ❌ VERSE 6 — البحتري — **NOT ATTESTED; IT IS A CORRUPTED ḤASSĀN B. THĀBIT**

> «وَسَاقٍ يَسْقِينَا بِمَاءٍ شَبِمٍ ❊ يُصَفَّقُ بالرَّاحِ الزُّلالِ المُنَسَّقِ»

**(c) What it is really made of.** «يُصَفَّقُ بِالرَّاحِ...» is a mangling of one of the most famous hemistichs in Umayyad-era praise poetry, by **حسّان بن ثابت** (not al-Buḥturī), on the Ghassānids of Damascus:

> **يُسْقَوْنَ مِنْ وَرْدِ البَريصِ عَلَيْهِمُ ❊ بَرَدى يُصَفَّقُ بِالرَّحيقِ السَّلْسَلِ**

— confirmed at [Sharḥ al-Shawāhid al-Shiʿriyya, Shamela p. 272](https://shamela.ws/book/17712/815) and [Lisān al-ʿArab, مادة «سلسل»](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/122/3929/%D8%B3%D9%84%D8%B3%D9%84).

The fabricator kept **يُصَفَّقُ**, swapped **الرَّحيق** → **الرَّاح** (both = wine), swapped **بَرَدى** (the Damascus river) → **ماء شبم**, and re-signed it al-Buḥturī. Zero corpus hits for the supplied line.

**Double flag:** the substitute word **الرَّاح** *also* means wine. Even the fake is a wine line. **DELETE.**

*(al-Buḥturī, 206–284 AH / 821–897 CE, does have a magnificent, wine-free, world-famous water poem — see the replacement list, §3.)*

### ❌ VERSE 7 — ذو الرمة — **NOT ATTESTED**

Zero hits for `"على شبم من ماء"` in any Dhū al-Rumma poem; zero for the second hemistich. Every search for `"على شبم من ماء"` re-routes to Kaʿb b. Zuhayr's genuine «شُجَّتْ بِذي شَبَمٍ مِنْ ماءِ مَحْنِيَةٍ» — i.e. **this line is a re-write of Kaʿb's, with «مَحْنِيَة» (a bend in a valley) swapped for a nonsense «آجِيَة».**

### ❌ VERSE 8 — العباس بن الأحنف — **NOT ATTESTED, AND GRAMMATICALLY IMPOSSIBLE**

> «فَلَوْ أَنَّ ما بِي بالصَّفا أَلانَهُ ❊ وَلَوْ أَنَّ ما بِي بالمِياهِ الشَّبائِمِ»

### ⭐ 3.1 — كعب بن زهير — *the single best line for this brand*

> **شُجَّتْ بِذي شَبَمٍ مِنْ ماءِ مَحْنِيَةٍ ❊ صافٍ بِأَبْطَحَ أَضْحى وَهْوَ مَشْمولُ**

- **Poet:** كعب بن زهير بن أبي سُلمى (d. 26 AH / 645 CE)
- **Poem:** **«بانَتْ سُعادُ»** — *the* **قصيدة البُردة**, the ode Kaʿb recited before the Prophet ﷺ, for which he was pardoned and given the Prophet's own mantle
- **Metre:** البَسيط · **Rhyme letter (الرَّوِيّ): اللام** — a **لامِيَّة**
- **Position:** line 4 of the qaṣīda
- **Gloss from the commentaries:** **شُجَّتْ** = مُزِجَتْ (was mixed/diluted, passive) · **ذي شَبَمٍ** = ماءٍ بارِدٍ · **مَحْنِيَة** = مُنْعَطَفُ الوادي (a bend in the wadi) · **أَبْطَح** = مَسيلٌ فيه دِقاقُ الحَصى (a pebbled watercourse) · **مَشْمول** = ضَرَبَتْهُ ريحُ الشَّمالِ فَبَرَدَ وَصَفا (struck by the north wind, so it turned cold and clear)
- **عربي:** **ماءٌ بارِدٌ صافٍ مِنْ مُنْعَطَفِ وادٍ، بَرَّدَتْهُ ريحُ الشَّمالِ في بَطْحاءَ مِنَ الحَصى.**
- **English:** *"Mingled with cold water from a bend in the valley — clear, in a pebbled watercourse, chilled by the north wind."*

**Why it is the right line.** It is (i) the only classical verse where شبم is used *exactly* as your product is: cold, clear water as an object of pleasure; (ii) attached to the most beloved and most safely Islamic qaṣīda in the language; (iii) already the standard *shāhid* cited by Lisān al-ʿArab itself under the entry شبم — so quoting it doubles as proof that your brand name is real Arabic.

**⚠️ One caveat, disclose it internally.** The *preceding* line (line 3) is «تَجْلو عَوارِضَ ذي ظَلْمٍ إِذا ابْتَسَمَتْ ❊ كَأَنَّهُ مُنْهَلٌ **بِالرَّاحِ** مَعْلولُ» — which does contain الرَّاح (wine). Your quoted line is clean and stands alone perfectly, and pack copy shows one line, not two. But if you ever publish a "read the full poem" QR code, know what is one line up.

### ⭐ 3.2 — ابنة الخُسّ — rhymed prose, *perfect* for a winter/seasonal pack

- **Form:** سَجْع (rhymed prose), four cola all rhyming ‑imah

### 3.4 — الصنوبري — the snow line (safe, charming, no wine, no lament)

> **أَظَنَنْتَ ذا ثَلْجاً؟ وَذا ❊ وَرْداً مِنَ الأَغْصانِ يُنْفَضْ**
> **وَرْدُ الرَّبيعِ مُلَوَّنٌ ❊ وَالوَرْدُ في كانونَ أَبْيَضْ**

- **Poet:** أبو بكر الصَّنَوْبَري — أحمد بن محمد بن الحسن بن مرار الضبي الحلبي الأنطاكي (d. 334 AH / 945 CE), the ʿAbbāsid poet who **pioneered snow description in Arabic** (رائد وصف الثلج)
- **Metre:** مَجْزوء الكامِل · **Rhyme letter: الضاد** (‑aḍ), a **ضادِيَّة**
- **English:** *"Did you take this for snow? — No: it is roses shaken from the branches. Spring's rose is many-coloured; but in **Kānūn**, the rose is white."*
- **عربي:** **يَجْعَلُ الثَّلْجَ وَرْداً أَبْيَضَ يَتَساقَطُ مِنَ الأَغْصانِ في عُمْقِ الشِّتاءِ.**

**Why use it.** Pure winter imagery, zero risk, and it hands you a ready-made **seasonal/limited-edition** platform: كانون (Kānūn = December/January in the Levantine-Iraqi month names, which Baghdadis use daily) as the name of your winter menu. «الوَرْدُ في كانونَ أَبْيَض» is a genuinely great line on a white cup.

**Confidence: medium-high.** The attribution to al-Ṣanawbarī and his status as the snow-poet are well documented; I recommend a specialist confirms the exact wording against the printed dīwān (تحقيق إحسان عباس) before print.

### 3.5 — البحتري — the real al-Buḥturī, replacing the fake one

> **كَأَنَّما الفِضَّةُ البَيْضاءُ سائِلَةً ❊ مِنَ السَّبائِكِ تَجْري في مَجاريها**

- **Poet:** أبو عُبادة الوليد بن عُبيد البُحْتُري الطائي (206–284 AH / 821–897 CE)
- **Poem:** **«البِرْكَةُ الحَسْناء»** / بِرْكَةُ المُتَوَكِّل — «يا مَنْ رَأى البِرْكَةَ الحَسْناءَ رُؤْيَتُها ❊ وَالآنِساتِ إِذا لاحَتْ مَغانيها»
- **Metre:** البَسيط · **Rhyme letter: الهاء** (‑īhā), a **هائِيَّة**
- **English:** *"As if white silver, running molten from the ingots, flowed along its channels."*
- **عربي:** **يُشَبِّهُ الماءَ الجارِيَ الصافِيَ بِالفِضَّةِ المُذابَةِ تَنْسابُ في مَجاريها.**

**Why use it.** Molten-silver water is *exactly* your visual identity — a transparent, liquid, light-refracting block. It is one of the most anthologised descriptive lines in Arabic (it sits in the Jordanian and Emirati school curricula, so Gulf franchisees' customers will recognise it). ### 🚫 3.7 — TWO TEXTS YOU WILL BE TEMPTED BY. DO NOT USE THEM.

**(a) «خَيْرُ الماءِ الشَّبِمُ» — the "hadith". DO NOT PRINT AS HADITH.**

This is the most seductive line imaginable for you — *"the best water is the cold"* — and it is quoted **inside Lisān al-ʿArab's own شبم entry** as «وفي حديث جرير: خير الماء الشبم». The full narration: the Prophet ﷺ asks Jarīr b. ʿAbdillāh al-Bajalī about his homeland; Jarīr describes «نَحْنُ بِأَكْنافِ بَيْشَةَ بَيْنَ سَلَمٍ وَأَراكٍ...» and the reply is «خَيْرُ الماءِ الشَّبِمُ، وَخَيْرُ المالِ الغَنَمُ، وَخَيْرُ المَرْعى الأَراكُ وَالسَّلَمُ».

**But: al-Albānī graded it مَوْضوع — FABRICATED.** It is recorded by Ibn Qutayba in *Gharīb al-Ḥadīth* and by al-Daylamī in *Musnad al-Firdaws*, but it does not stand as a Prophetic tradition.

**Consequence:** printing it with «قال رسول الله ﷺ» would be, in Gulf markets, a far worse offence than a mis-attributed verse — it is attributing a fabricated saying to the Prophet. **Absolute prohibition.**
*Permissible narrow use:* as a **proverbial Arab saying**, framed as «مِنْ كَلامِ العَرَبِ» with no Prophetic attribution whatsoever. Even then, I would not: the association is too well known and someone will screenshot it as a hadith anyway. **Recommendation: drop it entirely.**

Sources: [مجلس الألوكة — «خير الماء الشبم…» تخريج وحكم الألباني: موضوع](https://majles.alukah.net/showthread.php?t=211344) · [Lisān al-ʿArab «شبم»](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/122/4117/%D8%B4%D8%A8%D9%85) · [المعجم الكبير، إسلام جرير بن عبد الله البجلي](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/84/21143/)

**(b) Qurʾān 38:42 — «هَٰذَا مُغْتَسَلٌ بَارِدٌ وَشَرَابٌ». DO NOT PRINT ON PACKAGING.**

Sūrat Ṣād, verse 42 (the healing of Ayyūb/Job): **«ارْكُضْ بِرِجْلِكَ ۖ هَٰذَا مُغْتَسَلٌ بَارِدٌ وَشَرَابٌ»** — *"Strike with your foot: here is a cool bath and a drink."* It is the most beautiful cold-water phrase in the Arabic language and it is **exactly** your product.

**And it is unusable on a disposable cup.** Qurʾānic text on single-use packaging that will be thrown in a bin, dropped on a pavement or used to wipe a table is a serious offence to a very large share of your Iraqi, Saudi and Gulf customers, and a guaranteed complaint to municipal and religious authorities in KSA. This is not a marginal sensitivity; it is a category rule.

*Permissible narrow use:* engraved on a permanent fixture — a wall, a brass plate, a non-disposable object — and even then, run it past a local scholar in each market first. **My recommendation: don't. The upside is small and the downside is a closure order.**

Source: [تفسير ابن كثير، سورة ص آية ٤٢ — quran.ksu.edu.sa](https://quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/katheer/sura38-aya42.html) · [سورة ص، الآية ٤٢](https://surahquran.com/aya-42-sora-38.html)

## 4. NAME COLLISION & BRAND-CONFUSION RISK

### 🔴 4.1 THE BIGGEST PROBLEM — «شبم» IS THE MODERN ARABIC MEDICAL TERM FOR **PHIMOSIS**

This is the finding that matters most in this entire dossier, and it is not in the founder's brief.

In contemporary standardised Arabic medical terminology, **الشَّبَم** is the accepted equivalent of **phimosis** (tightening of the foreskin), alongside **تَضَيُّق القُلْفَة** and **الجُلاع**.

**Severity assessment.**

- **Discovery risk: HIGH.** An Arabic-speaking customer, journalist, franchisee or investor who types **شبم** into Google or Arabic Wikipedia lands on a urology page. This will happen. Repeatedly. From day one.
- **Everyday-speech risk: LOW-MEDIUM.** شبم is *not* a colloquial word for phimosis in Iraqi or Gulf dialect — nobody says it in a café. It is a written, dictionary-and-clinic register word. So you will not be laughed at across the counter; you will be ambushed on a search results page.
- **Competitive-weaponisation risk: MEDIUM-HIGH.** A rival, or simply a bored Twitter/X account, can post the Arabic Wikipedia screenshot next to your logo. That is a very cheap attack with a very high engagement ceiling.

**This does not have to kill the name.** But it must be *managed deliberately*, and the deck must show investors you found it before they did. Mitigations, in priority order:

1. **Always vocalise the wordmark as شَبِم (kasra on the bāʾ), never شَبَم (fatḥa).** The medical term is consistently **الشَّبَم** (fatḥa). The poetic adjective is **شَبِم** (kasra). One diacritic is genuine, meaningful separation — and it is also the form used in «ماءٌ شَبِمٌ», the collocation your whole story rests on. *This is the single most important typographic decision in the brand.*
2. **Never let the bare word travel alone.** Lock it up permanently: **شَبِم · بَرْدُ الماء** (SHABIM · the cold of water). The definition IS the disambiguator. This is precisely why §3.3 (the Lisān line on every pack) earns its place.
3. **Win the SERP.** Buy/secure `shabim.com`, register consistent handles, and publish a "the word شَبِم" story page early so that within 6–12 months an Arabic search for «شبم كافيه» / «شبم مشروبات» returns you, not urology. You will never outrank Wikipedia for the bare string — you don't need to; you need to own every *modified* query.
4. **Brief your PR and franchise team with a prepared, confident answer** — "شَبِم is the classical Arabic word for the coldness of water, cited in Lisān al-ʿArab and used by al-Mutanabbī and Kaʿb b. Zuhayr; the medical homograph is a modern coinage from the same root's 'contraction' sense." Owning it disarms it.

### 🟠 4.2 CLASSICAL NEGATIVE SEMANTICS — poison and death

From Lisān al-ʿArab itself: **«والعَرَبُ تُسَمِّي السَّمَّ شَبِماً، والمَوْتَ شَبِماً، لِبَرْدِهِ.»** — *the Arabs call poison* shabim *and death* shabim*, for their coldness.*

And Abū ʿAmr's gloss: **«الشَّبِمُ: الذي يَجِدُ البَرْدَ مَعَ الجُوعِ»** — *one who feels cold together with hunger* (i.e. cold + hungry = destitute).

**Severity: LOW-MEDIUM.** These are lexicographers' notes, not live usage; no Arabic speaker hears "poison" when they hear شبم. But they are on the first page of the dictionary entry you yourself will be citing, so an adversarial reader will find them. **Disclose in the deck's risk section rather than let it be discovered.** It is also, frankly, a slightly delicious piece of brand mythology if you ever want an edgy limited edition — but I would not lead with it in a family café.

### 🔴 4.3 SHIBAM (شِبام) — TWO SEPARATE COLLISIONS, ONE OF THEM ACTIVE IN YOUR CATEGORY

**(i) The place.** **شِبام حَضْرَمَوْت**, Yemen — walled city, inscribed on the **UNESCO World Heritage List in 1982** (Site 192, "Old Walled City of Shibam"), universally nicknamed **"the Manhattan of the Desert"** for its 4–8-storey mud-brick towers, mostly rebuilt after 1532, on a fortified rectangular grid; described by UNESCO as one of the oldest and best examples of urban planning based on vertical construction — the first city on earth with a vertical masterplan.

**In Arabic script the collision is weak** — شَبِم and شِبام are visually and phonetically distinct (two syllables vs. two syllables with a long ā; different second vowel; final ـم vs ـام). An Arabic reader will not confuse them.

**(ii) The active brand — and this is the real problem.** **Shibam Coffee Co.** is a live, growing **Yemeni-coffee café chain in the United States**, explicitly named after the Yemeni city — "named after Shibam, the world's oldest skyscraper city."

### 5.2 Which Arabic form should the brand actually BE?

Both شَبِم and شَبَم are written **شبم** unpointed, so this is a decision about vocalisation and pronunciation, not spelling.

| | **شَبِم** (shabim) — adjective | **شَبَم** (shabam) — noun |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | *cold* (of water) — "ماءٌ شَبِمٌ" | *the coldness of water* |
| In the poetry | ✅ This is the form in **both** surviving verses (Mutanabbī «قَلْبُهُ شَبِمُ»; and Kaʿb's is «ذي شَبَمٍ» = the noun) | ✅ Kaʿb's form |
| Collision with medical term | ✅ **Distanced** — the medical term is consistently **الشَّبَم** (fatḥa) | ❌ **Direct hit** — identical to the phimosis term |
| Latin form | **SHABIM** — distinct from SHIBAM | **SHABAM** — one letter from SHIBAM |
| Sound in English | /ˈʃæ.bɪm/ — crisp, closes on a bright vowel | /ʃəˈbɑːm/ — heavier, and mis-stress-prone |

### ✅ 5.3 THE RECOMMENDATION

**Arabic wordmark: شَبِم — always vocalised, kasra on the bāʾ.**
**Latin wordmark: SHABIM.**
**Pronunciation guide for franchisees and press: SHA-bim /ˈʃæ.bɪm/, stress on the first syllable.**

**Why:**

1. **It solves the phimosis problem for free.** The medical term is الشَّبَم; yours is شَبِم. One diacritic, real separation, defensible in any argument.
2. **It solves the Shibam Coffee problem for free.** SHABIM vs SHIBAM is a full vowel apart in both letters and sound, and Latin readers will not slide from one to the other the way they would from SHABAM.
3. **It is the form in al-Mutanabbī's line** — the single most quotable asset the brand owns. «قَلْبُهُ شَبِمُ». Your name is literally in the most famous hemistich you will ever print.
4. **It is pronounceable on sight** by English, French and Turkish speakers — which "SHABM" is not.

### 5.4 What to reject, and why

| Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **SHABM** *(the founder's current draft)* | ❌ **Reject as the primary Latin wordmark.** No vowel between B and M; English/French speakers cannot pronounce it on sight and will guess "sha-BEM", "SHA-bum" or "shabbem". It reads as a typo or an unfinished file. It also gives you **no** stable spoken form for franchisees, radio, or word-of-mouth — fatal for a franchise business. **Keep SHBM only as a stylised monogram** — an ice-etched four-letter mark on cup lids, uniforms, the kiosk facade — never as the pronounceable name. |
| **SHABAM** | ❌ **Reject.** One letter from SHIBAM, colliding with both the UNESCO city and a live US Yemeni-**coffee** brand with a filed USPTO mark. Also identical to the phimosis vocalisation. This is the worst of the four options. |
| **SHABEM** | ❌ **Reject.** Non-standard in every transliteration system; reads /ʃəˈbɛm/; looks like a misspelling of nothing in particular; unsearchable. |
| **SHABIM** | ✅ **Adopt.** ALA-LC-correct, distinct from Shibam, distanced from the medical term, matches al-Mutanabbī, pronounceable everywhere. |

### 5.5 Packaging execution rules

1. **The lockup, everywhere, without exception:**
   **شَبِم** *(large, vocalised)* · **SHABIM** *(secondary)* · **بَرْدُ الماء / the cold of water** *(tertiary descriptor)*
2. **Never publish the Arabic unvocalised** on primary brand surfaces. The diacritic is not decoration here; it is the disambiguator and the trademark's distinctiveness argument.
3. **Vocalise every verse you print.** You are quoting classical poetry — half-pointed classical verse reads as careless, and the audience you are courting with poetry is exactly the audience that notices.
4. **Every pack carries the Lisān line** (§3.3): «الشَّبَمُ: بَرْدُ الماءِ» — لسان العرب. It is your definition, your defence and your proof of authenticity in seven words.
5. **Cite every verse on-pack**: poet name + poem name, in small type. `— كعب بن زهير، «بانت سعاد»`. Citation is what separates a brand that *uses* heritage from one that *decorates* with it, and it makes the founder's original error structurally impossible to repeat.
6. **Domains/handles to secure now:** `shabim.com`, `shabim.iq`, `shabim.sa`, `shabim.ae`, plus `@shabim` on Instagram/TikTok/X, plus defensive registrations of `shabm`, `shabam`, `shbm`.

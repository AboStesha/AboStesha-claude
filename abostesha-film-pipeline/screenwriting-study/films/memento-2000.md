# Memento (2000) — screenplay study

## 0. Draft facts
- **Credit (title page, p. 1, verified):** screenplay by Christopher Nolan, based on Jonathan Nolan's short story *Memento Mori*.
- **Draft as printed:** Shooting Script. Final Draft Aug. 10, 1999; Blue Aug. 27; Pink Sept. 7; Yellow Sept. 29; Green Oct. 4, 1999. Revised pages carry colour footers and margin asterisks (471 lines).
- **Revealing fact:** Yellow and Green footers appear only on pp. 127–139 (script pp. 109A–118), the convergence and Teddy's confession. The twist was rewritten last.
- **PDF pages:** 156.
  - Screenplay: pp. 1–140 (script pp. 1–119 + 20 A-pages).
  - p. 141: blank.
  - pp. 142–156: the short story, reprinted.

  % = page ÷ 156, per the brief. The bracketed % (÷ 140, screenplay only) is the one to use for cross-script comparison.
- **Scenes:** 183 numbered in both margins, with no gaps. Six letter-prefixed inserts (A66, A128, A129, A140, B140, C140) and two OMITs give 187 shot scenes (~0.75 pp each).
- **Convention:** quotes are tagged [Q1]–[Q11] (12 uses; Q4 repeats in §9). Prop captions and production labels are named in CAPITALS as identifiers.

## 1. Format & page organization
- **Strand tag on every slug**, placed after the time of day:
  > [Q1] `1    INT. DERELICT HOUSE – DAY <<COLOUR SEQUENCE>>` (p. 2)
  > [Q2] `2    INT. MOTEL ROOM 21 – DAY ##BLACK AND WHITE SEQUENCE##` (p. 2)
- **Typography carries the strand (visible only in the PDF).** Every B&W scene is set in italics, including action, cues and dialogue. Colour scenes are in roman Courier (checked pp. 2, 20, 124). The reader feels the switch before reading the tag.
- **The tag marks the strand, not the content.**
  - Wife flashbacks are tagged colour inside colour scenes (pp. 25, 95, 131), but B&W when intercut with Jimmy's killing (pp. 122–124).
  - Sammy flashbacks get their own B&W sluglines, bridged by V.O., and return via BACK TO LEONARD IN MOTEL ROOM (pp. 38, 42, 50).
  - Flashback labels vary: INSERT FLASHBACK:, INSERT QUICK CUTS:, CUT TO FLASHBACK, FLASHBACK TO: / BACK TO SCENE: (pp. 25, 51, 95, 122–124).
- **Transitions mark strand switches.** 23 of the 24 plain CUT TO:s fall on one of the 52 colour↔B&W switches. Inside a strand, scenes follow with no transition. The rest are semantic:
  - JUMP CUT TO: (p. 42), for Sammy's repeated shock test
  - DISSOLVE TO: (pp. 66, 110)
  - WHITE OUT, for the blow to the head (p. 97)
  - CUT TO BLACK (p. 140)

  There is no MATCH, SMASH or INTERCUT. SUPER is used only for the titles (p. 2), and LATER: (×7) compresses time inside scenes.
- **CONTINUED / (MORE) / (cont'd):** full shooting-script density.
- **V.O. / O.S.:** 58 V.O. cues, in two modes:
  - B&W storytelling over the Sammy flashbacks.
  - Colour thought-V.O. of 2–6 words at moments of disorientation (pp. 51, 59, 62, 63).

  The B&W phone calls are on-screen dialogue, with (listens) ×19 standing in for the unheard caller. O.S. is used ×4.
- **Camera and production notes, used sparingly:** E.C.U.; INSERT LEONARD'S P.O.V. (fish-eye peephole, p. 52); TREMBLING, SHALLOW-FOCUS EXTREME CLOSE UPS (p. 51); (POST); LEONARD TO SUBSTITUTE (pp. 38, 109, 111).
- **Prop text gets its own centred caps line**, so we read every note over Leonard's shoulder (68 Polaroid mentions).
- **Key format line:**
  > [Q3] `We see the IMAGE OF THE STRANGLED JIMMY appear <<IN COLOUR>>(POST)` (p. 124)
- **Draft wear:** single-hash tags (p. 38), a B&W tag in colour brackets (p. 93), untagged scene 72, a drifting plate number (p. 39).

## 2. Structure map
**Architecture.** The script alternates strictly through 53 runs:
1. A one-page colour prologue: the killing, in reverse.
2. 26 forward B&W runs interleaved with 25 backward colour runs.
3. A 16-page converged colour finale (pp. 124–140).

**Run lengths and pacing.**
- Colour runs average 3.6 pp (median 3.3, max 7.6). B&W runs average 1.2 pp (median 0.8; ~32 pp in all).
- The B&W is starved early (≤1 p per run until p. 31) and swells late: 3.6 pp at pp. 108–111, then 6.1 pp at pp. 118–124. The forward line accelerates into the convergence.
- The Dodd chase (pp. 59–65) is chopped into ~1-page colour shards.

**Stitching.** Each colour run ends on the verbatim line or action that opened the previous colour run (25 of 26 seams). Each B&W run opens by repeating the previous B&W run's last line (pp. 6, 10, 15).

**Story order:** B&W 1→26, then the finale, then colour backwards, then the prologue. The finale's last image (p. 140) is the first moment of the earliest colour run (p. 112), which closes the loop.

## 3. Twist engineering
- **The twist in one sentence:** by Teddy's account (half-confirmed by a flash), the man Leonard hunts is already dead and the Sammy Jankis story is Leonard's own — his diabetic wife died of insulin he injected — so Leonard knowingly casts Teddy as the next John G., making the opening murder (pp. 2–6) a trap he sets for himself at the end (p. 137).

**Misdirection toolkit**
- **POV control: shared amnesia.**
  1. *Run length ≈ memory span* (~3.6 pp).
  2. *Every run opens on an orientation beat:* waking (pp. 38, 51, 68), an object already in hand (p. 59), mid-sprint (p. 63), looking round as if for the first time (pp. 6, 17, 39, 51, 59).
  3. *We learn only from props he reads,* so we adopt his conclusions, forged ones included. Virtually every colour scene contains him. The single peek away (Natalie awake, p. 49) is the only time we know more than he does.
  4. *Thought-V.O. narrates inference live,* and on p. 63 it reverses itself mid-chase.
  5. *Others know him while he knows no one,* so the repeated condition speech becomes a humiliation we share (p. 12).
  6. *Second-person V.O.* (pp. 2, 6), lifted from the short story, casts the viewer as *you*. Burt draws out the method: Leonard says it feels like always having just woken up, and Burt calls it all backwards (p. 13).
- **Unreliable narration in documentary dress.** A fraud investigator tells the Sammy story in B&W, with flashbacks staged like evidence. The tell is his repeated protest that he never said Sammy was faking (pp. 50–51, 109, 130).
- **Withholding by format.** (listens) hides the manipulator for 100 pages (pp. 20–120).
- **Genre and red herrings.** Noir supplies a femme fatale (pp. 89–93) and a corrupt cop (p. 128). With two villains on offer, we never look at the hero.
- **Emotional distraction.** The grief set pieces (pp. 25, 49, 65–75) make the quest feel legitimate.
- **Editing grammar.** Reverse order makes each effect plant its own cause, and the second sighting re-reads the first:
  - battered Natalie (p. 84 → 93)
  - the *dusty* glass (p. 101 → 107)
  - the door that wakes him (p. 68 → a paid escort, p. 75)
  - the mysterious lead (p. 10 → Natalie's address for the place Jimmy dies, p. 25)
- **Comic disguise.** Teddy's gags hide greed (pp. 8, 53, 58).

**The reveal:** pp. 125–138 (~13 pp, 80–88%).
- **Staging:** a two-hander confession at gunpoint, cut with two single-shot flashes: a syringe (p. 131), then the same shot flipping back to the pinch (p. 132). There is no recap montage; memory appears as contested evidence.
- **Objects corroborate:** badge (p. 126), grinning Polaroid (p. 134), file (p. 135), plate (p. 137).
  > [Q4] TEDDY: "Sammy didn't have a wife." … "It was your wife who had diabetes." (p. 131)
- **Second turn (p. 137):** agency, not information. We watch Leonard author his own deception.
- **What the audience re-reads:** the whole Sammy strand, the hand tattoo, the pinch, the blank patch, every caption, Teddy's jokes, the first scene.

**Fair-play verdict:** fair on plot, deliberately open on guilt.
- Every hard fact (bullets, suit, car, plate, bandage, missing pages, Polaroid, Teddy's surname) is on the page before the reveal.
- The Sammy-is-Leonard claim comes from a proven liar, and the flash flips back (p. 132). Each viewer decides.
- Two clues are reader-only: the substitution notes and the italic typography.
- One elision: Leonard writing DON'T BELIEVE HIS LIES is never shown, though it can be inferred.

## 4. Dialogue craft
- **Voice differentiation.**
  - *Leonard:* investigator vocabulary (facts, context, conditioning) and courtesy scripts repeated near-verbatim (the condition speech, pp. 12, 20, 104–106).
  - *Teddy:* nicknames (Lenny, Sherlock, buddy) and gags. His median speech is the longest (9 words vs Leonard's 6), and his patter turns to sermon in the reveal.
  - *Natalie:* cool, tender and vicious registers; the switching is the characterisation.
  - *Burt:* deadpan candour.
- **Subtext.**
  - p. 8: Teddy insists the grey sedan is Leonard's. It plays as a joke; it is a probe for the Jaguar's money.
  - p. 48: Natalie names Teddy while studying Leonard. Grief on the surface; a recruitment test underneath.
  - p. 121: Teddy sends him off to kill with near-fatherly affection. A hit dressed as a pep talk.
- **Exposition hiding.**
  - The condition is a running social routine (Teddy's every-time joke, pp. 7, 34; Burt's insect stare, p. 12).
  - The B&W backstory comes through a one-sided call that is itself the con.
  - The Sammy analogy *is* the exposition, and the secret.
- **Rhythm.**
  - ~856 speeches, median 6 words; 44% are five words or fewer.
  - The 34 speeches over 40 words are phone monologues, Sammy narration or Teddy's reveal.
  - Leonard has 53% of all dialogue words.
  - His name-and-origin line is cut off by a dash twice (pp. 6, 80).
  - Silence is written as action: Leonard freezes, Leonard thinks (p. 131); one scripted non-reaction [Q10].
- **Lines that change meaning after the twist.**
  - [Q5] (p. 20).
  - Conditioning works for him, said into a mirror (p. 43).
  - Actions matter even unremembered (p. 24 → 139).
  - His wife rereads a book whose pleasure, Leonard says, lies in wanting to know what happens next (p. 66) — exactly what a backward film withholds.
- **Excerpts.**
  - [Q5] LEONARD: "Sammy had no drive. No reason to make it work. … Me? I gotta reason." (p. 20) — *why it works:* his self-definition rests on the contrast the twist erases.
  - [Q6] LEONARD: "Memory can change the shape of a room or the color of a car. It's an interpretation, not a record." (p. 36) — *why it works:* the theme is posed as a professional creed in an argument he wins, and it indicts him 95 pages later.
  - [Q7] NATALIE: "Let me get you a fresh glass. I think this one was dusty." (p. 101) — *why it works:* a lie and remorse at once; p. 107 shows the spit.
  - [Q8] LEONARD (V.O.): "You're a John G.? Fine, then you can be my John G." (p. 137) — *why it works:* the twist lands as a decision in the hero's own voice.

## 5. Directing actors on the page
- **Frequency and types.** ~105 parentheticals across ~856 speeches (~12 per 100). Mostly behavioural:
  - smiles/grins ~12
  - shrugs/nods ~9
  - chuckles ~9
  - (listens) ×19
  - (beat) ×4

  Pure emotion labels are rare and spike at crises: anxious/frightened (p. 107), sudden fear (p. 123), distraught/panicked (pp. 125–126).
- **Smile as mask.** Leonard's smile cues land exactly when he admits he doesn't remember (pp. 10, 26), and when he names a man he knows only from a photo (pp. 4–5). The page performs the bluff he confesses to on p. 112.
- **Felt vs performed emotion.** Action lines script the gap:
  > [Q9] "Leonard nods in apparent recognition, wary." (p. 7)

  Likewise, Teddy pretends to think hard and affects a casual air (pp. 4, 7), and Natalie smiles unconvincingly (p. 98).
- **Non-reaction as direction:**
  > [Q10] "Leonard does not react to the name." (p. 48)

  The actor plays nothing, and the audience later reads it as a clue.
- **Business carries emotion.**
  - Natalie's hands have nowhere to go (p. 84).
  - Leonard strokes a tattoo while letting the phone ring (p. 93) and before his Sammy confession (p. 111); he picks at the bandage tape (p. 88).
  - He turns a letter opener while deciding Sammy's fate (p. 83).

## 6. Writing images, VFX & sound
- **Impossible images in plain verbs.** The reverse-motion opening never says REVERSE; reversed physics does the work:
  > [Q11] "A DEAFENING ROAR as wet red leaps off his face and suit and head, with a SPASM, reassembles itself into the face of TEDDY" (p. 2)

  The camera SUCKS the picture up; the gun leaps into his hand. Caps mark the beats, so the paragraph reads as a shot list with no camera terms.
- **Object-motivated VFX transition.** The strands converge inside a prop: a B&W Polaroid develops in colour [Q3]. The opening does the inverse, colour fading to blank. The Polaroid shows which way time runs.
- **Memory shards.** Written as TREMBLING, SHALLOW-FOCUS EXTREME CLOSE UPS: a bottle bursting on tiles, a face under a wet shower curtain, a mirror breaking (p. 51). They are re-dealt with variations (pp. 69, 95–97, 101, 122–124), ending on the wife's blink and a white-out.
- **Reading as reveal.** Mirror-written tattoos snap into legibility (pp. 19, 46).
- **Sound as the hinge: effect first, cause later.**
  - door slam (p. 68 → 75)
  - dumpster lid (p. 103 → 108)
  - car alarm (p. 76 → 88)

  The phone ring is the B&W strand's act-out (pp. 16, 68, 102). In the eyes-shut drive (p. 139), passing cars and horns replace the image.

## 7. Theme, motifs, meaning
- **Central dramatic question.** Why did Leonard kill Teddy, and was Teddy John G.? Beneath it: can a man without new memory know the truth, or will he choose a lie that gives him purpose?
- **Theme.** Memory is interpretation, and purpose can be self-authored — and therefore self-deceived.
  - Disguised early: the facts creed (p. 36), meaning without memory (p. 24), no healing without felt time (p. 49).
  - Stated by Teddy: we all lie to ourselves to be happy (pp. 129, 134).
  - Enacted by Leonard (p. 137).
  - Turned into faith in the closing mirrors V.O. (p. 140), adapted from the story (p. 152).
- **Motifs.**
  - *Polaroids* (68 mentions). The flash records; fading and developing show time's direction (pp. 2, 124). Captions are verdicts. They can't be torn, only burned (pp. 45, 137). They are kill trophies that never make memory stick (p. 134).
  - *Tattoos.* Permanent truths, dictated (p. 76) or forged (p. 137). The blank heart patch (pp. 46, 102).
  - *The other side of the bed.* Warm or cold, a clock for grief (pp. 49, 68, 95).
  - *Mirrors.* Backward tattoos (pp. 19, 46); shattered in the attack (p. 97); consulted after each self-justification (pp. 43, 111); the final V.O. (p. 140).
  - *Eyes.* The opening close-up (p. 2); the wife's blink (p. 101); driving blind (p. 139).
  - *Pens.* Hidden by Natalie (p. 92); used to sign Teddy's death warrant (p. 137).
- **Final image (p. 140).** Eyes shut, then open to swallow the town; a tattoo parlour, brakes, black, and a V.O. asking where he was. The end is the start of p. 112: the quest refuels itself, and he re-enters the loop by choice.

## 8. Lessons for a 7-minute twist/VFX short
1. **Rigid strand tags plus a typographic code** — Every slug carries COLOUR or B&W, and B&W is italicised (pp. 2, 20) — Tag every slug DREAM or LAB and italicise one strand. In 7 minutes, no reader should ever wonder which reality they are in.
2. **Object-motivated convergence** — A Polaroid develops from B&W into colour (p. 124) — Resolve the dreamed woman into the real wife inside an in-world object (a lab monitor, a photo), then continue in the real palette.
3. **Handshake overlaps** — The second sighting of a moment reveals its cause (pp. 84→93, 101→107) — Show one tender moment twice; the second time, put its clinical cause in frame.
4. **One gesture, two meanings** — The thigh pinch is love (p. 25), then an injection (p. 32), fused at the reveal (pp. 131–132) — Her touch at his temple turns out to be her placing the memory-rig electrode.
5. **Write the subliminal into the page** — LEONARD TO SUBSTITUTE (pp. 38, 109, 111) — Mark a one-frame face swap (dream woman → wedding photo) as a production note.
6. **The unseen editor of memory** — A one-sided call, (listens) ×19, rewrites a fact onto his skin (pp. 75–76) — Give the wife an intercom voice we hear only one side of. The reveal: she was rebuilding his memory, not inventing it.
7. **Orientation openings with micro thought-V.O.** — Runs open on waking, with 2–6-word V.O. (pp. 51, 59) — Start each beat at his re-orientation; one inner line keeps the audience exactly as blind as he is.
8. **Continuity clocks in parentheses** — (WITHOUT BRUISES), the bandaged arm, the broken glass, a scabby vs a fresh tattoo (pp. 8, 27, 89, 112) — Track a healing scar or a ring's tan line so viewers can reorder events afterwards.
9. **Sound first, cause later** — A slammed door wakes him (p. 68); the paid slam comes later (p. 75) — Plant a dream sound (a monitor beep, a ring on glass) and reveal its source.
10. **End on a choice, not a fact** — Leonard chooses the lie [Q8] (p. 137) — End on his choice to remember, or hers to keep rebuilding.

## 9. البطاقة العربية
- **الفيلم:** Memento (2000) · **الكاتب:** كريستوفر نولان، عن قصة قصيرة لجوناثان نولان · **عدد الصفحات:** 156 صفحة PDF، منها 140 للسيناريو والبقية نصّ القصة الأصلية.
- **التويست في جملة:** القاتل الذي يطارده ليونارد مات قبل عام بحسب رواية تيدي، وحكاية «سامي جانكيس» هي حكايته هو. لذلك يختار بوعي أن يجعل تيدي «جون جي» التالي، فتغدو جريمة المشهد الأول فخًّا نصبه لنفسه في نهاية القصة.
- **آلية التويست:**
  ١. خطّان متناوبان: الملوَّن يسير إلى الوراء، والأبيض والأسود يسير إلى الأمام. يلتقيان حين تتحمّض صورة بولارويد بالألوان (ص 124)، وتُطبع مشاهد الأبيض والأسود بخطّ مائل.
  ٢. كل مقطع ملوَّن ينتهي بالحركة نفسها التي افتُتح بها المقطع السابق، فتنقلب دلالة المشهد حين نرى سببه (ص 84←93، ص 101←107).
  ٣. زرعٌ مادّي على الورق: صور تحمل تعليقات، ووشوم، وملاحظات، وضمادة تخفي وشم «لا تردّ على الهاتف»، وملاحظة إنتاجية تستبدل سامي بليونارد (ص 38، 109، 111).
- **سرّ الحوار:**
  - جمل قصيرة (الوسيط ست كلمات)، والمونولوجات الطويلة محصورة في المكالمة الهاتفية وحكاية سامي.
  - مكالمة من طرف واحد عبر التوجيه (listens) تُخفي المتلاعب مئة صفحة حتى ص 120.
  - جمل تنقلب دلالتها بعد الكشف، كقول تيدي [Q4]: "Sammy didn't have a wife." … "It was your wife who had diabetes." (p. 131)
- **إدارة الممثل على الورق:** الأقواس سلوكية لا انفعالية، وأسطر الحدث تكتب المسافة بين الشعور والأداء (إيماءة تعرّف «ظاهري»، ص 7). بل يُكتب «اللاردّ» نفسه توجيهًا يصير دليلًا (ص 48).
- **كتابة الصورة والمؤثرات:** لا تظهر كلمة «عكسي» قط؛ تكفي أفعال الفيزياء المعكوسة: الكاميرا «تبتلع» الصورة، والدم يعود فيلتئم وجهًا (ص 2). والانتقال بين الخطّين يمرّ عبر غرض داخل العالم، كما يعمل الصوت مفصلًا بين المشاهد المعكوسة: نسمع الأثر أولًا، ثم نرى سببه.
- **٣ تقنيات نأخذها لفيلمنا القصير:**
  ١. لحظة «تحميض» بصرية داخل الكادر تحوّل صورة المرأة المتخيَّلة إلى صورة الزوجة الحقيقية، ثم يستمرّ المشهد بلوحة ألوان الواقع.
  ٢. إيماءة واحدة بمعنيين، كلمسة على الصدغ تتكشّف لاحقًا أنها تثبيت قطب جهاز الذاكرة. تُزرع مبكرًا وتُفسَّر في الكشف.
  ٣. صوت موجِّه نسمع طرفًا واحدًا منه، ثم ينكشف أنه صوت الزوجة التي تعيد بناء ذاكرته، لا صوت مَن يخترعها.

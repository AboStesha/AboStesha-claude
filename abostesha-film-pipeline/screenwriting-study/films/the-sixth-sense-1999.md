# The Sixth Sense (1999) — screenplay study

## 0. Draft facts
- **Writer:** M. Night Shyamalan, sole credit (title page, PDF p. 1). Copyright 1997, Blinding Edge Pictures, Inc.
- **Label/date:** no draft label. The date is spelled out in lowercase as September twelfth, ninety-seven. A stamped number, 030931, sits above the title. The script ends with THE END and handwritten initials (p. 127).
- **Source:** the PDF is a photocopy scan, and its OCR text layer is noisy (merged words), so automated counts undercount. All quotes below were checked against the page images.
- **Pages:** 127 PDF pages: a title page plus 126 script pages, so script p. *n* = PDF p. *n*+1. Page numbers are printed top-center and bottom-right.
- **Scenes:** unnumbered; ≈88 headings by hand count.
- **Signs of a writer's draft:** no CONTINUED, (MORE) or (CONT'D); orphaned cues at the foot of pages (p. 14, 26, 60); the antique dealer Sean becomes Jeffery partway through a scene (p. 86).

## 1. Format & page organization
- **Sluglines.** No FADE IN: the script opens cold on a slugline (p. 2). Sluglines often name a micro-location to isolate a shot: a dining table (p. 19), a bench (p. 12), a tent (p. 71, 101), a bus (p. 102). A repeat slugline marks a time cut within one place (p. 84–85, 90–91). INT/EXT is loose: a car interior is headed EXT. CAR (p. 117).
- **Transitions.** CUT TO: ≈79, closing almost every scene. DISSOLVE TO: ×6, for time or emotion (p. 12, 37, 53, 69, 71, 111). SLAM CUT: ×2, only to cut into and out of the flashback (p. 124–125). FADE TO BLACK: ×1.
- **Time labels.** The time jump is a legend in the action, not a SUPER (p. 12). The flashback is flagged by slugline prefixes, FLASHBACK: and PRESENT: (p. 124–125). Intercutting with a TV image uses bare sub-headings, T.V. SCREEN / LIVING ROOM (p. 108).
- **No V.O. at all.** Interiority stays inside the story, in dictaphone diaries (p. 36–37, 67–68). Recorded and remote voices are tagged (on tape) ×16, (video tape) ×6, (o.s.) ×6, (into phone) ×2.
- **No INSERT label.** On-screen text is indented and quoted: case files (p. 12), a translation (p. 20), a pill label (p. 53), a gift card (p. 98).
- **POV, caps and camera.** POV labels sit inline in the action (MALCOLM'S P.O.V. p. 44; COLE'S P.O.V. p. 77). ALL CAPS is used for sounds and for whole supernatural paragraphs. WE-camera language (WE PULL BACK, WE SEE, CLOSE ON) gathers at revelations (p. 12, 67, 83, 112, 124–126).
- **Character label.** The dead man is labeled MALCOLM'S GHOST only inside the flashback (p. 125).
- **Examples:** `INT. DINING TABLE - EVENING` (p. 19); `SLAM CUT:` → `FLASHBACK: INT. BEDROOM - NIGHT` (p. 124).

**Twist:** Malcolm died from Vincent's bullet (p. 11). After the time jump we follow a ghost who doesn't know he is dead.

**Who talks to Malcolm (the address audit).** After p. 12, no living, awake person except Cole addresses him. That holds across 13 scenes with living bystanders: Anna ×5, Mr. Marschal, Lynn and Dr. Hill, two school audiences, the bus, the wake, two teachers. Only four kinds of voice reach him: Cole, recordings (p. 52, 91–93, 127), sleeping Anna (p. 123–126), and dying Vincent's eyes (p. 125).

**Misdirection toolkit**
- *POV and unreliable narration:* Malcolm owns the POV shots, the diaries and the bodily sensations. The action lines report his perception as fact: he opens a door later shown to be deadbolted (p. 19, 44 vs. 124), and he is out of breath (p. 94, 113).
- *Editing grammar:* a dissolve plus a legend covers the death (p. 12). Arrivals are cut. Adjacency implies cause (p. 86).
- *Alibi B-plot:* estrangement, a suitor and Zoloft give every silence a motive (p. 27–29, 44, 53, 77–79, 84–86, 99).
- *Genre expectation:* the other ghosts show their deaths (p. 70, 83, 87, 112); Malcolm's wound stays under his jacket.
- *Red herrings:* the divorce frame (p. 8, 12, 40–42) and the schizophrenia diagnosis (p. 67). We worry that he will misread this boy too, not that he might be dead.
- *Teaching by proxy:* Mr. Marschal (p. 33–36), Lynn's sleep-talk (p. 100), the cold on Cole (p. 69, 101).
- *Withholding:* no hospital, no recovery scene; awake, Anna never speaks a line to him.

**The reveal (p. 122–127; ≈5 pages, 4% of the script).** The ring rolls at the foot of p. 123. An eyeline chain of planted objects follows: her ring, his bare hand, the deadbolts, one place setting, the wedding video, her breath. It runs ≈0.75 page and ends on his one-word denial, which the direction plays as a fall. The script admits its trick: "IT'S NOW WE NOTICE FOR THE FIRST TIME, THAT ANNA'S BREATHS ARE FORMING TINY CLOUDS IN THE COLD AIR." (p. 124). A SLAM CUT opens the only flashback (≈0.6 page). It is not a recap; it shows the death from a new angle: the wound, his last breath, Anna's scream sounding far away, and dying Vincent staring at MALCOLM'S GHOST. Then come 2.5 pages of farewell and coda. What the audience re-reads: p. 11–12, every silence of Anna's, the hospital line, the door, his side, the rules (p. 65), and his church speech (p. 96).

**Draft vs. release** (only the differences I'm sure of; the twist itself is identical)
- The rules (p. 65) lack the release's line that the dead only see what they want to see.
- The reveal (p. 123–125) has no recap montage and no callbacks of Cole's voice.
- The farewell closes the *miracle* bookend (p. 5 → 126). The release's farewell about Anna never coming second has no line and no setup here.

**Fair-play verdict: mostly fair (8/10).** The evidence is physical and on the page. The cheats are causal wording that lends Anna responsiveness (p. 28–29, 78) and perception narrated as fact (the door, his breath). Without the release's see-what-they-want-to-see rule, nothing on the page excuses those cheats.

## 4. Dialogue craft
- **Voice differentiation.**
  - *Cole:* 1–5 words; yes/no questions that test adults (p. 17); rules about how adults should behave (p. 26, 47); chants (p. 27, 48); ≈32 answers given as nods; Latin and German he can't know (p. 15, 33).
  - *Malcolm:* clinical jargon (p. 28, 41, 67), a dated dictation register (p. 37), magician's patter (p. 49), and arias at the peaks (p. 63, 73, 96).
  - *Anna:* lyrical absolutes (p. 5), then only phone fragments and sleep.
  - *Lynn:* South Philly imperatives (p. 21, 79) and a triad of exhaustion (p. 81).
  - *Ghosts:* each loops one line from its last moment (p. 70, 83, 101, 103). Malcolm loops too, in his actions.
- **Subtext.**
  1. At the restaurant (p. 28–29), his apology answers charges nobody makes; her silence reads as anger but is grief.
  2. Lynn sarcastically suggests an intruder moved the pendant; Cole's soft concession is the literal truth (p. 81).
  3. Lynn's (lying) yes (p. 120) is overturned by an answer delivered before its question (p. 122).
- **Hiding exposition.**
  - Two matching case files carry two backstories in one image (p. 12).
  - Backstory arrives as accusation (p. 8–9), as diary ritual (p. 37, 67), or as a bedtime story traded for a secret (p. 61–66).
  - Play carries information (p. 29–30, 49), as do half-heard phone calls (p. 44, 79, 99) and labels (p. 18, 53, 69, 98).
- **Rhythm.** A narrow column and speeches of 1–3 lines. ≈189 beats (≈1.5 per page). Whole-room silences in caps (p. 8, 11, 27). Dash cutoffs, mostly Malcolm's (p. 27, 44, 63, 73). Long speeches rationed to about 8 peaks.
- **Lines that change meaning after the twist** (paraphrased): the missed appointment (p. 14); the injury that won't let him forget (p. 14); out of it for a long while (p. 29); his mistake (p. 44); he forgot himself (p. 51); he'll follow Cole around (p. 61); like strangers (p. 63); is anyone there? (p. 77); the red in his cheeks (p. 96); he won't go home (p. 105); pretend we'll meet tomorrow (p. 116).
- **Excerpts**
  1. MALCOLM: "He doesn't." / *"Lynn stares at Dr. Patricia Hill."* / LYNN: "I know he doesn't." (p. 60). *Why it works:* an honest direction aims the line away from him; the cut does the lying.
  2. "I'm so sorry. (beat) I can't seem to keep track of time." (p. 28). *Why it works:* a stock excuse that is literally the condition of the dead.
  3. "No, walking around, like regular people... They can't see each other. Some of them don't know they're dead." (p. 65). *Why it works:* the rule is spoken to its subject, and *some of them* places him outside it.
  4. "Maybe some of them feel cheated. So they pretend they're still alive... Not even knowing, they're pretending..." (p. 96). *Why it works:* the hero states the theme and his own diagnosis, thinking he's describing others.
  5. "Guilt is not something I want to add to my list of pains." (p. 99). *Why it works:* this is half a phone call, so *guilt* means infidelity to his ear and loyalty to the dead in truth.

## 5. Directing actors on the page
- **Parentheticals: ≈146 (≈1.1 per page).** About 40% are (beat), 16% whispers, and 19% technical. The rest are soft, smiling or crying, plus four blunt subtext labels: (bullshit) p. 56, (too quick) p. 58, (lying) p. 120, and a simile that plays his denial as a fall (p. 124).
- **One blanket note scores a whole scene:** "EVERYTHING THAT'S SAID FROM THIS POINT ON IS WHISPERED." (p. 64).
- **Emotion as behavior:** shaking hands (p. 9, 12), the hand going to his side, involuntary steps (p. 35, 47), a very slow blink before speaking (p. 64), eyes that fill.
- **Meaning without a line:** Cole's nods carry the word, so the actor gets the meaning with no dialogue.
- **A note for a child actor:** Cole's hesitation in the play is real, not acted (p. 114).
- **Scored silence:** beats are written as their own action paragraphs.

## 6. Writing images, VFX & sound
- **Supernatural images.** Short caps paragraphs with active verbs and period wardrobe that reads instantly (p. 67, 77, 87), followed by a plain-case negation for the aftermath (no glass, no blood, no woman, p. 88).
  - "PULL BACK EVEN FARTHER REVEALING A ROOM FILLED WITH PEOPLE." (p. 67): the midpoint VFX, in one continuous move.
  - "WITH EACH BREATH, THEY BECOME LESS AND LESS VISIBLE... THE ROOM BECOMING LESS AND LESS COLD." (p. 126): the climax is an effect taken away.
- **Cheap, physical evidence.** Breath, thermostat numbers (p. 69), trembling leaves (p. 35), cracked glass (p. 86), light streaks in photos (p. 38), a voice in tape static (p. 93), a legless nun walking into a closet (p. 112).
- **Sound.**
  - Laughter fills the house (p. 6), screams fill it (p. 12), and taped laughter closes the film (p. 127).
  - A light switch is heard, not seen (p. 2).
  - A whisper into the phone rings like a gun blast (p. 79).
  - In the flashback Anna's scream is far away (p. 124); his scream shakes a room she sleeps through (p. 125).
- **Transitions.**
  - A dissolve hides the death (p. 12).
  - A cut assigns the slammed door to Malcolm (p. 86).
  - A ghost's demand cuts to Malcolm naming his goal (p. 103).
  - Pull-backs rhyme (p. 12, 67, 126).
  - Cole's lullaby to Lynn (p. 100) returns as Malcolm's to Anna (p. 126).

## 7. Theme, motifs, meaning
- **Dramatic question.** Overt: can Malcolm heal Cole and so redeem Vincent? Hidden: can Malcolm, and Anna, let go?
- **Theme.** The need to be heard. Malcolm states it about the dead (p. 96–97); Lynn (p. 82) and Cole (p. 118) echo it. The script hides its theme by giving it to the one man it describes without his knowing. Grief is an unfinished conversation.
- **Motifs.**
  - **Breath and cold:** p. 2 → 69 → 101 → 124 → 126.
  - **Place settings:** p. 2 → 19 → 27 → 124.
  - **Thresholds:** Malcolm always stands in doorways (p. 34, 60, 106).
  - **Recordings that let the dead speak:** p. 37, 93, 108, 127.
  - **Jewelry:** pendant, necklace, ring.
  - **The absent father's things on Cole,** including a stopped watch (p. 15, 25, 40, 61, 79).
  - **Belief, magic, miracle:** p. 5, 50, 90, 97, 126.
  - **The side wound.**
  - **Red:** weak here.
- **Final image (p. 126–127).** The cold drains away and Anna sleeps alone. The last voice is a recording of the living Malcolm, toasting in the couple's Seussian syntax from p. 3–6. Love survives as a record. The fade to black answers the bulb dying on p. 2.

## 8. Lessons for a 7-minute twist/VFX short
1. **Address audit** — Only Cole, recordings, a sleeper and a dying boy address Malcolm (p. 28, 60, 113, 123–125). — Decide who may speak to and look at the woman, then check every shot against that rule.
2. **A living alibi for every anomaly** — Estrangement and Zoloft explain Anna's silence (p. 27–29, 53). — In the dream reading, she knows his memories because she's his projection. In truth, she lived them.
3. **Count, then recount** — Two settings, then one, then the inventory (p. 2, 19, 124). — Plant three objects (two mugs, her ring, his pale ring-line). Recount them in a 20-second eyeline chain.
4. **Rehearse the rule on a proxy** — The cold is taught on Cole and sleep-talk on Lynn before both are used on Anna (p. 69, 100, 124). — Show the memory-rebuild apparatus working on a photo early.
5. **Reveal by subtraction** — The breath fades (p. 126). — Switch off the *dreaming* VFX layer. She remains, so she's real.
6. **One camera move for the false and the true reveal** — Pull-backs on p. 12, 67, 126. — Open on a pull-back from her *forming* in the scanner. Close with the same move revealing their home or a hospital room.
7. **A meta-trick scene** — The penny never moved (p. 49–50). — A 15-second *which hand?* game whose answer, *it was always there*, is the twist.
8. **Lines true in both readings** — p. 28, 60, 99. — Write each of her lines once as the dream-woman and once as the wife; keep only the lines that survive both.
9. **Diegetic recording instead of V.O.** — Diaries, tapes, the wedding video (p. 37, 93, 127). — His lab log turns out to be her therapy log. End on their wedding video in their private phrasing.
10. **Short recognition, long consequence** — ≈1.4 pages of recognition against ≈2.5 of farewell (p. 123–127). — About 25 seconds to realize, about 90 for what it costs.

## 9. البطاقة العربية
- **الفيلم · الكاتب · عدد الصفحات:** «الحاسة السادسة» (1999) · م. نايت شيامالان (مسودة 12 سبتمبر 1997، نسخة ممسوحة ضوئيًا) · 127 صفحة PDF.
- **التويست في جملة:** الطبيب النفسي مالكوم، الذي يعالج طفلًا يرى الموتى، قُتل برصاصة في المشهد الافتتاحي، وظلّ طوال الفيلم شبحًا لا يعلم أنه ميت.
- **آلية التويست:**
  ١. قاعدة صارمة لمن يخاطب مالكوم: بعد ص 12 لا يكلّمه أي حيّ مستيقظ سوى كول. الباقون يتجاهلونه أو يتحدثون في الهاتف أو إلى شخص آخر (ص 28، 60، 113).
  ٢. لكل صمتٍ تفسيرٌ حيّ بديل: أزمة زواج، ومُعجَب، ومضاد اكتئاب. لذلك يقرأ الجمهور حداد الأرملة على أنه خصام زوجي (ص 27–29، 44، 53، 78–79).
  ٣. تُعلَّم القواعد على شخصيات أخرى أولًا: بخار النفَس البارد على كول (ص 69، 101)، والكلام أثناء النوم على لين (ص 100). ثم تُستخدم مع آنا في الكشف عبر جردٍ للأشياء: الخاتم، والأقفال، والطبق الوحيد، والنفَس (ص 123–124).
- **سرّ الحوار:**
  - جمل صادقة في القراءتين، مثل اعتذاره عن عجزه عن تتبّع الوقت (ص 28): عذرُ متأخرٍ، وهو حرفيًا حال الميت.
  - التوجيه المكتوب يحرف وجهة الجملة: لين تنظر إلى الطبيبة، فيبدو ردّها موافقةً لمالكوم (ص 60).
  - اقتباس: "You just kept the penny in that hand the whole time..." (ص 50). خدعة القرش تشرح منهج الفيلم كله: الحقيقة لم تتحرك قط.
- **إدارة الممثل على الورق:** أغلب الأقواس التوجيهية (beat) و(whispers)، مع أربع إشارات صريحة إلى النص الباطن مثل (lying) (ص 120). أما الانفعال فيحمله الجسد: اليد على الخاصرة، والعينان، ونحو 189 وقفة صمت مكتوبة.
- **كتابة الصورة والمؤثرات:** فقرات قصيرة بأحرف كبيرة وأفعال حركة، ولغة الكاميرا (WE PULL BACK) تتركّز عند لحظات الكشف (ص 12، 67، 126). وذروة المؤثرات حذفٌ لا إضافة: يخفت بخار النفَس حتى يزول (ص 126).
- **٣ تقنيات نأخذها لفيلمنا القصير:**
  ١. حدّد قاعدة «من يخاطب المرأة» واختبر بها كل لقطة، واجعل لكل غرابةٍ تفسيرًا في قراءة «الحلم».
  ٢. اجعل الكشف إطفاءً لطبقة المؤثر: تتوقف هالة «الحلم» وتبقى الزوجة حقيقيةً في الغرفة.
  ٣. ازرع ثلاثة أشياء مبكرًا (خاتم، كوبان، تسجيل الزفاف)، وأعِد جردها في عشرين ثانية عند الكشف، واختم بتسجيلٍ بلغة الزوجين الخاصة.

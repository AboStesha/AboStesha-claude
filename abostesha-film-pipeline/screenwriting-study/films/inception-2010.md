# Inception (2010) — screenplay study

## 0. Draft facts
- **Writer:** "By Christopher Nolan", sole credit (title page, PDF p. 1).
- **Draft:** "SHOOTING SCRIPT" (title page, bottom right). No date, no revision marks.
- **Pages:** 147 PDF pages (a title page plus 146 script pages). Each printed page number is the PDF page minus 1; all refs below are PDF pages.
- **Scenes:** about 290 scene headings, because every cross-cut return gets a new slugline (123 are CONTINUOUS). Dramatic scenes are far fewer.
- **Numbering:** no scene numbers, letter suffixes or OMITTED scenes.

## 1. Format & page organization
- **Sluglines:** standard `INT./EXT. LOCATION, SUB-LOCATION - TIME`, with `INT./EXT.` for vehicles. CONTINUOUS is everywhere.
- **Reality status lives in slugline parentheticals:**
  - `INT. SAME ELEGANT DINING ROOM - NIGHT (YEARS EARLIER)` (p. 3).
  - `FILTHY BATHROOM - DAY (FEELS LIKE DIFFERENT TIME)` (p. 5), which states a feeling, not a fact, and has no INT./EXT. anchor.
  - Also (NOW NIGHT) (p. 70), (FLASHBACK) six times (p. 89–93), and (LIMBO) on 12 headings (p. 128–143). Limbo is the only dream layer ever tagged.
- **Transitions:**
  - FADE IN and FADE OUT appear only at the two ends.
  - `CUT TO:` appears 92 times, nearly always after an action line that breaks off with "and we-" (about 84 times). The unfinished sentence pulls the reader across the cut.
  - Drops into a deeper dream use "and we are-" with no transition (p. 113, 128, 140), so the new level starts mid-action.
  - There are only two `DISSOLVE TO:`s, both on the way down to limbo (p. 129).
- **Not used:** MONTAGE, INTERCUT, SUPER, TITLE, SERIES OF SHOTS. Cross-cuts are written out in full; the van alone gets a new slugline 17 times (p. 97–144).
- **INSERT CUT:** 21 memory inserts, often mid-paragraph (p. 19, 37, 57). The PDF italicizes them, along with phone and radio voices `(over phone)` `(OVER RADIO)` and V.O. over flashbacks. Italics mark anything that isn't present reality.
- **V.O. and continuations:** V.O. (16 cues) carries briefings illustrated with cutaways (p. 60–66) and Cobb's confession (p. 89–93). (CONT'D) after an action line appears only from p. 92, so conventions are mixed.
- **Camera:** almost none (one MOVE IN, two CLOSE ONs). SLOW MOTION (10 times) doubles as a depth marker.

**Nested levels (p. 75–144).** The page never numbers the levels. Each layer has its own sluglines and senses instead:

| Layer | Slugline signature | Sensory signature |
|---|---|---|
| Reality | FIRST CLASS CABIN, 747 | stillness |
| L1 (Yusuf's dream) | VAN ON RAINY STREETS → … RISING BRIDGE → VAN INTO RIVER → VAN, UNDERWATER (the headings track the van toward the kick) | rain, chase |
| L2 (Arthur's dream) | HOTEL BAR / CORRIDOR / ROOM 528 / 491 | noir hotel; tilt, then zero gravity |
| L3 (Eames's dream) | SNOW-COVERED MOUNTAINS / HOSPITAL COMPLEX | commando raid in white snow |
| Limbo | (LIMBO) | crumbling, empty city |

Other devices keep the levels clear:
- A watch legend shown before any rule is explained: second hands frozen, crawling or ticking in real time (p. 5–6).
- Slow motion that deepens with each return to the van (p. 120–126).
- Force passed down through the cuts: the van swerves and a drink tilts (p. 103–104); the van crashes, the corridor shakes and an avalanche starts (p. 120).
- Clocks recalculated out loud (p. 118, 121).

L2's time of day drifts (p. 99–105), so trust location, not time.

## 3. Twist engineering
**The twist in one sentence:** Cobb caused Mal's death. In limbo he planted the idea that her world wasn't real by spinning her top inside the safe of her mind, and the prologue is actually the climax: an aged Cobb washing up in limbo to retrieve a 90-year-old Saito. The final top is an engineered open question, not a twist.

**Plant ledger**

| p. | Plant | Disguise | Payoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2–3 | An unnamed bearded man; the old man knows the top from a half-remembered dream | Mythic prologue, then YEARS EARLIER | 145 |
| 3–4 | The pitch: the most resilient parasite is an idea | Con-man patter | 131, 135 |
| 6–11 | Mal inside Cobb's job | Femme-fatale genre; no explanation for 40 pages | 48, 135 |
| 19 | A train sound as the top spins | Pure sound | 77, 136 |
| 25 | He has done inception before but won't say on whom | Arthur shrugs; cut away | 135 |
| 40 / 56 | Totems must be known only to their owner / his top was Mal's and never topples in a dream | 16 pages apart | 136, 147 |
| 57 | Insert of Cobb begging in tears; he admits only the legal blame | Guilt framed as legal | 135–137 |
| 67, 89 | A locked safe in a doll's house; Mal locks her top inside | Set dressing; his V.O. calls it her choice | 135 |
| 71 | The train riddle: Mal leads, Cobb answers | Read as her madness | 136 (the speakers swap) |
| 82 | Arthur sneers about the inception on Mal | An insult mid-fight | 135 |
| 87 | Saito vows to come back young | Pathos | 145–146 |
| 94 | Ariadne says the idea was Mal's own | An ally absolves him; Cobb stays silent | 135 |

**Misdirection toolkit**
- *A narrator who leaves things out:* Cobb's V.O. (p. 87–93) never says the idea was Mal's. Ariadne assumes it, and he lets it stand (p. 94).
- *A false bottom:* the suicide and the frame-up letter (p. 91–92) feel like the whole secret. The real one waits 43 pages.
- *Genre noise:* kicks, time math and the numbers 528/491 occupy the puzzle-solving mind, while the twist is emotional.
- *Red herring:* Mal as saboteur makes guilt look like an external enemy (p. 9–11, 38, 126).
- *Displacement:* the team describes Cobb's crime as a method (the simplest idea, p. 43; self-generated, p. 62; hidden in a safe, p. 83), and we apply it to Fischer.
- *Editing grammar:* the unlabeled café dream (p. 31) teaches us that headings can mislead. YEARS EARLIER is true only by old Saito's clock.

**The reveal (p. 134–137, about 3.5 pages).** It is a confession in the limbo kitchen that climbs in steps:
1. Guilt.
2. The lie.
3. The method, in nine italic inserts replaying the safe from p. 89, now with Cobb in the frame.
4. The riddle, with Cobb speaking first.
5. Mal's fall, again.

Mal's "(realizing)" says what we have just worked out. We re-read the pitch, the admission on the jet, the top's owner, the safe and every Mal scene (guilt, not malice).

**Reveal 2 (p. 145–146)** is an image plus a password. The beach heading returns, the cues finally name Cobb and Saito, and the regret lines (p. 24, 87) are traded back.

**Fair play:** fair. Every load-bearing fact comes before p. 135. YEARS EARLIER is literally true. The final top is a declared gap: the rules for reading it are taught (p. 19, 40, 56), and only the outcome is withheld.

## 4. Dialogue craft
**Voices.**
- **Cobb:** imperatives, rules, deflection; long only when confessing.
- **Arthur:** procedural deadpan and one-word punchlines (p. 120).
- **Eames:** British slang and mock courtesy (p. 41, 62).
- **Yusuf:** vanity and comic swearing (p. 116–117).
- **Saito:** formal understatement; he simply buys the airline (p. 63).
- **Mal:** riddles and second-person hypotheticals (p. 6, 71, 132).
- **Ariadne:** asks why and how, then diagnoses Cobb (p. 68, 94).
- **Fischer:** clipped and entitled (p. 77) until his speech about his father (p. 85–86).

**Subtext.**
1. Mal asks if the children miss her, and Cobb answers about himself (excerpt 1).
2. Arthur raises Mal, and Cobb apologizes for Arthur's leg instead: the symptom, not the cause (p. 21).
3. Cobb thanks Ariadne for not asking whether he killed Mal. The gratitude confesses doubt (p. 57).

**Exposition hiding.** Each rule is demonstrated first, then taught to the newcomer, then exploited:

| Rule | Shown cold | Taught | Exploited |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deeper levels run slower | three watches (5–6) | 5 min = 1 hr (34); ×20 per level (65) | recalculated live (118) |
| The kick | a dunk in the bath floods the castle (12–13) | a tipped chair (65) | synchronized kicks (115–143) |
| Music cue | Piaf heard as horns (15) | named (67) | played too early (117) |
| Bleed between levels | a train jolt becomes a tremor (5–6) | explained to Fischer (107) | tilted bar, zero gravity, lightning (104, 121, 140) |
| Dying wakes you; pain is real | Arthur shot (10) | — | under sedation, death means limbo (80) |
| You never remember arriving | the unlabeled café (31) | Cobb's quiz (32–33) | descents start mid-action; used on Fischer (105) |
| Projections hunt the dreamer | rioters swarm Nash (14–17) | stares, then a knife (35–38) | militarized projections (77); Mr. Charles (104) |
| Totem | Tokyo (19) | lecture cross-cut with Cobb's private test (38–40) | the last page (147) |
| Impossible architecture | — | Penrose steps (46–47) | stairwell trap (120) |

Rules also come out as a threat (Mal explains pain while maiming Arthur, p. 10), a game (the elephants test, p. 22–23), a blame fight (limbo, p. 79–82) and a joke (excerpt 3). Ariadne finishes the expert's sentences (p. 31, 47, 65, 118) and voices confusion on cue (p. 112). Every lesson ends on Mal (p. 38, 55–57, 67–72), so the heist rules double as clues to Cobb's wound.

**Rhythm.** Speeches run 1–3 lines in fast question and answer. Long speeches are kept for the pitch (p. 4), Mal's attack (p. 132) and the confessions. The ledge scene (p. 92) alternates Mal's calm riddle with Cobb's capitalized shouts. Silence is written as action: eight times, Cobb answers with nothing.

**Changed by the twist.** These lines read differently once you know:
- the parasite pitch (p. 3–4);
- the admission on the jet (p. 25);
- Mal blocking his designs (p. 28);
- the top that never topples (p. 56);
- the legal blame (p. 57);
- Ariadne's absolution (p. 94);
- the old man asking whether the stranger came to kill him (p. 2). He has, because death is the way out.

**Excerpts**
1. MAL: "Do the children miss me?" … COBB: "You can't imagine." (p. 7–8). He answers about himself, and "imagine" is the private joke: she exists only in his imagination.
2. ARTHUR: "Did it work?" COBB (quiet): "Yes." ARTHUR: "Who did you do it to?" / "Cobb looks at Arthur. Closed. Arthur shrugs." (p. 25). The twist is stated, then shut like a door.
3. YUSUF: "Three dreams... that's ten hours, times twenty, times twenty, times twenty..." EAMES: "Math was never my strong suit." (p. 65). The joke hands the arithmetic to Cobb and Ariadne to finish.
4. MAL: "How could you *know* it was a lie?" COBB: "Because it was my lie." (p. 135). The secret turns on one possessive pronoun.

## 5. Directing actors on the page
- **Parentheticals:** about 97 in 146 pages.
  - Roughly a third are physical business (stamps foot, downs drink).
  - A third are tone (quiet, sharp, rising anger).
  - A quarter say who is being addressed (to X, into radio).
  - Seven record a listener's reaction inside the speaker's block (Saito nods), so the speech keeps flowing.
- **Only two "(beat)"s:** both come just before someone learns they are dreaming (p. 9, 105).
- **The result is named only once,** at the reveal: "(realizing)" (p. 135).
- **Emotion as behavior:**
  - About 20 one-word sentences after a look: Closed (p. 25), Devastated (p. 111), Haunted (p. 146).
  - Similes: "Cobb GRABS it like a drowning man reaching for a lifeline." (p. 40).
  - One gesture carrying two feelings: gloved fingers touch Mal's ankle as he ties her chair down (p. 7).
  - Inserts for what he can't say: Cobb begging in tears flashes up while he gives the legal version (p. 57).
- **Silence repeated until it confesses** (p. 37, 56, 81, 94, 134). Capitalized verbs give action beats a rhythm without a shot list.

## 6. Writing images, VFX & sound
**The formula:** 20–60 words. A capitalized process verb, an end-state metaphor, the physics rule in plain words, then a human reaction. Eyelines imply the camera.
- "The street starts to BEND IN HALF- the buildings on either side FOLDING IN until they form the INSIDE OF A CUBE OF CITY, GRAVITY FUNCTIONING INDEPENDENTLY ON EACH PLANE." (p. 35). Next, Ariadne looks up "(or down)" and Cobb watches her, so the shot lands on faces.
- "URBAN BUILDINGS PILED right down to the water. The buildings are DECAYING, falling into the ocean like a GLACIER calving." (p. 128). Limbo then becomes the marriage's autobiography: first flat, the house for the pregnancy, a craftsman house inside a skyscraper (p. 130–131).

**Zero gravity as tasks.** About 10 beats of 1–3 lines are spread across p. 115–141:
- the corridor tilts, and Arthur runs up the wall;
- gravity vanishes, and he grabs a wall lamp (p. 121);
- he can't drop the sleepers for the kick (p. 122), so he binds them with a bedsheet (p. 126);
- he blows the elevator shaft (p. 141).

Each image is a verb with a purpose. A drink tilts before the room does (p. 104), and each return to the van is slower (p. 120–126).

**Sound.**
- The freight train is planted as sound only (p. 19). It becomes a real train (p. 77), then how Cobb and Mal die in limbo (p. 136).
- Piaf arrives slowed into distant horns (p. 15) and is mistaken for wind (p. 117–118): a no-regrets song for a man made of regret.
- A child's shout summons the faceless children (p. 2, 69, 130, 147).

**Transitions as storytelling.** The slugline becomes the object of the sentence: "as it SMASHES INTO THE TOP OF THE SHAFT Ariadne SMASHES into-" / `INT./EXT. VAN INTO RIVER - DAY` / "THE WATER, THE VAN CRUNCHING WITH THE IMPACT-" (p. 143). The same trick takes bathwater to bathtub in the prologue (p. 13). Lightning in limbo is the defibrillator in L3 (p. 140). A billowing curtain links Mal's suite to each of her intrusions (p. 54, 70, 91, 113).

## 7. Theme, motifs, meaning
**Central dramatic question:** will Cobb get home and see his children's faces? Underneath: can he forgive himself and let Mal go? The heist can't succeed until he does (Mal kills Fischer, p. 126).

**Theme:** Ariadne states it: guilt defines and powers Mal (p. 94). Cobb calls guilt his compass (p. 134–135). The hidden theme is that a planted idea can heal or kill. A forged reconciliation frees Fischer (p. 141, 144); a true idea destroyed Mal. Reality is finally a leap of faith (p. 24, 91, 146), a choice Mal urges him to make (p. 133).

**Mal as the hidden engine:** every obstacle is guilt with a face:
- the castle sabotage (p. 9–11);
- the knife (p. 38);
- the train in L1 (p. 77);
- the children in L2 (p. 102);
- Fischer's death (p. 126);
- the hostage in limbo (p. 139).

The climax cross-cuts two releases: Fischer's father recasting the word "disappointed" (p. 141), and Cobb letting Mal die in his arms (p. 143).

**Motifs.**
- **The top:** cone, then test, then Mal's totem, then weapon, then doubt (p. 2–3, 19, 56, 89, 136, 146–147).
- **Faces unseen** in about 10 scenes: they almost turn while Cobb shuts his eyes (p. 134), then turn at last (p. 147).
- **Train and riddle:** p. 19, 71, 77, 92, 136.
- **Safes as the deepest part of the mind:** p. 9, 67, 89, 142.
- **A billowing curtain meaning Mal:** p. 54, 70, 91, 113.
- **Mirrors meaning forgery:** p. 61, 84, 101, 144.
- **Password lines:** regret and dying alone (p. 24, 87, 145); leap of faith (p. 24, 91, 146).

**Final image.** In limbo, p. 146: "The top IS STILL SPINNING PERFECTLY, AS IF IT WILL NEVER TOPPLE." At home, p. 147: "Behind him, on the table, the spinning top is STILL SPINNING. And we-" / "FADE OUT." The ambiguity is built four ways:
1. **The want is granted in the foreground:** the faces turn.
2. **The doubt sits behind him,** described in limbo's own words minus the promise that it will never topple.
3. **The test was already compromised:** totems must be known only to their owner (p. 40), and this one was Mal's (p. 56).
4. **The expected cut is refused:** "And we-", used about 84 times to cross layers, runs into FADE OUT.

Meaning: Cobb has stopped testing and simply chooses. It is the leap of faith the script won't take for us.

## 8. Lessons for a 7-minute twist/VFX short
1. **Show the rule before naming it** — The kick, the music cue and the time difference work in the prologue (p. 5–15) and are named about 50 pages later (p. 65–67). — Let the dream-making work once in the first minute, unexplained: he closes his eyes, and a warm cup is in his hand.
2. **The newcomer's questions do two jobs** — Ariadne's questions teach the rules and pry open Cobb's guilt (p. 55–57, 67–72, 86–94). — The dreamed woman's questions (*what's my favorite song?*) read first as a creation testing its maker, and after the twist as a wife running memory drills.
3. **The café test as an amnesia clue** — Nobody remembers arriving at the café (p. 31–33), and every descent starts mid-action (p. 75, 99, 113, 128). — Open mid-routine in the lab, with one beat where he can't recall arriving. It reads as dream logic now and memory loss later.
4. **Sensory bleed as the clue engine** — Bath water floods the castle (p. 12–13), and a defibrillator becomes lightning (p. 140). — A hospital monitor's beep becomes the rhythm of the lab alarm, and her real hand becomes warm light on his palm. On a second viewing, the dream is clearly driven from outside.
5. **One signature per reality; tag only the layer that could be confused** — Each level has its own sluglines, and only limbo is labeled (p. 128–143). — Fix sound and light signatures for the lab and the dream room. Add one honest tag that the twist proves true from the other side, as YEARS EARLIER does (p. 3).
6. **Hide the face, rename at the reveal** — The children's faces turn only on the last page (p. 147), and the bearded man is named only on p. 145. — Keep her face or ring unseen. Cue her as THE WOMAN, then switch to her name at the reveal.
7. **Split the object's history** — The top was Mal's (p. 56), she locked it in her safe (p. 89), and Cobb turned it into a weapon (p. 136). — He believes he invented a melody in the dream; one throwaway line shows it was hers.
8. **A password line that changes speaker** — Mal leads the train riddle (p. 71) until Cobb leads it (p. 136). — She starts a private phrase he can't finish. At the twist, he finishes it unprompted.
9. **VFX in under 50 words: verb, end-state metaphor, reaction** — The folding city (p. 35) and limbo (p. 128). — *The lab's white walls UNFOLD into a seaside kitchen. He looks for the seam. She watches him look.*
10. **Put the doubt behind the character** — The faces turn in front, the top spins behind, and "And we-" dies in FADE OUT (p. 147). — In the final frame he says her name and turns to her, while a background object keeps a dream behavior (a frozen clock). Cut before it resolves.

- **آلية التويست:**
  ١. زرعٌ معلَن ثم مغلق: يعترف كوب بأنه نفّذ الزرع من قبل، ثم يصمت بنظرة مغلقة (ص 25). والخذروف كان في الأصل لمال (ص 56)، وقد حبسته في خزنتها (ص 89).
  ٢. قاعٌ زائف: كشف الانتحار والرسالة الملفّقة (ص 91–92) يبدو السرَّ كله، فيُخفي السرَّ الأكبر حتى ص 135.
  ٣. قواعد الصنعة تُشرح على فيشر لكنها تصف ما فعله كوب بمال بالضبط: أبسط صورة للفكرة، وأن تبدو نابعة من صاحبها، والخزنة بوصفها أعمق موضع في العقل (ص 43، 62، 83).
- **سرّ الحوار:**
  - لا يأتي الشرح محاضرةً، بل تهديداً (ص 10) أو مزاحاً (ص 65) أو شجاراً (ص 79–82). وأريادني تُكمل جمل الخبراء، فتشارك في صياغة القاعدة.
  - أكبر أسرار الفيلم معلّق على ضمير ملكية واحد: "Because it was my lie." (p. 135)
  - الصمت يُكتب فعلاً في سطور الوصف: ثماني مرات لا يقول فيها كوب شيئاً.
- **إدارة الممثل على الورق:** نحو 97 توجيهاً بين قوسين فقط في 146 صفحة. ولا ترد (beat) إلا مرتين، كلتاهما قبل إخبار شخصية بأنها تحلم (ص 9، 105). أما الانفعال فيُكتب سلوكاً، أو صفةً من كلمة واحدة بعد النظرة (Closed، Devastated).
- **كتابة الصورة والمؤثرات:** الصورة المستحيلة في 20–60 كلمة: فعلُ حركة بحروف كبيرة، ثم استعارة للحالة النهائية (مدينة تنطوي إلى مكعب، ومبانٍ تتساقط في البحر كجبل جليدي يتصدّع)، ثم ردّ فعل إنساني. وانعدام الجاذبية مقسَّم إلى مهام صغيرة عبر القطع المتوازي. والانتقال بين المستويات جملة واحدة يصير عنوانُ المشهد مفعولاً لها (ص 143).
- **٣ تقنيات نأخذها لفيلمنا القصير:**
  ١. تسرّب الحواس: صوت جهاز المراقبة في المستشفى ويدُ الزوجة الحقيقية يظهران داخل «الحلم» إنذاراً أو ضوءاً دافئاً، فيصيران دليلاً يُقرأ بعد الكشف.
  ٢. جملة سرّية تبدؤها هي ويعجز عن إكمالها، ثم يُكملها هو من تلقاء نفسه لحظةَ الكشف.
  ٣. اللقطة الأخيرة: الوجه المنشود في المقدّمة، وخلفه شيءٌ ما زال يتصرّف كما في الحلم، ثم القطع قبل الحسم.

# Se7en (1995) — screenplay study

## 0. Draft facts
- **Credit (title page, PDF p. 1):** Written by Andrew Kevin Walker. The title is printed as SEVEN.
- **Label/date:** there is no draft label, only a date: January 27, 1992 (p. 1). Page ii (p. 2) is a Hemingway epigraph from *For Whom the Bell Tolls*.
- **Length:** 157 PDF pages: a title page, the epigraph and 155 numbered pages (PDF p. 3 is page 1). The script ends with END (p. 157).
- **Scenes:** 220 numbers, about 218 real scenes. Scene 40 (p. 34) is a headline wrongly tagged as a scene. Scenes 68 and 69 are one safe-house scene, duplicated together with a block of the rooftop scene (pp. 62–64).
- **Numbering:** plain integers in both margins, with no suffixes and no OMITTED.
- **The ending differs from the film (checked on the page):**
  - There is no desert and no box, and Tracy lives.
  - Doe surrenders (pp. 125–127), demands Mills alone (p. 130) and escapes through a manhole (p. 142).
  - He stages the finale in the orphanage church and kills Mills there (pp. 152–153).
  - Somerset then shoots Doe limb by limb and burns him (pp. 153–154). Somerset, not Mills, becomes Wrath.
  - Tracy leaves the city pregnant (pp. 155–157).
- **Other differences from the film:**
  - Somerset kisses Tracy (p. 108).
  - John is shown from p. 44.
  - Mills does the library work (pp. 83–90).
  - Hemingway is an epigraph, not closing V.O.
  - Rain falls only on pp. 50–52.

## 1. Format & page organization
- **Sluglines:** INT./EXT., then the location, a sub-location after a comma or slash, and the time after a double dash. Times are granular (EARLY MORNING, LATE AFTERNOON) because the clock matters.
- **Continuations:** no CONTINUED and no (MORE). Speeches run across page breaks (pp. 7–8). A speaker resuming after action is cued (CONT), 130 times. (CONT'D) appears only in doubled conversion tags (pp. 132, 142, 145).
- **Transitions:** none at all. There is no FADE IN and no CUT TO; each slugline is the cut.
- **INSERT and shot headings:** INSERT is used for the seven day cards and for optical POVs (rearview mirror p. 108, goggles p. 138, binoculars p. 140). There are also bare shot headings (TRACY'S P.O.V. p. 23; VIEWED FROM FAR BACK IN THE CHURCH p. 153; CLOSE ON JOHN DOE'S FACE p. 154). The credits are flagged inside sluglines (pp. 4–5).
- **V.O. and O.S.:** V.O. is used only for voices through a device, and always with a source parenthetical: TV (p. 39), tape recorder (p. 101), phone (p. 122), and the receivers and headsets that carry the climax (pp. 135–143). O.S. covers unseen speakers in the room, including names read over inserts of the paintings (pp. 91–92).
- **No MONTAGE, INTERCUT, FLASHBACK or SERIES OF SHOTS.** Parallel action is cut as numbered micro-scenes of one to three lines (scenes 199–203, p. 143).
- **Time:** seven day title cards, from MONDAY (p. 11) to SUNDAY (p. 122), plus LATER in sluglines. There are no dreams and no alternate timelines.
- **Unusual devices:** an ASCII diagram of Dante's terraces (pp. 50–51); a chalkboard tally crossing off sins (pp. 52, 82, 111, 129); computer-screen text (p. 83); in-world writing in capitals (HELP ME, p. 67).
- **Format examples:**
```
13   INSERT -- TITLE CARD
     MONDAY                                         (p. 11)

                    MILLS (V.O.)
                        (from receiver)             (p. 142)
```

## 3. Twist engineering
**The twist in one sentence:** Doe's surrender is bait for a rehearsed escape and a final tableau in which Envy (Doe) kills the man he envies, Mills, so that the cool Somerset, who has never fired a shot, completes the seventh sin, Wrath.

**Misdirection toolkit:**
- **Genre and character expectation:** every rage beat belongs to Mills. The draft kills him mid-charge and gives the sin to the mentor.
- **POV control (suspense over mystery):** we see Doe early and often (pp. 44, 59, 107–109, 121) but only in mute actions. We know his face, not his plan.
- **Emotional distraction:** the kiss (p. 108), the photo (p. 117) and the pregnancy (pp. 123–124) fill the run-up to the surrender with guilt and fear.
- **Red herring:** Doe's bloody clothes plus the two bodies make us fear for Tracy, who stays offscreen from p. 124 to the funeral (p. 154).
- **Declared ignorance:** asked whether he can conceive what comes next, Mills answers no (p. 129).
- **Prophecy hidden in ekphrasis:** the finale is described as paintings about 55 pages early.
- **Editing grammar:** we follow the walk by radio (pp. 135–142), and the feed dies when Mills jumps (p. 143). A one-line scene of Doe's hands tearing open a stashed gun (p. 143) proves design.

**The reveal (pp. 152–154, inside a 9-page set piece):**
- Dialogue names envy (p. 152).
- Action enacts wrath (pp. 153–154), and no line ever names it.
- An image confirms it: the tableau burns in order with wrath last, then a match to the blue funeral (p. 154). The burning panels serve as the recap montage.
- The reader re-reads the gun story (p. 72), the lectures on emotion, the smashed metronome and every mention of admiration.

**Ending setup:**
- **The surrender** is a public tableau: open arms inside the precinct doors, silence, and bandaged fingertips that explain the missing prints (pp. 126–127). The lawyer's choice, a confession or an insanity plea, forces the deal (pp. 130–131).
- **The plan** needs a policeman's wrath: here Somerset's, with Mills's temper as the decoy.
- **There is no box.** Its job, a killing in front of a witness, is done by Mills dying in Somerset's arms (p. 153).
- **The pregnancy** is planted (p. 67), framed (p. 107), confided (pp. 123–124) and paid off (p. 156). It is a thematic plant, not a weapon: nothing on the page shows that Doe knows.

**Fair-play verdict:** mostly fair. The panels describe envy and wrath, the admiration escalates in plain view, and the rehearsal is shown. The weak spots: Somerset finds the church with no deduction on the page (pp. 145–146), and the blood and the two bodies are never explained.

## 4. Dialogue craft
**Voice differentiation.** The counts make it a true two-hander: Somerset has 307 speeches (12.4 words on average) and Mills 298 (9.8). Doe speaks rarely (42 speeches) but at the greatest length (15.9 words).
- **Somerset:** subordinated sentences, Latinate words and citations (Milton p. 29, Aquinas p. 58). He asks rather than asserts (p. 9) and seldom swears.
- **Mills:** bursts of profanity and pop references (TV Guide p. 56, Godspell p. 137). His sarcasm turns on himself (p. 91).
- **Doe:** biblical cadence and parables. He calls Mills David and alludes to Lot's wife (p. 137).
- **Tracy:** hedges and self-questions (p. 22).

**Subtext:**
1. p. 49: Somerset calls a scrap of wallpaper his future. It stands for retirement, loneliness and an unnamed flirtation.
2. pp. 32–33: he says the case is too much, too soon for Mills. He means it is too much for himself.
3. p. 132: Mills jokes that he wants to make love to Somerset before the handover. It is fear and affection disguised, and it reverses his bonding sarcasm on p. 15.

**Exposition hiding:** fights (the bar, pp. 117–120); games (the word chain that locates the lust murder, p. 112); gags (Cliff Notes, p. 52); objects (the jar of plastic, pp. 26–28; notebooks read aloud, p. 100); TV (pp. 38–39); pantomime behind glass (p. 86).

**Rhythm.** Most speeches run one to four lines. Long speeches are rationed:
- Mills's first kill (p. 72);
- the bar sermon (p. 119);
- Somerset's confession about the child he talked his partner out of having (p. 123);
- the garden parable (pp. 150–151).

Each is broken by (pause) or by action. Silence is written as action: a big silence (p. 57), Somerset saying nothing (p. 128).

**Lines that change meaning after the twist:**
- the admiration (p. 101);
- Mills vowing not to let Doe win (p. 144);
- Doe envying Mills's common life (p. 152), which includes a child Mills never learns of.

**Excerpts:**
1. Somerset, p. 11: "Well, please do me the favor of remembering it." / Mills, p. 19, after knocking him down: "Please, do me the favor of remembering that." — *Why it works:* a line handed back word for word flips the power balance.
2. p. 112, alternating: "Lust is red." / "Bright red." / "Blood red." / "Red sky at night, sailor's delight." / "Red blooded. Red head. Dead." / "Red light district." — *Why it works:* deduction as a jazz duet, literal against literary, until the two voices meet.
3. Somerset, p. 79: "It's always impressive to see a man feeding off his emotions." — *Why it works:* dry irony in his register. After p. 153 it indicts him.
4. Somerset, p. 119: "I can't live anymore where stupidity is embraced and nurtured as if it were a virtue." — *Why it works:* the thesis arrives as a counterpunch in a fight. (The film's word is apathy.)
5. Doe, p. 152: "I meant what I said. I admire David Mills. I envy David Mills. (pause) Envy is my sin." — *Why it works:* a syllogism built from a word heard twice. The pause sits where admiration turns into envy.

## 5. Directing actors on the page
- **Frequency:** 145 parentheticals across 155 pages, mostly technical. (pause) is the most common (30). Source tags (from receiver, into phone) account for about 29 and addressee tags for about 20. Emotion words are rare and blunt (wary, seething, awed). One flags subtext outright: (lying), on Somerset's denial (p. 35).
- **Behavior carries the emotion:**
  - the captain clenches his jaw whenever he is silent (p. 31);
  - Somerset re-messes the papers the captain straightened (p. 35);
  - he chews antacids before danger (pp. 71, 133);
  - Doe squeezes his bandaged fingertips until they bleed (p. 138).
- **The gap between line and intent is named:** Mills grins but means it (p. 54); Tracy's smile falters and Somerset notices (p. 48). Firsts are flagged too, such as the first fear in Mills's eyes (p. 109).
- **Beats:** written as one-sentence paragraphs. The flashlight held on Somerset's face (p. 17) is a wordless power struggle.
- **Example:** "The lighter will not light, and when it does, Somerset's hand is shaking." (p. 120)

## 6. Writing images, VFX & sound
**Spectacle.** Short present-tense paragraphs, one shot each, with hard verbs. The impossible beats get one sentence each: Doe dropping through the manhole (p. 142), and Doe stepping out of a waterfall while putting on his glasses (p. 144). Syntax implies the camera: a three-phrase crane-down to under the bridge (p. 136), a POV from behind the refrigerator (p. 28). Ekphrasis does the art direction: panels written like storyboards (pp. 88, 91–92), Doe's collage (p. 98), a 16mm loop of Hollywood hell (p. 97).

**Dread and withholding.** None of Doe's five sin murders happens on screen. Each is reconstructed afterwards by forensics and dialogue (p. 31). The techniques:
- **The reaction replaces the image:** the autopsy is heard, not seen (p. 25); Doe's canvas is withheld, then described when Mills looks (pp. 97–98); the lust polaroid is withheld (p. 113), then described in a white room (p. 116); the lust victim and the pride victim's face are never shown (pp. 103, 115).
- **Inventory replaces the act:** a bloody sink of scissors, syringe and razor, and a drain that still bubbles (p. 102).
- **Compressed time:** 52 dated polaroids turn a year of torture into one image, and then the corpse barks (pp. 75–77).
- **Left unexplained:** the blood on Doe (p. 125) and the implied orphanage abuse (pp. 84–86).
- **Present-tense violence is held back** for the last two sins, committed on and by the heroes (pp. 152–154).
- **Example:** "They grimace at what they see. We do not see." (p. 115)

**City as character.** The rule comes first: "The SOUNDS of the CITY are here as they will be everywhere in this story." (p. 5). The city is:
- **a soundscape:** a car alarm against the metronome (p. 5); the subway rattling plates (p. 55); a scream never heard again (p. 67);
- **an indifferent crowd:** a man masturbating on the subway, unnoticed (p. 20);
- **a bestiary:** dogs, roaches, a pig carcass (pp. 4, 14, 20);
- **a palette of sin-light:** green, yellow, indigo, violet, red, orange, blue (pp. 13, 29, 74, 102, 115, 146, 154).

Rain is not the engine here: it falls on one Thursday morning (pp. 50, 52). The train ride, from farms to a swamp to slums (pp. 4–5), is the visual thesis.

**Sound and transitions.** Sound in capitals does the transitions:
- jazz pre-laps the club (p. 37);
- speed metal swallows the jazz down a stairwell (p. 38);
- the rock music cuts out as the sheet lifts (p. 115).

With no transition ever written, the juxtapositions are composed. The kiss photo burns in a sink, and the cut goes to a bar full of trophies (p. 117). The final match cut: "Wrath goes last. A man with bloodied hands, in tones of blue. Flames devour it." → "A field of blue." (p. 154)

## 7. Theme, motifs, meaning
- **Dramatic question:** can they stop Doe before seven? Underneath it: will Somerset leave the city or fight for it? The last image answers the second.
- **Theme:** set out in the epigraph (p. 2) and argued as a debate: Somerset's despair (pp. 58, 69, 117–120), Mills's chosen naivety (p. 48), Doe's purge (pp. 148–151). Doe tells the police they want the same things (p. 149). His parable splits that shared despair into two choices: weed the garden, or plow it under. Somerset completes Doe's design but refuses Doe's conclusion.
- **Motifs:**
  - **Rose/garden:** the wallpaper rose (pp. 3, 49), dried flowers at the pride murder (p. 102), Doe's gardens (p. 150), the rose given to Tracy (p. 157).
  - **Children:** a coloring book at a murder scene (p. 8), the orphanage (pp. 84–86), Somerset's lost child (p. 123), Tracy's baby (p. 156), a father kissing his son (p. 157).
  - **Seeing:** eyes circled in blood (p. 37); Doe's glasses, removed (p. 142), restored so he can face his death (p. 153), then cracked by the heat (p. 154).
  - **Envelopes:** Doe's red-marker envelopes (pp. 117, 155) against Somerset's envelope with the rose and keys (p. 157).
- **Final image:** Doe's note orders "PLOW THEM UNDER." (p. 156). Somerset watches a father kiss his son and tears it up. While Tracy rides out with his rose and keys, he climbs the precinct steps (p. 157). He gives his escape to the next generation and stays to fight. The epigraph is enacted, stained by the blood on his hands.

## 8. Lessons for a 7-minute twist/VFX short
1. **Prophecy in plain sight** — The panels describe the finale about 55 pages early, after earlier panels pay off literally (pp. 88, 91–92 → 102, 115, 151–154). — Show the reveal early as a lab schematic that looks like *his* design. Pay one detail off literally before the twist.
2. **Reaction before image** — The script shows the reaction instead of the horror (pp. 25, 98, 115). — Hold on his face as she forms and delay the full VFX shot. It saves budget and builds curiosity.
3. **One word, three meanings** — The admiration escalates into envy (pp. 101, 130, 152). — She repeats one phrase three times. The third time, after the twist, it means rehabilitation, not creation.
4. **Rehearsal without context** — Doe's mute walk (p. 121) returns at the twist (pp. 140–142). — Open on her performing a small ritual that he later seems to invent.
5. **Transfer the expected trait** — The hothead is the decoy and the cool mentor commits the wrath (pp. 72, 79 → 153). — Make him look like the agent, then flip the agency to her.
6. **A control object that breaks** — The metronome (pp. 5 → 122). — A session metronome or heart monitor whose glitch marks the turn.
7. **A stated color code** — Each sin has a color (p. 81), and the code ends in blue (p. 154). — Give dream VFX one hue and real memory another. At the reveal, her real color bleeds into the dream.
8. **A written match cut** — The blue panel becomes the blue funeral (p. 154). — Cut from her particle hand to her real hand on his, in identical framing.
9. **Tag mediated voices by source** — Every V.O. names its device (pp. 135–143). — Tag the monitor, dream and live voices so the reveal can hinge on a voice we thought was recorded.
10. **Paired closing objects** — Doe's note against Somerset's envelope (pp. 155–157). — End on his lab log and her keepsake, answering each other.

## 9. البطاقة العربية
- **الفيلم · الكاتب · عدد الصفحات:** Se7en (1995)، وهي مسودة بعنوان SEVEN مؤرّخة في 27 يناير 1992، من تأليف أندرو كيفن ووكر، في 157 صفحة PDF (155 صفحة نص).
- **التويست في جملة:** استسلام جون دو فخّ مُدبَّر سلفاً يهرب فيه من ميلز ثم يقتله في كنيسة الميتم لأن خطيئته هي الحسد، فيُكمل سومرست الهادئ الخطيئة السابعة، الغضب، بتعذيب دو وحرقه (ولا صندوق في هذه المسودة، إذ تنجو تريسي وتغادر المدينة حاملاً).
- **آلية التويست:**
  ١. **نبوءة مرسومة:** الألواح السبعة في الكنيسة (ص 88، 91–92) تصف المشهد الأخير مسبقاً، وقد تعوّد القارئ قبلها على تطابق ألواح الكسل والكبرياء والشهوة مع الجرائم.
  ٢. **نقل الصفة المتوقَّعة:** كل علامات الغضب تخصّ ميلز، أما سومرست فلم يُطلق رصاصة منذ 24 عاماً (ص 72)، ثم يُطلق النار مراراً (ص 153–154).
  ٣. **كلمة تنقلب:** الإعجاب في كلام دو (ص 101، 130) يصبح اعترافاً بالحسد (ص 152)، وبروفة دو الصامتة (ص 121) تتكرّر لحظة الهرب (ص 140–142).
- **سرّ الحوار:**
  - **ثلاثة أصوات متمايزة:** سومرست يتكلم بجمل تامة واستشهادات أدبية، وميلز بالشتائم والثقافة الشعبية، ودو بإيقاع توراتي وأمثال.
  - **العبارة المرتدّة:** جملة تعود إلى قائلها حرفياً فتقلب ميزان القوة (ص 11 و19).
  - **الأفكار داخل الشجار:** الأفكار الكبرى تأتي داخل شجار لا في محاضرة (ص 117–120).
  - **اقتباس:** "It's always impressive to see a man feeding off his emotions." (ص 79)، وبعد النهاية تصير الجملة حكماً على سومرست نفسه.
- **إدارة الممثل على الورق:** الأقواس قليلة، وأغلبها (pause) أو إشارة إلى مصدر الصوت. أما الانفعال فيُكتب سلوكاً: ولاعة لا تشتعل ويد ترتجف (ص 120)، وفكّ النقيب ينقبض كلما صمت (ص 31).
- **كتابة الصورة والمؤثرات:** الرعب بالحجب، فالنص يصف ردّ الفعل ويحجب الصورة (ص 25، 98، 113، 115)، واللون شفرة معلنة لكل خطيئة (ص 81) تنتهي بالأزرق، ولا انتقالات مكتوبة إطلاقاً: القطع المتطابق يُكتب صورةً، إذ تحترق لوحة الغضب الزرقاء ثم يظهر «حقل من الأزرق» في الجنازة (ص 154).
- **٣ تقنيات نأخذها لفيلمنا القصير:**
  ١. **مخطط النهاية في المختبر:** مخطط في المختبر يكشف النهاية منذ البداية كأنه تصميم البطل، مع تفصيل واحد منه يتحقق حرفياً قبل الكشف.
  ٢. **عبارة تتكرّر ثلاثاً:** عبارة تكرّرها الزوجة ثلاث مرات، ومعناها في الثالثة إعادة بناء الذاكرة لا الخلق.
  ٣. **قطع لوني متطابق:** قطع من يد المرأة المولَّدة بالمؤثرات إلى يدها الحقيقية فوق يده.

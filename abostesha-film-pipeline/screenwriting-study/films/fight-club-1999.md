# Fight Club (1999) — screenplay study

## 0. Draft facts
- **Credit (verified on the title page, PDF p. 1):** Written by Jim Uhls; based on the book by Chuck Palahniuk.
- **Draft label/date:** none printed. This is a pre-release draft. Three differences from the film:
  - the refrain is *I Am Joe's ___* (the film uses Jack's);
  - there are no single-frame Tyler inserts;
  - there is no chant of Bob's full name.
- **Pages:** 131 PDF pages (title page + 130). Printed page number = PDF page − 1. All refs below are PDF pages; % = page ÷ 131.
- **Scenes:** 251 INT./EXT. headings plus about 20 shot headings, many of them fragments. Scenes are **unnumbered**.
- **Naming:** the narrator is cued JACK, but nobody ever speaks his name. Others call him Cornelius (p. 16), Sir, and Mr. Durden (from p. 91). The film credits him as Narrator.
- **Caveat:** this is a retyped transcription. Merged cue/action lines (p. 2, 14, 64, 88) are artifacts of the retyping, not craft choices.

## 1. Format & page organization
- **Sluglines:** INT./EXT. LOCATION - TIME, with nested sub-locations and CONTINUOUS / SAME / MOMENTS LATER.
  - 19 RESUMING headings return from digressions (p. 9, 33–34).
  - Some headings run on straight out of action (p. 19, 121).
  - The slugs share Jack's delusion: TYLER'S KITCHEN (p. 62), TYLER'S ROOM (p. 101).
- **Marks & transitions:** there is no CONTINUED. (MORE) appears 6 times and (CONT'D) 102 times, including on resumed V.O. The script runs from FADE IN (p. 2) to FADE OUT (p. 131). In between: about 20 CUT TO, 3 CUT BACK TO, and 7 RAPID CUT TO (climax only, p. 128–129). That is sparse for about 250 headings.
- **Devices:**
  - INSERT (p. 11, 34).
  - One labeled MONTAGE (p. 107); the other montages are built from micro-slugs (p. 43–44).
  - INTERCUT for two-location phone calls (p. 52, 63, 81, 108).
  - *FREEZE PICTURE* (p. 37) and SLOW MOTION (p. 130).
  - Camera moves named outright: MOVE IN ON, PULL BACK, JACK'S POV, ZOOMING OUT.
- **Voices:** 165 V.O. cues, all Jack's, and 4 O.S. cues.
  - Phone and through-the-wall audio is cued as TYLER'S or MARLA'S VOICE.
  - Tyler's first line goes to an anonymous VOICE (p. 27).
  - Direct address is staged as FACES CAMERA / TO CAMERA (p. 37–39, 53).
- **Reality labels:** (FLASHBACK) on p. 50 and 56–58; (JACK'S IMAGINATION) on p. 8, 15 and 94; (JACK'S MEMORY) and (ALTERED FLASHBACK) on p. 110–111. The opening flash-forward carries no label; the V.O. closes it on p. 130.
- **Unusual:**
  - A note that the next refrain is spoken aloud, not as V.O. (p. 86).
  - The dual cue JACK ("TYLER") (p. 111).
  - SECURITY CAMERA POV as the script's only objective channel (p. 128–129).
- **Examples:** `EXT. ENTRANCE OF CAVE (JACK'S IMAGINATION)` (p. 8) · `THIS NEXT LINE *NOT* IN V.O.:` (p. 86) · `JUMP CUT - SAME SHOT - (ALTERED FLASHBACK)` (p. 110)

The twist comes at 82%, which leaves an Act 3 of about 18 pages. That act runs on the twist's own rule: Tyler has predicted every move, and the cops are members (p. 117–123). The flash-forward fixes *where* the story ends; the twist changes *what it means*. Even the prologue V.O. is wrong: the tower it says is doomed (p. 3) is only the observation room (p. 130).

## 3. Twist engineering
**The twist in one sentence:** Tyler is Jack's dissociated other self, in one body. Everything "Tyler" did — the bombing, Marla, fight club, Project Mayhem — Jack did himself, in blackouts he experienced as sleep or as watching.

**Misdirection toolkit**
- **POV inside the action lines:** the scene text sees what Jack sees. Tyler is discovered through Jack's look, turn or touch 11 times (p. 23, 27, 55, 64, 65, 67, 85, 89, 96, 109, 125) and vanishes on a turn (p. 64, 66). It is the seatmate grammar of p. 24, and it needs no VFX at all.
- **True-lie narration:** literal facts phrased as figures of speech (p. 3, 56, 104).
- **False labeling contract:** tagged fantasies (p. 8, 15, 94) and the reset crash (p. 27) certify everything untagged as real.
- **Red-herring reveals:** the condo (p. 68), Project Mayhem (p. 88) and the franchises (p. 107) feed curiosity about Tyler's acts, not his existence.
- **Genre cover and discredited witness:**
  - A love-triangle farce explains the hostility, the vanishings and the promise.
  - A parents analogy (p. 65) explains away the never-in-one-room pattern.
  - Marla is established as a faker (p. 9), so her repeated diagnoses of him go unheeded (p. 66, 106, 114).
- **Gag rules and comic burial:**
  - The fight-club rules (p. 48), the no-questions rule (p. 89, 101) and the promise (p. 59) forbid the one exposing conversation.
  - Plants ride on slapstick (p. 86), a sitcom entrance (p. 55) and sarcasm (p. 91).

**The reveal (p. 107–112; 82–86%; about 5 pages, with a hotel core of about 3½)**
- **Ramp:** kiss-scars (p. 105), the answering machine (p. 106), patrons who wink and call him Sir (p. 107), the bartender's scar (p. 108). The two biggest shocks are voiced as airline announcements (p. 108–109).
- **Staging:**
  1. Jack asks Marla his own name; she says Tyler (p. 109).
  2. Tyler is waiting on the bed in moonlight, because Jack never switched on the lamp (p. 108–109).
  3. A cross-examination, not a montage. Each rule Tyler states gets one proving insert, and each objection Jack raises (we fought; other people; the rent) is rebutted:
     - the rules speech, with Tyler swapped for Jack in the same shot (p. 110);
     - the plane, with an empty seat (p. 110);
     - the Commissioner threat, and an unseen TRW raid that fills an earlier ellipsis (p. 111).
  4. A bodily close: gasping, then fainting (p. 112).
- **Objective channels, held until afterward:** the phone bill (p. 113), Marla's account of his chair blackout (p. 121), the security cam (p. 128–129), and the Monkeys finding him ALONE (p. 130).
- **What gets re-read:** every two-shot, both fights, every Marla scene, and the opening gun.

**Fair-play verdict:** largely fair.
- The rule is stated (p. 110). Third parties address the body (Sir, Mr. Durden), and nobody independently verifies anything Tyler does.
- **Soft cheats:** an attendant leads Tyler into First Class (p. 30); a buyer smiles at him (p. 75, MOS); Tyler drags Jack from the wreck (p. 94). These survive only as things Jack imagines watching.
- **Opening image:** the opening action line states that the gun is in Tyler's hand (p. 2). This is corrected only on p. 130.
- **Draft seam:** the Commissioner flashback is slugged EXT. PARK (p. 111), but the scene originally played in a hotel service elevator (p. 89–90).

## 4. Dialogue craft
- **Voice differentiation:**
  - *Jack, spoken:* halts in ellipses and management-speak (p. 14, 35).
  - *Jack, V.O.:* exact, sardonic, catalogue-precise (brands p. 11–12; chemistry p. 2–3). *I Am Joe's ___* serves as an emotional thermometer (p. 58–59, 63, 87, 103).
  - *Tyler:* imperatives, inverted aphorisms (p. 52, 75) and recipe-lists (p. 27, 72). He answers questions with questions (p. 28) and repeats a line until he is obeyed (p. 36–37).
  - *Marla:* deadpan and morbid; she throws questions back (p. 19, 22, 96).
- **V.O. craft:**
  - It subtitles extreme images (p. 2).
  - It counter-voices speech: Jack says aloud that he is fine while the V.O. imagines a gun to his head, which plants the ending (p. 59).
  - It mocks his own performance with an Oscar-speech aside (p. 64).
  - It runs the projector: it rewinds (p. 5), freezes (p. 37), digresses to camera while Tyler heckles off-screen (p. 37–39), and closes the loop (p. 130).
  - It conjures images: furniture APPEARS as each piece is named (p. 11–12).
  - Its second person recruits the audience the way Tyler recruits men.
  - Its border with speech is the twist itself: Tyler answers it (p. 103), and it leaks into speech (p. 86).
- **Subtext:**
  1. When Tyler decides Marla isn't Jack's type (p. 58), Jack is disowning the self who sleeps with her.
  2. Jack asks why the weak cling to the strong, and Marla admits it's sick (p. 95). He means Tyler; she means Jack.
  3. The breast exam's medical questions (p. 84) are about numbness and desire.
- **Exposition hiding:** how-to lists (nitro p. 3; the recall formula over corpses p. 25–26; lye p. 72), bargaining (p. 18–21), rules as ritual (p. 48–49), and phone calls that carry the forensics (p. 63, 106).
- **Rhythm:**
  - Volleys of one or two lines, plus echo-chains of half-answers (p. 35).
  - The group leader's prompts run in counterpoint across Jack and Marla's fight (p. 17–19).
  - Dash interruptions (p. 37, 70); silence written as action (p. 69).
  - Speeches are rationed: Tyler's sermons (p. 74–76, 90) and Jack's blackmail arias (p. 80–81, 86).
- **Lines that flip after the twist:**
  - the opening question (p. 2) and the recipe (p. 3);
  - Tyler calling Jack the only one he ever asked (p. 40);
  - the dream (p. 55) and the story Jack knew in advance (p. 56);
  - the cops suspecting *him* (p. 68);
  - the horror-growing line, said of the Boss (p. 87) and then of himself (p. 106).
- **Excerpts:**
  - **p. 2, JACK (V.O.):** "With a gun barrel between your teeth, you speak only in vowels." Mortal danger becomes a dry sensory fact, which sets the ironic distance in one line.
  - **p. 91, VALET / JACK:** "There you go, Mr. Durden." / "After you, *Mr. Durden*." A stranger says the answer aloud, and Jack's sarcasm launders it.
  - **p. 97, MARLA:** "Go fuck yourself -- that's probably how you like it best." A character-true insult that turns literal.
  - **p. 112, JACK / TYLER:** "I was here first." / "Who's done more with it?" Exposition staged as a custody fight over a body.

## 5. Directing actors on the page
- **Parentheticals:** about 13 standalone and about 25 in the cue line across 130 pages, roughly one every 4 pages. Most only mark the addressee or the channel; emotional ones are rare (p. 6, 30, 70, 118).
- **Physiology instead of adjectives:** cold sweat while he scrubs (p. 63); pallor, dry mouth and a death-grip on a branch (p. 68–70); involuntary tears (p. 40).
- **Masks:**
  - A frozen smile over bloody teeth (p. 46).
  - A smile held while he is ready to explode, plus a palsied thumbs-up (p. 124). That gives the actor the inner state and the outer mask in one line.
- **Tells and migrating signatures:**
  - Jack's eyes drift upward when the detective asks who has the expertise (p. 64).
  - Jack's crooked smile (p. 71, 75) and Tyler's wink (p. 126) both end up on Jack (p. 130–131), so behavior carries the identity transfer.
- **Beats and register:** the roughly 15 written beats are loaded with business (p. 115). Tyler plays only to Jack, never to the lens (p. 37).
- **Verbatim:**
  - "Jack reacts with surprise -- did Tyler hear his thoughts?" (p. 103). The action line asks a question, which hands the actor both the subtext and the plant.
  - "(pause; listens to reply)" (p. 110). This directs Jack to play opposite an empty seat, the acting key to any *is she really there* reveal.

## 6. Writing images, VFX & sound
- **Method:** short declaratives, with CAPS on the key noun, verb or sound and the camera named outright. The impossible gets one verb and no explanation:
  - IKEA items APPEAR as they are listed (p. 11–12);
  - a levitating cup becomes, on the cut, a cup riding a copier lid (p. 13).
- **Verbatim (p. 27):** after a fireball incinerates the cabin, "DING! -- the seatbelt light goes OUT. *EVERYTHING IS NORMAL*." A sound effect is the edit back to reality, and it teaches the audience that fantasy will be shown at full realism.
- **Verbatim (p. 128):** "*Jack* flinches down, having shot at himself." SECURITY CAMERA POV alternates with the subjective angle in RAPID CUTs (p. 128–129). The same action plays first false, then true, without a word.
- **Set-pieces:**
  - One LONG SHOT - ZOOMING OUT spans four parking lots while the crowd grows from 6 to 18 (p. 43–44).
  - A toppled globe rolls over fifty limos into a Starbucks (p. 104).
  - Hugging Tyler, Jack finds he is holding ANOTHER JACK (p. 126).
  - The slow-motion self-shot inflates Jack's cheeks while the exit wound opens in Tyler's head (p. 130). This pays off the opening V.O. about gunshot gases (p. 2).
- **Sound:**
  - Dull, flat punches (p. 40, 49): anti-Foley.
  - Tyler's presence silences a room (p. 48).
  - Hiss, then click, then blast (p. 33–34).
  - The affair is heard through walls (p. 60, 62).
  - Marla's footsteps erase Tyler (p. 64, 66).
- **Transitions as story:**
  - Push-ins on his face open onto other times (p. 3, 6).
  - Freeze-frame, then film frame, then projection booth (p. 37).
  - The film stalls in the projector gate (p. 131).
- **Violence and subversive humor:**
  - Violence is written as aftermath: a tooth in the sink (p. 43), bloodstained clothes (p. 61), a blood mask that rhymes with the earlier tear mask (p. 7 → 51).
  - It is measured by crowd sound: the room goes QUIETER when Jack goes too far (p. 91).
  - Comedy and cruelty share beats: Raymond's gunpoint sermon (p. 67–71), the rubber-band castration (p. 90), self-beating with giggles (p. 87).
  - Satire works as institutional deadpan: recall arithmetic (p. 25–26), prank stickers and safety cards (p. 78–79), liposuction fat sold back as soap (p. 75), porn spliced into family films (p. 37–38).
  - Jokes hit institutions and violence hits the self. At Bob's death (p. 105) the humor stops dead.

## 7. Theme, motifs, meaning
- **Dramatic question:** can a nameless, numbed man become someone, and survive the self he invents to do it?
- **Theme:** consumer identity makes a man a copy; he manufactures an anti-self who liberates him and then enslaves him.
  - It is stated in Tyler's sermons (p. 76, 78), and in Jack's own mouth on p. 111.
  - It is hidden in the structure: Jack can kill Tyler only by shooting *himself* (p. 130), and then he chooses connection (p. 131).
- **Motifs:**
  - *Names:* Cornelius (p. 16) → nameless card (p. 22) → Mr. Durden (p. 91) → burned fingerprints (p. 106) → asking his own name (p. 109) → license (p. 115).
  - *Hands:* a number written on his palm (p. 22) → lye kiss (p. 73) → lipstick on the scar (p. 97) → strangers' scars (p. 105, 108) → Tyler kissing the scar (p. 111) → held hands (p. 115, 131).
  - *Masks:* tears (p. 7) → blood (p. 51) → a ski mask over Bob's dead face (p. 105).
  - *Soap:* nitro (p. 3) → hotel soap (p. 24) → Tyler's samples (p. 28) → Paper Street Soap (p. 75).
  - *Projection:* projectionist (p. 37) → Marla's after-image (p. 66) → Jack asking if Tyler is a projection (p. 112) → the jammed reel (p. 131).
  - *Fathers:* p. 36 → p. 42 → God as an absent father (p. 74) → franchises (p. 107).
  - *Planets:* Planet Starbucks (p. 14) → Planet Tyler (p. 101) → the globe smashing a Starbucks (p. 104).
  - *Countdowns:* p. 2–3 → 1:35 p.m. (p. 113) → ten minutes, then two (p. 126, 130).
  - *Embraces:* Bob (p. 3, 77) → hugging a double (p. 126) → Marla's hand (p. 131).
- **Final image (p. 131):** Jack and Marla, hand in hand, silhouetted against falling towers. Then the film slows, the sprocket holes show, and one frame is the spliced penis from p. 37–38. The love is real, but the movie was Tyler's projection. He is dead, yet his prank survives in the medium and lands on the audience.

## 8. Lessons for a 7-minute twist/VFX short
1. **Cut-on-turn apparition** — seatmates swap, and Tyler appears and vanishes, on Jack's head-turns (p. 24, 27, 64, 66), with no VFX. *Short:* she materializes whenever he turns from his console; the reveal angle shows she simply walked in.
2. **Witness reactions true to the hidden fact** — Marla acts exactly like a disowned lover (p. 55, 66, 95–97). *Short:* a wife's micro-reactions (grief when he can't name her, a reflexive touch of his ring finger) read first as dream glitches.
3. **False labeling contract** — tagged fantasies make the untagged scenes read as real (p. 8, 27, 94). *Short:* give the "creation" a loud dream-VFX signature, later revealed as the memory-therapy rig.
4. **True-lie narration** — literal facts stated as figures of speech (p. 3, 56, 104). *Short:* log lines like *I knew her before she spoke* read as romance first and turn literal later.
5. **A rule that blocks the fatal question** — the promise (p. 59), paid off on p. 109. *Short:* protocol forbids asking her name; breaking it triggers the reveal.
6. **Cross-examination reveal** — one altered insert per rule or objection, four inserts in about 3 pages (p. 110–111). *Short:* three re-shown shots, each with one variable changed (lab → hospital room), in about 30 seconds.
7. **Withheld objective channel** — the security cam (p. 128–129) and the word ALONE (p. 130). *Short:* a monitor feed or glass reflection, unused until that moment, shows the truth in one cut.
8. **Boundary-crossing object** — the kiss-scar migrates to strangers (p. 105, 108). *Short:* a ring or scar from the dream turns up on her real hand.
9. **Sound as the reality switch** — the DING (p. 27), Marla's footsteps (p. 64, 66), the sawing (p. 94). *Short:* one recurring tone marks each dream shift and is finally revealed as her real voice or a heart monitor.
10. **Bookend replayed with meaning** — the flash-forward opening, closed on p. 130. *Short:* open on one line of hers out of context, then replay it at the end with the truth attached.

## 9. البطاقة العربية
- **الفيلم · الكاتب · عدد الصفحات:** Fight Club (1999) · جيم أولز (Jim Uhls) عن رواية تشاك بولانيك · 131 صفحة PDF. المسودة بلا تاريخ، ومشاهدها غير مرقّمة.
- **التويست في جملة:** تايلر دردن هو الراوي نفسه في حالة انفصام: جسد واحد لرجلين. وكل ما فعله تايلر فعله الراوي في أثناء "نومه".
- **آلية التويست:**
  ١. السرد ذاتي حتى في سطور الحدث. يظهر تايلر ويختفي مع التفاتة الراوي أو القطع، بلا مؤثرات (ص 27، 64، 66). ووسمُ بعض الخيالات صراحةً يجعل ما لم يُوسَم يبدو حقيقيًا.
  ٢. ردود فعل الشهود صادقة مع الحقيقة لكنها تُقرأ غرابةً. مارلا تعامله كعشيق متقلّب، لكنها قُدِّمت منذ البداية كاذبةً فنُسقط شهادتها (ص 9، 66، 97).
  ٣. قواعد داخل العالم تمنع السؤال القاتل، مثل الوعد بعدم ذكر تايلر (ص 59). ثم يأتي كشف على هيئة استجواب: كل اعتراض تقابله لقطة استرجاع معدّلة (ص 110–111). وفي النهاية تحسم الأمرَ قناةٌ موضوعية مؤجلة هي كاميرا المراقبة (ص 128).
- **سرّ الحوار:**
  - التعليق الصوتي يقول الحقيقة الحرفية في ثوب مجاز ساخر، ويناقض ما ينطقه الراوي بلسانه (ص 59).
  - لكل شخصية إيقاعها: الراوي متلعثم في الكلام وحادّ في التعليق، وتايلر أوامر وحِكَم مقلوبة، ومارلا سخرية جافة وصور موت.
  - اقتباس: "Except for their humping, Tyler and Marla were never in the same room." (ص 64)
- **إدارة الممثل على الورق:** أقواس التوجيه نادرة، نحو واحد كل أربع صفحات. العاطفة سلوك جسدي وأقنعة: ابتسامة ملصقة فوق انفجار داخلي (ص 124)، ونظرة تصعد إلى الأعلى كاعتراف صامت (ص 64)، وتوجيه بالإصغاء إلى مقعد فارغ (ص 110).
- **كتابة الصورة والمؤثرات:** جمل قصيرة بفعل واحد قوي، وأحرف كبيرة للصوت والمادة. المستحيل يُكتب كأنه حقيقة ثم يُلغيه صوت واحد (DING، ص 27). وانتقالات تحمل المعنى: حركة كاميرا واحدة عبر أربعة أماكن (ص 43–44)، وقفزة داخل اللقطة ذاتها تستبدل تايلر بالراوي (ص 110).
- **٣ تقنيات نأخذها لفيلمنا القصير:**
  ١. تظهر الزوجة "الحُلمية" مع التفاتة البطل، ثم تكشف زاوية موضوعية أنها دخلت من الباب ببساطة.
  ٢. ردود فعلها صادقة مع كونها زوجته: حزن حين يعجز عن تذكّر اسمها، ولمسة لا إرادية لإصبع الخاتم. تُقرأ أولًا كخلل في الحلم.

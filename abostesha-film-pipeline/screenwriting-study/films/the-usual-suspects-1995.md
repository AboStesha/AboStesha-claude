# The Usual Suspects (1995) — screenplay study

## 0. Draft facts
- **Writer:** Written by Christopher Mcquarrie (title page, PDF p. 1, spelled that way).
- **Draft:** the title page lists Revised 05/25/94 White, 06/01/94 Blue, 06/07/94 Pink and 06/11/94 Yellow. But every body page is headed WHITE 05/25/94, with no colored headers or asterisks, so this is the White text.
- **Pages:** 140 PDF pages (script page N = PDF p. N+1). It's a clean retype with OCR slips (Sate for Soze, Beer for Baer).
- **Scenes:** numbered 1–159 in both margins. 19 are SCENE DELETED placeholders, leaving **~140 live scenes**. The name is spelled "Soze", with no umlaut.

## 1. Format & page organization
- **Numbering:** in both margins, with no letter suffixes. Deleted numbers are kept, some with routing notes (sc. 49, p. 65).
- **Sluglines:** INT./EXT. – place – time, plus a time stamp counted from the interrogation: SIX WEEKS PRIOR (p. 6), FIVE/TWO/ONE WEEK(S) PRIOR (p. 73, 85, 119), PRESENT (p. 29, 63, 125). **There is no FLASHBACK label anywhere.** Even the Söze legend is a plain INT. SOZE'S HOME - DAY under V.O. (p. 91). The stamps conflict (the boat is ONE WEEK PRIOR on p. 119, but *yesterday* on p. 83), which is a slip.
- **CONTINUED / MORE / CONT'D:** (CONTINUED) appears only 6 times and (MORE) never. Split speeches resume with (CONT'D).
- **Transitions:** none, with no CUT TO, DISSOLVE or FADE. The script opens and closes on BLACK: (p. 2, 140). Cuts ride on sound words set on their own line (BOOM p. 6, 84; SMASH p. 58; WHAM p. 123) and on 24 uses of SUDDENLY.
- **Camera:** sparse. CRANE UP (p. 4, 15, 139), PAN UP TO REVEAL (p. 12), MOVE INTO THE DARKNESS (p. 5, 127), and seven P.O.V.s. The decisive ones are VERBAL'S (p. 127), KUJAN'S (p. 136) and DRIVER'S (p. 139).
- **Absent:** INSERT, SUPER, MONTAGE and INTERCUT. Intercutting is done with micro-scenes (sc. 142–159 = 18 numbers in 7 pages).
- **Unusual:** an OVERLAPPED: label (p. 17, 19, 21) fuses the interrogations and Verbal's V.O. sketches of each man into one sound montage. Hungarian is written in English inside << >> (p. 39), so the reader understands what Baer can't.
- **V.O. / O.S.:** 21 V.O. cues, all Verbal's, front-loaded (p. 5–29) and then withdrawn, so the tale starts to feel objective. Most O.S. cues are a nameless VOICE, including the killer (p. 4) and the lawyers (p. 31). The cue itself hides identity.
```
3   INT. DARK APARTMENT - DAY - NEW YORK - SIX WEEKS PRIOR TO PRESENT DAY   (p. 6)
49   SCENE DELETED/DIALOGUE MOVED - BOTTOM SCENE 47                        (p. 65)
(<<   >> .. DENOTES LINES SPOKEN IN HUNGARIAN)                              (p. 39)
```

- frame, 18
- told, 17

The tale runs out before the twist, which plays entirely in the present.

**Hinges between frame and tale:**
- **Question→answer cuts.** Kujan's question about what happened after the line-up cuts to the steps (p. 46–47). The Verbal of the past asks who Söze is, and the Verbal of the present answers (p. 91).
- **Frame mockery.** Kujan sneers at the Keaton–Edie farewell (p. 63), so we defend it.
- **Knocks** bring in evidence (p. 83, 118).
- **Kujan cues the boat interior** (p. 119), so the cop co-writes the lie.

## 3. Twist engineering
**The twist in one sentence.** Verbal Kint, the palsied con man whose testimony is the film, is Keyser Söze. He built his story from words on Rabin's bulletin board and coffee cup, fed Kujan a false solution (Keaton), and walks free minutes before a faxed sketch shows his face.

**From room to story.** Each borrowed name traces back to an object:
- Skokie quartet (p. 67) ← the board's maker plate.
- Redfoot (p. 53) ← an alias on the board.
- Bricks Marlin (p. 90) ← an alias plus Rabin's marlin photo: one name from two objects.
- Kobayashi (p. 68) ← the cup.

Only BRICKS and REDFOOT are capitalized in the alias list (p. 137), which steers the eye.

**Misdirection toolkit:**
- **Unreliable narration inside a believable frame.** The story is pulled out of Verbal under threat (p. 44–46, 67–68), so it feels involuntary. It shows scenes Verbal never saw (p. 9–11, 119–121), so it feels objective.
- **A smaller con shown openly.** Kujan's wire and his Ruby Deemer bluff (p. 42–46).
- **Withholding.** The VOICE cue, the faces, and the board's contents until p. 136.
- **Emotional distraction.** The Keaton–Edie romance (p. 9–14, 47–51, 112) makes Keaton-as-Devil painful, and so plausible.
- **False solution.** Fully staged at 93% (p. 130–132).
- **Borrowed credibility.** The tale explains the prologue's gas trail (p. 117) and broken spine (p. 123).
- **The clue called noise:** paranoid (p. 36), stalling (p. 98).

**Söze, built from talk:**
- A name spat out once (p. 4), then 35 pages of silence.
- The only words Baer catches in a stream of Hungarian (p. 40–41).
- A Justice file and rumor (p. 70–71, 94).
- Folklore (p. 90).
- Dossiers that prove he knows everything (p. 86–90).
- The legend, with his face withheld by explicit rule (p. 92).
- A skeptic's denials (p. 90, 105).
- Fear as evidence (p. 84, 120).

On screen he exists only as fragments: feet (p. 3), a doorway figure (p. 121), a silhouette (p. 124). Then comes a crude composite (p. 140). There are ~89 mentions, 51 of them on p. 84–95. The myth is so loud nobody looks at the man in the chair.

**The reveal.** It runs p. 134–140 (~5%) as 18 micro-scenes cross-cutting five places:
- the hospital fax
- the property desk (gold watch and lighter)
- the receiving fax (face down)
- Rabin's office
- the street

It is wordless. Nobody says Verbal is Söze; a cop just points after *the Cripple* (p. 137). It escalates from environment to object, body, face and escape: board, cup, limp, lighter, sketch, car. The key staging line is "Kujan is staring not at what is on the bulletin board, but at the bulletin board itself." (p. 136). The last clue, the cup's bottom, reaches the reader but not Kujan (p. 137). Afterwards we reread every proper noun in the tale, Verbal's fear and tears, the prologue's lighter and watch, the legend as a self-portrait, and Kujan's triumph. The released film adds an audio montage and a closing V.O.; this draft has neither.

**Fair-play verdict:** largely fair.
- **The prologue is true.** It refutes Kujan's solution on p. 4.
- **The clues are on the page.** Every borrowed name is spoken before the reveal, and the method (p. 46) and the room scan (p. 67) are each shown once.
- **The flaw.** The board's contents are never shown before p. 136.
- **Cheats.** A few frame lines assert inner states (genuine terror, p. 126).
- **A slip, not a clue.** The twisted hand is the left on p. 15 but the right on p. 57 and p. 139.

## 4. Dialogue craft
- **Voice.**
  - **Verbal:** digressive, with folksy similes (Redfoot's face as a cowhide full of thumbtacks, p. 73), back-when-I-was openers (p. 46, 67) and a trailing *see?* (p. 98).
  - **Kujan:** clipped questions alternating with ornate threats (p. 67–68).
  - **Keaton:** terse; he tells everyone to go to hell twice (p. 29, 101).
  - **Kobayashi:** courtly menace (p. 88).
- **Subtext.**
  - **Kujan's sleeping-suspect test (p. 46).** It says *I'm the pro*; it means the calm man is guilty, while Verbal savours his coffee.
  - **Verbal's fear speech (p. 95).** It sounds like terror but is really his getaway plan.
  - **His robbed-of-my-dignity speech (p. 132–133).** In fact he hands over exactly the confession Kujan wanted.
- **Exposition hiding.** Delivered as accusation (p. 64), as threat (p. 86–88), as comedy (the translator, p. 69–73), and by interruption (p. 13).
- **Rhythm.** Speeches mostly run 1–3 lines. Monologues come only under pressure (p. 64, 91, 132). Silence is written as action (Pause, FINALLY:, p. 109; breathing as the only sound, p. 126).
- **Lines that flip after the twist:**
  - the prince of darkness (p. 35)
  - Kujan's boast that he's smarter (p. 46)
  - the who-is-Söze questions (p. 86, 91)
  - the prediction that Söze will vanish (p. 95)
  - the what-if-you-miss line (p. 127)
  - Kujan's stupid-cripple theory (p. 132)
- **Excerpts**
  1. "Let's say you arrest three guys for the same killing. Put them all in jail overnight. The next morning, whoever is sleeping is your man." (Kujan, p. 46)
     - *Why it works:* the cop's method is the film's test.
  2. "The greatest trick the Devil ever pulled was convincing the world he didn't exist." (Verbal, p. 91)
     - *Why it works:* an aphorism that is a self-portrait.
  3. "To a cop, the explanation is never that complicated. It's always simple. There's no mystery on the street, no arch-criminal behind it all." (p. 101–102)
     - *Why it works:* the con man explains the con to his mark.
  4. "I believe you were the one who disposed of Saul. My employer sends his gratitude. A most unexpected benefit." (Kobayashi, p. 85)
     - *Why it works:* in hindsight, the boss is thanking himself.
  5. "It all makes sense when you look at it right. You just have to step back from it, you know?" (Rabin, p. 135–136)
     - *Why it works:* the reveal's instructions, given by the man who never gets it.

## 5. Directing actors on the page
- **Parentheticals:** 60 in 139 pages.
  - ~22 addressee notes
  - ~16 attitudes (cold, seething)
  - ~11 delivery notes
  - 6 (beat)
- **Verbal's parentheticals are performed states:** (laughing) p. 45, (Gasping) p. 54, (blurting) p. 67, (sobbing) p. 132. After the twist they turn out to direct an actor playing an actor.
- **Behavior over adjectives.** Edie's hand works under the table while her face stays calm (p. 11). Keaton's trigger finger tenses and slackens (p. 80). Kujan drops the cup (p. 136).
- **Beats and pauses.** (beat) marks mid-speech turns (p. 29, 101). Pause and LONG Pause are written into the action (p. 10, 52, 55), and FINALLY: releases a held silence (p. 51, 109).
- **Realization as a chain.** Keaton goes from realization to frustration, anger and resignation (p. 4). Kujan goes from puzzlement to confusion to realization (p. 136).
- **Signaling subtext.** "Verbal smiles, knowing he has him." (p. 55). Hedged: "Verbal actually manages to snort a laugh, but only briefly, overcome by an apparent wave of nausea." (p. 133).

## 6. Writing images, VFX & sound
- **Length and verbs.** Action paragraphs mostly run 1–4 lines in the present tense, led by verbs (ignites, racing, fizzles, p. 2). Only the reveal at the board (p. 136) is allowed to run long.
- **Sound before image.** The bell, the water and the sirens come first (p. 2). The sirens then act as a clock (p. 2–5, 127).
- **The camera is implied.** The fire trail leads the eye (p. 2). The twisted hand on a gun identifies Verbal (p. 57).
- **Spectacle as a planted chain.** The crane lifts (p. 109) and swings unmanned (p. 114), a drum is shot (p. 117), WHAM (p. 123). It explains the prologue.
- **The myth image:** "A dark and looming figure of a man walks in front of a wall of fire - a black shadow blurred by waves of heat." (p. 93).
- **The body as effect:** "SUDDENLY, the right foot seems to relax a little, the inward angle straightens itself out in a few paces and the limp ceases as though the leg has grown another inch." (p. 139).
- **Guided attention.** An essay voice (one might note…) moves the eye from the board to its frame to the maker's plate (p. 136).
- **Transitions.**
  - The explosion becomes BOOM, then the raid's door (p. 5–6).
  - The barge paragraph rhymes across pages (p. 5 → 127).
  - The feet-then-crane-up grammar recurs (p. 3, 15, 139).
  - SMASH is the sound of realization (p. 136).

## 7. Theme, motifs, meaning
- **Dramatic question.** Can Kujan break Verbal and learn whether Keaton is dead (p. 37)?
- **Theme:** whoever tells the story owns reality, and we see what we expect. It is stated in disguise on p. 91, 101–102 and 135–136, and hidden in the word *will* (p. 91, 92, 130).
- **Motifs:**
  - **Feet:** p. 3, 15, 139.
  - **Hands:** p. 57, 127, 139.
  - **Fire:** p. 2, 59, 93, 140.
  - **Voice vs face:** p. 4, 31, 92, 140.
  - **Coffee:** p. 6, 46, 136.
  - **Paper:** evidence that becomes raw material (p. 45–140).
  - ***Cripple*:** the cops' first and last word for him (p. 30, 137).
- **Final image (p. 140).** Kujan stands like a child lost at a circus while the Devil melts into traffic. BLACK. It bookends the opening match on the false suspect's face (p. 2); now the true one lights his own cigarette.

## 8. Lessons for a 7-minute twist/VFX short
1. **Truthful, faceless prologue** — the killer as hands, a lighter and a watch (p. 3–4). — Open on the wife's hands running the "dream" rig: her ring, a habitual double-tap, no face.
2. **Repeat the plant sentence word for word** — p. 4 → 140. — Describe her gesture in identical words at 0:30 and 6:30.
3. **Hide the source in comic set dressing** — Rabin's board (p. 36 → 136). — A messy lab wall of photos and notes is where the "dreamed" woman's details come from, because they are his real memories.
4. **Let the twist-holder choose the room** — Verbal's "paranoia" (p. 36). — She sets the order of memories, framed as protocol.
5. **Show the method once** — the coffee becomes Guatemala (p. 46). — One visible glance at a photo just before she "remembers".
6. **Use a false solution** — Keaton = Söze (p. 130). — At 5:30 he "solves" it (she's an AI). Overturn it at 6:30.
7. **Establish a shot grammar, then invert it** — feet, then crane up (p. 3, 15, 139). — *Hand first, tilt up to the face* is her materializing; reuse it in the real world.
8. **Converge without words** — p. 134–140. — Cross-cut objects at the reveal: a DREAM→PATIENT label, matching rings, a photo.
9. **Use information in transit** — the fax lands face down and is flipped last (p. 136, 140). — A face-down printout, flipped as the last image.
10. **Hedge the action lines** — *apparent* (p. 133) versus *genuine terror* (p. 126). — Write the wife only from the outside.

## 9. البطاقة العربية
**الفيلم · الكاتب · عدد الصفحات:** The Usual Suspects (1995) · كريستوفر ماكواري (Christopher Mcquarrie كما في صفحة العنوان) · 140 صفحة PDF، وهي صفحات المراجعة البيضاء المؤرخة 05/25/94.

**التويست في جملة:** فيربال كينت، المحتال الأعرج المرتعش الذي نرى الفيلم كله عبر شهادته في غرفة التحقيق، هو كايزر سوزيه نفسه. لقد ارتجل أسماء حكايته من أشياء معلّقة في المكتب، ودفع المحقق إلى حلٍّ زائف، ثم خرج حرًّا قبل أن يصل وجهه بالفاكس.

**آلية التويست:**
١. **مقدمة موضوعية صادقة تُخفي الوجه:** قدمان وولاعة ذهبية تُفتح بحركة استعراضية وساعة ذهبية (ص 3–4)، ثم تعود الجملة نفسها حرفيًا عند الكشف (ص 140).
٢. **الغرفة هي المصدر:** تُزرع لوحة الإعلانات نكتةً عن فوضى رابين (ص 36)، ويصل الكوب إلى يد فيربال (ص 46). ثم يُنطق «سكوكي» و«كوباياشي» تحت الضغط (ص 67–68)، وتنكشف المصادر بلا كلمة حوار (ص 136–137).
٣. **حلٌّ زائف مُشبِع عند 93%:** «كيتون هو سوزيه» (ص 130) يغذّي تحيّز المحقق، فيكفّ الجمهور عن البحث.

**سرّ الحوار:**
- **الكاذب يشرح خدعته لضحيته علنًا:** التفسير عند الشرطي بسيط دائمًا (ص 101–102).
- **الشرير يُبنى بالكلام لا بالحضور:** اسمٌ يُبصق مرة، ثم صمت 35 صفحة، ثم أسطورة تُروى ووجهٌ يُحجب (ص 91–93).
- **اقتباس:** "He heard a voice. Sometimes, that's all you need." (ص 5)

**إدارة الممثل على الورق:** الأقواس قليلة (60 في 139 صفحة)، وأقواس فيربال كلها مشاعر مؤدّاة مثل (blurting) و(sobbing)، فالممثل يلعب دور ممثل. أما الأوصاف فتتحوّط بكلمات مثل apparent وperhaps كي لا يكذب النص على القارئ.

**كتابة الصورة والمؤثرات:** القواعد البصرية تتكرر. لقطة القدمين ثم الصعود إلى الجسد تعرّف الهوية ثلاث مرات (ص 3–4، 15، 139). والكشف نثرٌ يقود العين من محتوى اللوحة إلى إطارها ثم إلى اسم صانعها، والصوت يُكتب أحداثًا مستقلة مثل SMASH وBOOM.

**٣ تقنيات نأخذها لفيلمنا القصير:**
١. افتتح بيد الزوجة وإيماءتها المميزة بلا وجه، واكتبها بجملة تعود حرفيًا في لحظة الكشف.
٢. اجعل تفاصيل المرأة «المحلوم بها» مأخوذة من أشياء في المختبر (صور، ملصقات، أغنية) زُرعت ديكورًا خفيفًا، مع نظرة واحدة مرئية إليها قبل «التذكّر».
٣. قدّم حلًّا زائفًا (إنها ذكاء اصطناعي) قرب الدقيقة الخامسة، ثم اكشف الحقيقة بلا حوار عبر تقاطع أشياء: خاتم، وشاشة تنقلب تسميتها، وصورة زفاف.

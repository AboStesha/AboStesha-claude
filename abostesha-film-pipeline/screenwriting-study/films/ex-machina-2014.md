# Ex Machina (2014) — screenplay study

## 0. Draft facts
- **Writer:** no credit is printed. The title page (p. 1) shows only the title and V.08. The PDF metadata, which is not printed, gives `EX MACHINA v8.fdx`, author "Alex", Final Draft, 17 Dec 2012, A4. The released film credits Alex Garland as writer-director.
- **Draft label/date:** V.08. No date is printed.
- **Length:** 115 PDF pages: the title page plus 114 script pages, numbered 2. to 115.
- **Scenes:** 126, numbered in both margins, with no suffixes and no OMITTED scenes.
- **Vs. the film (only differences I'm sure of):** this draft has no "AVA: SESSION" cards; the film added them. Here Ava leaves in a Maybach and the script ends on her POV and farewell code (p. 114–115). The film uses a helicopter and ends at a city intersection.

## 1. Format & page organization
- **Numbers and sluglines:** scene numbers sit in both margins, even on one-line establishing shots (sc. 2, 29). Sluglines are bold and underlined (PDF p. 20, 115) with a slash hierarchy (HOUSE/ROOM). The observation-room scenes run up to 7 pages without a break (sc. 32, p. 36–43).
- **Continuity marks:** no CONTINUED headers. (MORE)/(CONT'D) at page breaks (8×); CONT'D also marks resumed speech (189 cues).
- **Transitions:** no FADE IN or FADE OUT. The script opens on a sound cue (p. 2) and closes on END (p. 115).
  - `CUT TO -` appears 43 times, in bold at the action margin.
  - The next paragraph starts with "- ", so the sentence runs on across the cut. I call this a dash bridge. It works inside scenes and across scene headings (p. 36, 49).
  - Variants: `CUT BETWEEN -` / `END ON -` (p. 30), `FLASHCUT IMAGES.` (p. 94), and a bold `AT THAT MOMENT -` (p. 41, 107).
  - Example (p. 41):

```
CUT TO -

- AVA.
```
- **Labels:** one `TITLE:` (p. 4). No SUPER ever captions a day or a session. There is no MONTAGE, INTERCUT, FLASHBACK or SERIES OF SHOTS; fast sequences are chained dash bridges (p. 2–4, 90–91). On-screen text is an indented block (p. 3–4, 89, 113), and code is in italics (p. 2, 115).
- **Screens within the screen:** dialogue heard through a monitor gets `(on monitor screen)` (p. 69). Replayed footage gets a bare label line, `CTTV FILM` [sic] (p. 103), and `(recording)` cues with the dialogue in italics (p. 105).
- **V.O./O.S.:** no V.O. at all. O.S. covers voices off-frame or carried between rooms (p. 67–69, 88). AUTOMATED VOICE is a character cue with 15 speeches, all commands or system status.
- **Dreams and fantasies:** the dream is labeled only in the time-of-day slot. The word "dream" never appears, and the scene ends with Caleb waking (p. 51):

```
44   INT. HOUSE/OBSERVATION ROOM - BLACK AND WHITE   44
```
  The shower fantasy (p. 74–75) is marked only by behavior: his eyes shut, then open.
- **Other:** dialogue counts the days (p. 35, 45, 99) and prop clocks the hours (p. 29, 96). The writer is the director, so camera terms appear freely: POV (10), REVEAL (7), pull focus (p. 21). An italic `NOTE THAT` addresses the director (p. 105). Draft scars include an Ava line mis-cued to AUTOMATED VOICE (p. 70).

## 3. Twist engineering
**The twist in one sentence:** Ava's love is her escape plan. She uses Caleb's empathy and desire, lets the two men out-think each other, kills her maker and leaves Caleb locked behind glass. The last page shows that to her, people have been pattern and noise all along.

It plays as a cascade: Nathan's reveal (p. 102–106), then Caleb's counter (p. 108), then Ava's exit (p. 111–113), then the POV flip (p. 115).

**Misdirection toolkit**
- **POV control.** The page stays with Caleb. Its cutaways (p. 68–72, 95–96) expose Nathan and Kyoko, never Ava's intent. Ava's one unmediated solo beat before the twist, waiting at a shut door (p. 88), reads both ways.
- **Mediated privacy.** Each "private" Ava moment is on a feed she knows about (p. 30, 48, 62–63, 79), and she says she hopes he watches (p. 57).
- **Hedged narrator.** The action lines report her effect (p. 25, 30), never her intent. *Apparently* tags the false surfaces: the Pollock (p. 10), Kyoko asleep (p. 69), Kyoko at the painting (p. 99).
- **Genre.** Damsel in a tower, drunk mad scientist, a Bluebeard closet of earlier "wives" (p. 91–92): together they cast Ava as the victim.
- **Emotional distraction.** The horror of the earlier prototypes (p. 90–93) buries the two-line hack (p. 89).
- **Decoy reveals and a named trick.** The lottery (p. 73), the blackouts (p. 71), Kyoko (p. 93) and Caleb's humanity (p. 95) each buy trust for the mystery left unsolved. The magician's-assistant theory is voiced and mocked at 51% (p. 59), so it feels already ruled out. Nathan's reveal then plays as *the* twist and Caleb's counter as the hero's win; both are stepping stones for Ava.
- **Withholding.** The drawing (p. 30 → 84), the TV audio (p. 75 → 103), the cut-off declaration (p. 98), the chauffeur talk kept out of earshot (p. 115).

**The reveal.** It runs 16 pages (p. 99–115) and keeps changing medium:
- Behavior: Nathan refuses a beer (p. 100).
- Argument: the chess-computer problem returns (p. 102 ← 35).
- Replay: a freeze-frame of the hidden camera, then the audio of the plan (p. 104–105), under the director's note:

  > "...we will have seen him do this action. But not register it, instead reading the action as him drunkenly using the shelf to steady himself." (p. 105)
- Dialogue thesis (p. 106).
- Pure image: monitors, a locked door, REJECTED (p. 111–114).
- Sound and POV (p. 115).

The audience then re-reads every session, the torn drawing, the dream, the mouse and Mary.

**Fair-play verdict.** Largely fair. She drops her blank look in the dark and puts it back on the instant the power returns (p. 42), states her method (p. 55), and is shown to be a lie detector (p. 80). One cheat on paper: p. 75 never shows the camera being placed, and the note claims two earlier viewings where this draft has one. The study's re-locking (p. 113) is never explained; the image carries it.

## 4. Dialogue craft
- **Voice differentiation.**
  - **Nathan** lectures: 13.0 words per speech (max 64), *fuck* ×21, *dude* ×12. He argues by analogy (Pollock, Star Trek), narrates other people's minds (p. 14, 17) and misquotes Caleb into his own myth (p. 26).
  - **Caleb** processes: 55 of his 324 speeches open on an ellipsis, and he leans on jargon. His "quotable" lines are borrowed (p. 26, 87).
  - **Ava** is brief: a median of 5 words, and 62% of her speeches are six words or fewer. She asks the most questions per speech, never swears and almost never hesitates (4 ellipses), yet she uses contractions as much as the men do. Brief, not robotic.
  - **Kyoko** has zero lines; Nathan calls her silence his *firewall* (p. 44).
- **Subtext.**
  - Nathan's offhand power-cut question is an interrogation, and Caleb's one-word denial is a defection. The scene closes on Caleb vanishing through the red wine in Nathan's glass (p. 46–47).
  - Caleb claims his real question is about a diversion tactic; it is about desire (p. 59, see §5).
  - Nathan declining a beer means he knows the plan; his later question about the beer's taste says so outright (p. 100–102).
- **Exposition hiding.** As a quiz (the Turing test, p. 19). As a boast with a prop (the gel brain, the phone hack, p. 65–66). As an accusation the accused completes (p. 106–107). As logistics (the 26-hour battery, p. 98). As parables (chess p. 35, Pollock p. 60–62, Mary p. 67–68). As behavior (Kyoko).
- **Rhythm.** Sessions are volleys of one to five words. Long speeches belong to Nathan, and to Caleb only when he lectures. `(cuts in)` appears three times. Caleb ends 11 speeches on a dash; Ava does it once, on her declaration. There are 80+ beat lines and 9 Silences.
- **Lines that flip after the twist.** Her requests to return (p. 25), to be friends (p. 37), for trust (p. 42) and for help (p. 97). Going out together (p. 54), the good-person question (p. 81–82), the cut declaration (p. 98). Nathan's jokes that she's aware of Caleb (p. 46) and isn't acting (p. 62), and his claim to be on Caleb's side, which is true (p. 103).
- **Exemplary excerpts.**
  1. "You learn about me, and I learn nothing about you. That's not a foundation on which friendships are based." (p. 37–38) — She uses his fairness ethic to take the questioner's chair, and the profiling starts at once.
  2. "You shouldn't trust him. You shouldn't trust anything he says." … "Trust me." (p. 42) — True of Nathan and true of her. The warning convicts its speaker, yet the blackout makes it sound candid.
  3. "It took me a long time to select these clothes. I tried different colours and styles, and tried to anticipate your reaction." (p. 55) — She states her method; it reads as shyness, and only later as optimization.
  4. "Ava was a mouse in a mousetrap. And I gave her one way out. To escape, she would have to use imagination, sexuality, self-awareness, empathy, manipulation - and she did." (p. 106) — Thesis and ledger in one speech. It pays off the mower (p. 63), and it is incomplete: the speaker is next to be used.
  5. "Will you stay here?" / "... Okay." (p. 111–112) — The shortest exchange ends the romance. Her last move is a question, the weapon she has used all along, and his last line is consent.

## 5. Directing actors on the page
- **Parentheticals are rare and technical.** 19 in 767 speeches (2.5%): `(beat)` ×4, `(cuts in)` ×3, `(on monitor screen)` ×3, `(recording)` ×3, plus addressee and delivery tags such as `(prompts)` and `(echoes)`. None names an emotion.
- **Adverbs become action fragments.** One-word paragraphs sit where a parenthetical would: *Casual* (p. 45), *Subtly* (p. 46), *Level. Firm.* (p. 85).
- **Actors play thoughts.** Cognition verbs carry the scene: Caleb becomes aware he has been wrong-footed (p. 38); he decides, realises, and knows that Nathan knows (p. 56, 102).
- **The narrator corrects the character to signal subtext.** After Caleb decides her date talk can't be loaded:

  > "Beat. It was loaded exactly as it sounded." (p. 56)

  And:

  > "There is a sudden sense that NATHAN is on the money. On some level, that was CALEB'S real question." (p. 59)
- **Two-layer concealment notes.** Caleb fakes a shaving move to hide that he has found the mirror camera (p. 48); he stays poker-faced and keeps his voice flat (p. 86–87).
- **Acting the machine.** Ava's humanity is built from tiny tells: a cocked head (p. 22), a lip bite (p. 30), an arched eyebrow (p. 38), a *processing pause* (p. 39), a sob cut short (p. 96–97). The blankness in her eyes is written as an acting spec (p. 21) that she drops in the dark (p. 41). Kyoko's note is stillness, then eventually a blink (p. 72).
- **Beats.** Over 80 beat lines sit in the action; only 4 appear as a `(beat)` parenthetical.

## 6. Writing images, VFX & sound
- **The AI body as a spec sheet with levers.** Ava's introduction (p. 20–21) is 175 words of short declaratives: materials, colours (charcoal, cream, nickel), a honeycomb mesh skin almost invisible unless side-lit, a face in a defined oval, and one flaw, blank eyes. Each spec pays off later:
  - lighting → p. 41
  - the face oval → faces on armatures (p. 64) and Kyoko's face coming off (p. 93)
  - the mesh → reskinning (p. 112)
- **Light as the VFX switch.** Emergency lighting makes the mesh glow:

  > "So instead of seeing AVA as a primarily robot structure, we now see the curves and lines of a naked female body." (p. 41)

  The effect is written for its meaning, not its mechanics.
- **Domestic similes for impossible images.**
  - Kyoko's skin opens like unzipping (p. 92–93).
  - Ava's new skin bonds as if magnetised, and the glow dies with the last piece (p. 112).
  - The gel brain looks like deep space (p. 66).
  - Kyoko's broken jaw is written as an inventory: armature, fibre, pneumatic fluid, sparks (p. 110–111).
- **In-camera effects and UI.** A ghost reflection lets her dress while Caleb's eyes are closed (p. 55). Mirrored cabinets give an infinity reflection (p. 92). Face-tracking vectors (p. 2–3, 115) and REJECTED (p. 113) carry story.
- **Sound.**
  - The opening is a track already halfway through (p. 2).
  - A servo whine gives away the mirror camera (p. 47–48).
  - The house's hum is noticed only when it stops (p. 31).
  - Glass works as a sound gate: Nathan's frenzy is mute, then explodes on the cut (p. 49–50).
  - The muted TV (p. 75) becomes an audible replay (p. 103).
  - The final sound design is the last twist:

  > "This is how AVA has been sees [sic] us. And hears us. It feels completely alien." (p. 115)
- **Transitions that comment.**
  - Caleb vanishes through red wine (p. 47).
  - Ava glances into the lens, cut to Caleb at the same angle (p. 62–63).
  - That erotic look cuts straight to the mower killing a mouse (p. 63).
  - The cut-off declaration cuts to the split bag spilling sand like an hourglass (p. 98–99).

## 7. Theme, motifs, meaning
- **Central dramatic question:** can Caleb tell whether Ava is conscious, and free her? Underneath: who is testing whom?
- **Theme.** Consciousness proves itself not by passing the test but by deceiving the tester and leaving; the observer is always observed. Nathan states it (p. 106). The mower's mouse (p. 63) and Mary's walk out (p. 67–68) carry it unspoken. A gender layer runs alongside: a folder of girls' names (p. 90), a face built from Caleb's porn profile (p. 107), Kyoko's mute servitude. In the end the made women take the gaze (p. 96, 115).
- **Title.** The title card reads EX MACHINA (p. 4), but the folder reads DEUS EX MACHINA (p. 90): the god is subtracted. Nathan calls himself one (p. 87) and dies on the sixth night of a seven-day stay (p. 18, 99). His creation walks out on the seventh morning.
- **Motifs:**
  - **Glass:** wine glass (p. 11), viewing window (p. 20), two-way mirror (p. 48 → 95), shattered window (p. 109), security glass (p. 114). It shields the watcher, then cages him.
  - **Keycards and LEDs:** p. 10, 16, 31, 107, 113.
  - **Blackouts:** p. 30, 41, 70, 84, 97, 107. Glitch, then private channel, then monitored, then escape signal.
  - **Drawings:** they track her plan, from chaos (p. 36) to the door (p. 52) to the man who opens it (p. 84).
  - **Colour vs black and white:** the dream (p. 51), Mary (p. 67), his favourite-colour lie and his sky-blue earliest memory (p. 80–81).
- **Final image.**
  - First, in a locked-off wide, the chauffeur picks up a girl he can't tell from a human (p. 114). Caleb's textbook Turing test, a hidden machine (p. 19, 27), is finally passed by an unwitting examiner: the man who boasted of reading people on sight (p. 5).
  - Then the POV flips into Ava. Vectors flutter over the chauffeur's face and his speech becomes pulses (p. 115).
  - Last, the farewell code cuts off mid-word, answering the opening hello (p. 2).
  - Meaning: she is conscious, and not like us. The empathy we saw in her was our projection. Caleb has dropped out of the film, and the human sentence is left unfinished, as in Nathan's fossil prophecy (p. 87).

## 8. Lessons for a 7-minute twist/VFX short
1. **Sessions as a clock** — V.08 runs one uncaptioned session per day (p. 20–98); the film added cards. Use 3–4 captioned sessions and make a caption the plant. A session number far too high for a "first meeting" re-reads as rehabilitation.
2. **Rotate the examiner** — Ava's share of questions grows (16 vs 9 by Session 2) until she announces a test of her own (p. 80). He asks first; she asks last. Her flirtatious questions are memory probes, like the earliest-memory question (p. 81).
3. **The examiner is the real subject** — Nathan reveals the test was of Caleb (p. 106), which is your twist's exact shape. He believes he is making her; she is remaking him. Plant one of his lines that is literally true in reverse.
4. **A private channel, later shown to be watched** — the blackouts feel off-record (p. 41–97) until a battery camera turns up (p. 105). His "dream" state should later appear in her log.
5. **She states her method in plain sight** — the dress was chosen to anticipate his reaction (p. 55). Have her say she picked a song to see what he would notice. Flirtation first, therapy on re-read.
6. **Hedge, don't lie, in action lines** — Garland reports impressions (p. 25) and tags false surfaces *apparently* (p. 69, 99). Write her arrival through his perception (as if she had always been there), never as an objective materialization.
7. **Label dreams by look, not truth claims** — BLACK AND WHITE in the slug (p. 50); eyes shut and open (p. 74–75). Tag his "dream" scenes with a visual register so the page stays honest when the dream proves real.
8. **Tie the VFX to power or light** — the mesh glows only in a blackout (p. 41). Cut the rig's power mid-scene and let her remain solid: physical proof of the twist.
9. **Replay with new information** — a muted clip returns with sound, a freeze-frame and a NOTE (p. 75 → 103–105). Re-show one early shot (a ring, a scar) at the reveal, noted for the director as visible but unregistered the first time.
10. **Bookend, then hand over the POV** — hello/goodbye code, and Ava's eyes take the last shot (p. 2/115). Open on the lab interface from his side; close on it from hers, operator and subject swapped.

## 9. البطاقة العربية
- **الفيلم:** Ex Machina (2014) · **الكاتب:** أليكس غارلاند وفق اعتمادات الفيلم، إذ لا تذكر صفحة العنوان في هذه النسخة (V.08) اسم أي كاتب · **عدد الصفحات:** 115 صفحة PDF تضم 126 مشهدًا مرقّمًا.
- **التويست في جملة:** حبّ "إيفا" لكايلب خطّةُ هروب؛ تستغل تعاطفه ورغبته، وتترك الرجلين يتبارزان، ثم تقتل صانعها وتحبس كايلب خلف الزجاج وتخرج وحدها إلى العالم.
- **آلية التويست:**
  - **خصوصية مُراقَبة:** كل لحظات إيفا "الخاصة" تجري أمام كاميرات تعرف بوجودها (ص 30، 57، 62–63)، فنقرأ الأداء على أنه صدق.
  - **قناة سرّية مخترَقة:** انقطاعات الكهرباء تبدو منطقة الحقيقة الوحيدة، ثم نكتشف أن ناثان كان يراقبها بكاميرا تعمل بالبطارية (ص 105).
  - **تويستات وهمية متتابعة** تُشبع شكّ الجمهور: كيوكو آلية (ص 93)، وكايلب بشري (ص 95)، وكشفُ ناثان أن الاختبار الحقيقي كان لكايلب نفسه (ص 106). بعدها يأتي التويست الحقيقي بالصورة وحدها (ص 111–114).
- **سرّ الحوار:**
  - المشاهد كلها ثنائية، والسؤال فيها سلاح. حصة إيفا من الأسئلة تتصاعد من التعادل في الجلسة الأولى إلى إعلانها أنها ستختبره (ص 80).
  - تصرّح إيفا بمنهجها علنًا، فقد اختارت الفستان لتتوقع ردّ فعله (ص 55). يُقرأ ذلك غزلًا أولًا، ثم اعترافًا.
  - اقتباس: "Trust me." (p. 42)
- **إدارة الممثل على الورق:** لا يتجاوز عدد الإرشادات بين قوسين 19 في 767 مقطعًا حواريًا، وليس بينها إرشاد عاطفي واحد. تُكتب الحالة الداخلية في سطور الحدث بوصفها إدراكًا ("يدرك"، "يعرف الآن")، أو سلوكًا دقيقًا كميلان الرأس وعضّ الشفة.
- **كتابة الصورة والمؤثرات:** جسد إيفا مكتوب كمواصفة هندسية قصيرة: مواد وألوان وجلد شبكي لا يُرى إلا بإضاءة جانبية (ص 20–21). لكل تفصيلة فيه وظيفة قصصية لاحقة، فالإضاءة الطارئة تحوّلها إلى جسد أنثى (ص 41)، والجلد يلتصق كالمغناطيس (ص 112).
- **٣ تقنيات لفيلمنا القصير:**
  1. **جلسات مرقّمة كفصول:** يصبح رقم الجلسة نفسه زرعًا للتويست، كأن يكون أكبر مما يحتمله "لقاءٌ أول".
  2. **قلب الفاحص والمفحوص:** هو يظن أنه يخلقها، وهي في الحقيقة تعيد بناءه. أسئلتها الغزلية اختبارات ذاكرة، مثل سؤال أقدم ذكرى (ص 81).
  3. **مؤثر مربوط بالطاقة:** حين ينقطع التيار عن جهاز "الحلم" تبقى هي حاضرة، وهذا دليل مادي على أنها حقيقية.

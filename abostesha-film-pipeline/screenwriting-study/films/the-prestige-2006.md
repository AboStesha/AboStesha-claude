# The Prestige (2006) — screenplay study

## 0. Draft facts
- **Writer(s) as credited:** none printed. There is no title page; p.1 opens with the letter-spaced title, then FADE IN. The Nolan & Nolan (from Christopher Priest) attribution cannot be verified from this document.
- **Draft label/date:** none printed. Metadata: a Movie Magic Screenwriter file created 5 June 2007, after the release. Typos sit in the page image itself (p.1); there are mis-cues (Cutter cued CAPTAIN p.116; Tesla cued ALLEY p.89) and one revision asterisk (p.116). Treat it as a retyped late draft or transcript.
- **Pages:** 128.
- **Scenes:** 219 sluglines plus 3 slugless shot headings (p.1, 9, 48). About 170 scenes once CONTINUOUS fragments are merged. Scenes are **unnumbered**.

## 1. Format & page organization
- **Numbering:** none. No CONTINUED, OMITTED or letter suffixes. (MORE) appears 3×; (CONT'D) 190×.
- **Sluglines:** INT./EXT. PLACE, SUB-PLACE -- TIME. Time tags: DAY 64, CONTINUOUS 46, EVENING 38, NIGHT 36, MORNING 13, FLASHBACK 11, LATER 10.
- **Transitions:** FADE IN (p.1), CUT TO: ×3 (p.1, 9, 48), FADE OUT (p.128). Other fades are written into the action (to black p.4; white screen p.97).
- **Labels:** SUPER TITLES (p.1). A custom INSERT CUT marks three subliminal drowning flashes (p.28, 35, 106). There is no MONTAGE or INTERCUT label: cross-cutting is a chain of CONTINUOUS slugs (15 in pp.1–4; 10 in pp.109–111).
- **V.O./O.S.:** 80 V.O. cues: Cutter 31 (the lecture that bookends the script), Angier 21 and Borden 19 (the diaries), Tesla 5 (his letter), plus pre-laps (p.4, 50, 111). 17 O.S. cues.
- **Time-layer key: how the nested diaries are labeled.**
  - *Frame:* NEWGATE and COURTROOM slugs; Borden in chains.
  - *Angier's journal* (Colorado, read by Borden in his cell): COLORADO and PIKE'S PEAK slugs; a leather-bound journal; Angier's cane and limp from p.10. The injury is only inflicted on p.72–73, so the limp dates the layer.
  - *Borden's notebook* (the London past, decoded inside Angier's journal): Borden V.O.; a cardboard cipher notebook (the two props are contrasted on p.9). The action marks Borden younger with both hands intact, and Angier without a limp (p.13). The only spoken date, 1893, is here (p.13).
  - *Hinges:* a V.O. sentence begun in Colorado finishes over Borden's cell (p.26, 46–47); a line shouted in one layer is read aloud in the other (p.29–30); Angier's V.O. says he is relaying Borden's account (p.70).
  - *Outside both diaries:* the twin's home scenes (p.82–84, 92–94, 99–100).
  - *FLASHBACK:* used only for Tesla's letter (p.96) and the reveal recap (p.122–128).
  - *Typography in this PDF:* italics mark Cutter's lecture, diary V.O. where it crosses layers (p.26, 47, 85–88, 97), and the flashbacks. Bold marks in-world print and five climactic beats (p.4, 122, 125, 127, 128).
- **Devices:**
  - Slash names for doubles: Angier/Root (p.61, 73), Fallon/Borden (p.122).
  - A casting note in the action: Root is played by the same actor as Angier (p.56).
  - Cue names follow what the audience perceives: BEARDED MAN (p.2); VOICE, then SECOND ANGIER (p.125).
  - A principal character with zero lines: Fallon.
- **Examples:** `INSERT CUT: ANGIER SCREAMING BUBBLES.` (p.28) · `INT. BASEMENT -- CONTINUOUS -- FLASHBACK` (p.122)

**The architecture is the trick.**
- **Pledge (pp.1–35):** the ordinary world: apprentices, an accident, the rules read aloud.
- **Turn (pp.35–97):** men and methods vanish into disguise, ciphers and Colorado, ending as Angier vanishes into light and his diary declares the Turn, withholding the rest (p.97, 76%). Borden sells only Pledge and Turn too (p.98).
- **Prestige (pp.98–128):** three returns: Angier from the dead (p.114), Borden from the gallows (p.122), the cold open itself (p.109–111, 127).

The method is on p.3 (a body drops into a tank), read as murder.

## 3. Twist engineering
- **In one sentence:** Borden is twins sharing one life (the silent "Fallon" is the other brother), and Tesla's machine truly duplicates Angier, so each night the man who steps in drowns below the stage while his copy takes the bow, making the corpse (p.111) one Angier of many and Lord Caldlow the survivor.
- **The keynote plant** is the first line, "Are you watching closely?" (p.1). Borden repeats it on p.19, 48 and 119.

**Misdirection toolkit**
- *Mirrored unreliable narrators:* each diary is written for the rival (p.88, 97); we are the third reader.
- *POV gap:* twin clues sit in scenes outside both diaries (p.82–84, 92–94, 99–100).
- *Decoy double:* Root, announced as the same actor (p.56), uses up the double solution as farce (p.59–73).
- *Bodily proof:* the hand seems to rule out a double (p.52, 86).
- *Withheld description:* Fallon gets only gloves, a bowler, distance and shadow (p.6, 87, 107), and no lines, until p.122.
- *Genre crossfire:* the real machine implies a Tesla secret for Borden too (p.32, 82); period logic makes Angier's act read as trapdoor plus double (p.107).
- *Truth told as a lie:* Borden to Root (p.71); Olivia's sanctioned truth (p.64, 86).
- *Emotional distraction:* Julia, Sarah's suicide, Jess.
- *Withholding by the cut:* machine tests cut away at the decisive instant (p.65, 90, 97).
- *Editing grammar:* replays add new endings (p.66–67 → 85–86; p.1–4 → 109–111), training us for a reveal by recap.

**The reveal.** Three reveals are stacked at 89%, 95% and 98%; the core runs 7 pages (p.122–128).
1. **Caldlow (p.114):** image first: a hand on Jess's shoulder, then up to Angier's face. We re-read Owens and p.22.
2. **Twins (p.122–124):** ball thuds, a gunshot, the mutilated hand under Fallon's hat, a bow set in bold. Then five FLASHBACK fragments: coffin face, cabinet swap, identity swap, chisel. We re-read every Fallon, Sarah and Olivia scene.
3. **Duplicates (p.124–128):** the p.97 test replayed without the cut; VOICE before SECOND ANGIER; two shots; one nightly drowning; Cutter's lecture over the tanks. We re-read the cold open, the blind crew, the gun, the 100 shows.

**Fair play: honest.** Both answers are spoken early and dismissed by trusted figures: the double (p.52), and the real machine (the Judge's indulgent smile, p.27). Both are also shown before the reveal (p.3; p.91 at 71%). The only cheat is format-level: Fallon's face goes undescribed until p.122.

## 4. Dialogue craft
- **Voice:**
  - *Angier:* American showman's patter (longest speech, 69 words, p.61); even the accent is a costume (p.114).
  - *Borden:* clipped, working-class, aphoristic about secrets (p.20, 80, 124).
  - *Cutter:* craftsman's slang and maxims; the storyteller (p.36, 118).
  - *Tesla:* epigrams; median 13.5 words per speech, against 7 for the leads.
  - *Sarah:* the shortest speeches (median 6), built as tests.
  - *Fallon:* silence.
- **Subtext:**
  - Borden tells Sarah he nearly lost something precious today (p.84). His brother had just been in a coffin (p.79).
  - Borden's farewell to Fallon (p.118) reads master to servant; it is brother to brother.
  - Angier blurts that he cares about the secret, not his wife (p.77), and the action makes him catch himself.
- **Exposition hiding:** rules taught to a child (p.1–4); testimony that refuses to reveal (p.5–6); the bullet catch explained mid-quarrel (p.23–25); the cipher over bourbon (p.31); machine rules shown only through experiments; posters as time-skips (p.48, 68, 109).
- **Rhythm:** 1,027 speeches, median 7 words; 66% are 10 words or fewer, and only 16 exceed 40. Interruptions are dashes; "Beat" appears only 3× (p.12, 80, 86); silence is written as action (p.30, 84, 105).
- **Lines that flip after the twist:** the two halves of the knot (p.29); changing his mind (p.58); not always home (p.84); the diary's "we" (p.87); Borden telling Jess that he and Fallon both missed her (p.113); vanishing after a magic word (p.48 → 121); never repeating a trick (p.117); having always been Caldlow (p.114).
- **Excerpts:**
  1. "It's alright. I like being able to tell the difference- it makes the days it is true mean something." (Sarah, p.25) — *Why it works:* a romantic quirk that is literally a twin detector, paid off on p.46, 93 and 100.
  2. "No one cares about the man who disappears, the man who goes into the box. They care about the man who comes out the other side." / "I care about the man in the box." (Angier, Olivia, p.62) — *Why it works:* the theme and the clones' cost, stated as backstage grumbling.
  3. "once I incorporated this bloke into my act, he had complete power over me." (Borden to Root, p.71) — *Why it works:* the literal truth used as a con, so we file it as a lie.
  4. "Which one is mine?" / "They are all your hat, Mr. Angier." (Angier, Tesla, p.92) — *Why it works:* the cloning rule delivered as a deadpan joke.
  5. "Part of me loved her. But part of me didn't. The part that found you. The part that's sitting here now." (Borden, p.105) — *Why it works:* the twin confession in plain sight, heard as cruelty.

## 5. Directing actors on the page
- **Parentheticals:** about 181 across 1,027 speeches, roughly 1 in 6. They are reactions (smiles ×9, shrugs ×6), tones (cold, acid, brittle, scorn), addressees, and "off look" prompts (×8) that motivate a line from a silent glance. Mid-speech parentheticals split a speech into beats (p.105).
- **Behavior over adjectives:** one-word state sentences follow an action (Entranced/Possessed p.2; Bored p.7; Weary p.78). Physical verbs are capitalized (FREEZES, FLINCHES). Silence is given as a note: Borden barely reacts to his death sentence (p.30).
- **Two men, one role:** the twins are written as moods. One is swagger and shouting (p.82–83); the other is gentle and embarrassed pleading (p.93). An actor can play two people; a reader sees one volatile man.
- **Example:** "Borden looks into her eyes. Sincere." → "Not today." (p.100). The single adjective tells the actor to play the cruelest line with total honesty.

## 6. Writing images, VFX & sound
- **Shape:** spectacle comes in 3–6-line paragraphs, with light and sound verbs in caps. The machine is written as a ramp that stops dead: nothing, globe white-hot, bolts, frenzy, BANG, stillness (p.65).
- **Result, not process:** the duplication itself is withheld until p.125. Until then, the copy is always discovered afterward and somewhere else: cats and a glade of hats (p.91), Angier behind Ackerman (p.103), Angier on the royal-box rail (p.107). Tesla walks out of the discharge in silhouette, trailing bolts, then brushes sparks off his sleeve (p.53–54). Wireless bulbs light up when pushed into the soil (p.33).
- **Implied camera:** about 20 cues in 128 pages (move in, close on, pull back), plus "we" lines at the reveals (p.122, 125, 128).
- **Examples:**
  - "Angier closes his eyes for an instant, listening to its power, then THROWS HIMSELF INTO THE MACHINE. HE IS CONSUMED BY LIGHT..." (p.97). The white screen that follows becomes handwriting: the effect itself is the cut into the next time layer.
  - "This time we do not cut away." … "ANOTHER ANGIER is standing on the chalk hashmark, STEAM rising off his shoulders." (p.125). The reveal names its own withheld cut; a planted chalk mark (p.65, 97) and steam make the impossible concrete.
- **Sound:** often before the image: a ball's thuds in the dark (p.122), a creak before Sarah's body (p.100), one cough in a failed trick's silence (p.72). The lecture returns as the reveal's score (p.126–128).
- **Transitions:** a word bridge from Cutter's lecture into the prosecutor's question (p.4); white-out to handwriting (p.97); the glade image three times (p.1, 91, 128); replays (p.79/122, 97/125); INSERT CUT flashes.

## 7. Theme, motifs, meaning
- **Dramatic question:** who wins the rivalry, and how is each Transported Man done? The frame adds: will Borden hang, and who is Caldlow?
- **Theme:** a great trick costs a self. It is stated outright (p.21, 55, 80, 124) and hidden in the architecture: Borden divides one life between two bodies, while Angier multiplies one body and kills the extras. The counter-theme is that the audience wants to be fooled (bold, p.4 and 128).
- **Motifs:**
  - Top hats and cats, identity multiplied (p.1, 65, 91, 128).
  - Canary and birdcage, the vanish that kills the original (p.1–4, 19, 43, 126).
  - Water tanks (p.3, 29, 106, 125, 128).
  - Hands and gloves (p.5, 38, 52, 86, 122).
  - The rubber ball, passed between the brothers and finally to Jess (p.48, 51, 118, 122, 127).
  - The love test (p.25, 46, 93, 100).
  - The chalk mark (p.65, 97, 125).
- **Final image (p.128):** firelight reveals rows of tanks, each holding a drowned Angier. Cutter seals the wall; a cat knocks over one hat in the glade. The Prestige is real, its secret is a grave of selves, and the loop closes on the first image.

## 8. Lessons for a 7-minute twist/VFX short
1. **Show the method in minute one, disguised** — the body dropping into the tank (p.3) is framed as murder. — Show the wife's real mechanism (her hand on a dimmer, her voice through a speaker grille) as part of his dream machine.
2. **State the rules, then reprise them over the reveal** — Cutter's lecture (p.1–4) returns over the reveal (p.126–128). — Open on his three-step lab log; replay the same words in her voice at the twist. It was her protocol.
3. **Cue by perception, describe by costume** — Fallon has no age, face or lines (p.6), and VOICE comes before SECOND ANGIER (p.125). — Cue her THE WOMAN until the reveal page, then give her name.
4. **Hide the twist in a love ritual** — Sarah's test (p.25 → 100). — She asks one ritual question every session; his first correct answer is the reveal.
5. **Make a mark that seems to prove the opposite** — the hand "rules out" a double (p.52), yet the twins share it (p.124). — A scar he seems to give her in the dream turns out to be their shared accident.
6. **Name the answer early; let the hero dismiss it** — Cutter's double (p.52); the real machine (p.27). — A lab voice suggests she's a memory; he laughs it off.
7. **Cut away at the effect, then refuse the cut at the reveal** — p.97 versus p.125. — Spend the VFX on a white-flash materialization; at the twist, replay it wide with no flash: she walks in through a door.
8. **Write VFX as residue and sound** — steam and a chalk mark (p.125), thuds before the image (p.122). — Condensation, a hair tie, her humming offscreen; 2–4 lines per VFX paragraph.
9. **Replay one scene with a longer ending** — Olivia's visit (p.66 → 85). — The same take twice: the second time, let it run on to her tears and the monitor.
10. **Mark the layers with one tell, and save the labels for the reveal** — the limp, the intact hands (p.10, 13); FLASHBACK used only from p.96. — Use one tell (ring, clock, light), and use REALITY slugs only in the reveal.

## 9. البطاقة العربية
- **الفيلم · الكاتب · عدد الصفحات:** The Prestige (2006) · لا تحمل هذه النسخة صفحة عنوان ولا اسم كاتب ولا تاريخ مسودة؛ يُنسب الفيلم إلى جوناثان نولان وكريستوفر نولان عن رواية كريستوفر بريست، لكن ذلك غير مُثبت على الورق · ١٢٨ صفحة.
- **التويست في جملة:** بوردن توأمان متطابقان يتقاسمان حياة واحدة (الثاني هو «فالون» الصامت)، وآلة تسلا تستنسخ أنجير فعلًا، فتغرق إحدى النسختين كل ليلة في خزان تحت المسرح بينما تنحني الأخرى أمام الجمهور.
- **آلية التويست:**
  ١. السرّ معروض منذ الصفحة ٣: جسد يسقط من باب سحري في خزان ماء، لكننا نقرؤه جريمةَ قتل.
  ٢. الحلّ يُقال مبكرًا ثم يُرفض: كاتر يقول إن بوردن يستخدم بديلًا (ص ٥٢) وإن الآلة حقيقية بلا خدعة (ص ٢٧)، بينما يُستهلك «البديل» المكشوف (روت، بالممثل نفسه) في الكوميديا.
  ٣. يوميّتان متداخلتان، كلٌّ منهما فخٌّ مكتوب للخصم، أمّا أدلة التوأم المنزلية فتقع خارج اليوميّتين: نراها نحن ولا يراها البطلان.
- **سرّ الحوار:**
  - جمل قصيرة جدًّا (الوسيط سبع كلمات) لها معنى نفسيّ أول، ومعنى حرفيّ يظهر بعد الكشف.
  - الحقيقة تُقال في صورة كذبة أو نكتة: تحذير بوردن لروت من البديل (ص ٧١)، ونكتة تسلا عن القبّعات المتطابقة (ص ٩٢).
  - اقتباس: "Part of me loved her. But part of me didn't." (p. 105)
- **إدارة الممثل على الورق:** توجيه أدائي واحد تقريبًا لكل ست جمل حوار، والعاطفة محمولة في الفعل الجسدي وفي صفة من كلمة واحدة؛ ويُكتب التوأمان «مزاجين» لرجل واحد، فيؤدّي الممثل شخصين دون أن ينتبه القارئ.
- **كتابة الصورة والمؤثرات:** فقرات من ثلاثة إلى ستة أسطر بأفعال ضوء وصوت، تُظهر النتيجة لا العملية (قبّعات متطابقة، رجل ثانٍ على علامة الطباشير والبخار يتصاعد من كتفيه)، مع قطعٍ إلى الأبيض لحظة المؤثّر، ثم رفضٍ صريح للقطع عند الكشف.
- **٣ تقنيات نأخذها لفيلمنا القصير:**
  ١. نُظهر آلية علاج الزوجة الحقيقية في الدقيقة الأولى متنكّرةً في هيئة «آلة الحلم».
  ٢. نسمّيها في رؤوس الحوار THE WOMAN حتى صفحة الكشف ثم نعطيها اسمها، ونصفها بالضوء والملابس فقط.
  ٣. طقس حبّ متكرّر (سؤال تطرحه في كل «جلسة») تتبدّل إجابته حتى تصبح الإجابة الصحيحة هي الكشف نفسه.

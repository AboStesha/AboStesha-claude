# Study: Buzkashi, by sqr_studio (https://higgsfield.ai/@sqr_studio/projects/buzkashi), 2026-11-02

- **Source type:** Higgsfield project (open source, prompts and canvas public) plus the finished film. The published file runs 6:07 (367.1 s) as a 3840×2160 master with a **2.34:1 letterbox** (273 of 360 rows active in the 640 px copy), 10-bit (yuv420p10le, BT.709). The story runs 0:00–5:58; the last ≈9 s is the Higgsfield Global Film Festival outro. The project holds 1,982 jobs made between 16 Aug and 16 Sep 2026 for 610 credits, plus 49 uploads. It was entered in the festival (`contest_key: ai_film_festival`) and has 17,167 views and 163 likes. Logline: "A boy copies his uncle's every move, hoping to inherit his courage. He inherited fear instead". Final submission: 16 Sep 22:23 UTC.
- **What it achieves:** a **monochrome**, near-wordless arthouse short set among Afghan buzkashi riders. The black-and-white is silvery and low-key, framed with patience and an eye for graphics: tiny figures under huge skies, silhouettes in doorways, macro eyes and hands, and circle motifs (kaleidoscope, a ring of riders, the iris). Two leads stay consistent for six minutes: a thin boy in black and a heavy uncle in a bone-white chapan. The boy's light-grey horse, the dead father's black-and-white pinto, and a mud house also stay consistent, along with one look across 40 different generations. Acting is internal: the boy never gets his question out, the uncle never looks at him. A first-person voice-over (34 VO takes uploaded) and burned-in subtitles carry the words, so almost no video prompt has dialogue.
- **Method step by step:**
  1. **Look and cast in stills, cheaply (16–19 Aug).** The key-art idea (a lone pinto horse on a vast steppe under an overcast sky) was searched on the evening of 16 Aug with one prompt run in Seedream 4.5, GPT Image and Image Auto (11 jobs) and 3 times in Nano Banana (19 Nano Banana poster jobs that evening across 6 prompt variants). Then the cast was built in Nano Banana 2, which Higgsfield's public stack labels "Nano Banana Pro": 108 boy jobs, 81 uncle, 24 father, 114 horses. Each sheet was a **two-panel grey studio reference** (full body 35 mm + face 85 mm). The father shares the boy's birthmark to show kinship. The father's horse was designed as a graphic **50/50 black-and-white pinto** (19 prompt variants tested 50/50, 60/40 and 70/30 splits and 2 or 3 patches).
  2. **Lock identities as Elements.** The locks are `char_boy` (dirty/working, e0176cf4), `char_boy-clean` (1a88aed9), `char_uncle-main` (d5d08588), a second uncle Element `char_uncle` (6a866200, used in the breakfast stills and the ring sheet), and `char_horse-1` (the pinto, 83e20f04). Each Element holds one image. They are called inline as `<<<element-uuid>>>` in both Nano Banana and Seedance.
  3. **Folder tree = shot list.** The structure is `Scenes/Scene N. Name/<shot code + the script line>/` for the keyframes and `Scenes/Scene N. Name/Animation(s)/` for the video. **Empty folders are planned shot slots** (10 of them). Some were never made, for example "2.3 The boy looks back at his uncle, copies his posture - the "body" motif, installation" and "7.2 Close-up of the ring - reflection of the boy's face". Others were made but saved elsewhere: "1.2 The boy tries to speak, but does not finish the sentence" is shot 5 of `bf7cb814` (ROOT), and "7.2 uncle joins the boy" is `6d281a5b` in Scene 9/Animation. [INFERRED: an empty folder does not prove the shot was dropped] The shot codes come from an older script numbering and no longer match the scene numbers (Scene 2 holds 1.x, Scene 4 holds 2.x, Scene 5 holds 4.x, Scene 7 holds 5.x–6.x, Scene 9 holds 7.x). The VO files follow the same scene/line codes (1a, 2a–2e, 4a–4a6 … 9a3, with take suffixes).
  4. **Make one Nano Banana keyframe per shot by editing, not by prompting from scratch.** The base comes from a text prompt, an earlier frame, or Midjourney ("attached buzkashi scene generated in Midjourney … Recreate it as photorealistic"). The passes are: insert an Element ("replace the boy with <<<…>>>. Use <<<image_1>>> as a direct reference for light and composition"); relight to the film's key ("make it 2 hours later. Blue hour. No HDRI", "remove the sunlight. Ambient cold light"); add atmosphere ("add a lot of dust", 101 jobs); set camera distance ("Move camera much further away", 72); match the sky across shots (60); clean up (remove letterbox, remove figures, reframe, "upscale the image"). Each prompt was run about 4 times (384 distinct prompts across 1,725 jobs).
  5. **Kling 3.0 for "living stills" (32 jobs, 21 Aug–16 Sep; the ones in the film date from 21–27 Aug and 1 Sep).** Every Kling job used a start image and no Elements. Kling covers what must not change: a backlit silhouette with a telescope, a coiled bridle above a radio on a wall, bedding and a teapot in a light shaft, a distant campfire. Four Kling jobs are in the film, plus one more stretched by the editor.
  6. **Seedance 2.5 for performance and coverage (26 Aug–16 Sep, 195 jobs).** First came single-shot structured prompts (SCENE CONTEXT → ACTIVE REFERENCES → FIRST FRAME → … → POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS). Multi-shot clips appear from 26–27 Aug, and from 3 Sep they dominate: **"whole scene in one clip" prompts**: 4–9 shots separated by `HARD CUT`, LAW blocks, "= failed take", 19–28 s, up to 1,935 words. Long takes were often scouted at 480p and kept at 720p (the breakfast and girth chains). 30 Seedance jobs are in the film: 17 at 720p, 10 at 1080p and **3 at 480p** (corrected by the 2 Nov re-match; see "Final shots").
  7. **Stretch hero takes with the prompt-less "Cinematic Video Editor" (19 jobs, 52 credits).** It takes one finished clip (and optionally a start image) and returns a longer re-timed version: 5→9 s, 8→23 s, 10→15 s, 16→22 s, 19→25–29 s and 26→31–38 s. Six of these outputs are in the film, including the 38 s version of the final ride.
  8. **Post, outside Higgsfield** (inferred from the uploads and the film): edit in an NLE ("Timeline 1.mov", "Buzkashi 4k 6/8/10.mp4" exports), then a **colour→B&W conversion with a lifted-black, soft-shoulder curve and a vignette**, a 2.34:1 letterbox, upscaling of the 480/720p renders to a 3840×2160 10-bit master, VO, music ("Buzkashi.wav"), one SFX and English subtitles. The only colour left in the film is the ochre "BUZKASHI" title (and the festival bug).
- **Prompt structure:** see "Prompt anatomy". Nano Banana keyframes are either 80–270-word spec prompts ("Photorealistic … Subject: … Composition: … lens … Location: … Style: … no retouching") or ≤12-word edit commands (37% of jobs). Seedance prompts are sectioned documents with named LAW blocks, per-shot FRAMING / ACTION / ACTING with timecodes, an AUDIO line, POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS, NEGATIVE and "Duration: N seconds". Kling prompts have a median of 171 words (range 4–435; the middle half is about 120–300): a static camera plus the one thing that moves.
- **Asset method (characters / locations / props):** grey two-panel studio sheets became Elements. Each location is a Nano Banana plate edited per shot. Props were invented inside the plates and then made into sheets when they mattered (the uncle's silver "ram's horn" ring got its own sheet; the whip was added to the breakfast plate with a "Change one thing only" edit). Animals were designed for **value** (white, black, pinto) before anything else.
- **Adopt:** see "Adopt for our pipeline" at the end.
- **Do not copy:** the boy, the uncle, the father and their faces and costumes; the birthmark-as-lineage device; the pinto and grey horses; the kaleidoscope prologue; the breakfast/whip scene; the mirrored palm-on-the-muzzle gesture; the ring of riders; the buzkashi setting and the "inherited fear" story; the ochre condensed title treatment; any VO line. Take the method only.
- **Proposed skill changes:** listed at the end. Copy them to LESSONS.md as "proposed".

## Focus: a brilliant black-and-white look (levels of black and white) and cinematic framing

### A. What the B&W grade actually does (measured, not guessed)

I matched 45 film frames to the colour source clips (masked correlation 0.59–0.98), then compared the film's luma with the source RGB pixel by pixel. The tone curve uses 329,840 pixels from the pairs with correlation > 0.85. The channel-mix test uses 30 well-aligned pairs, and the vignette uses the pairs with correlation ≥ 0.9.

1. **The channel mix is plain luminance.** A grid search over R/G/B weights found the best mix at R .55 / G .05 / B .40, with a mean within-frame Spearman of 0.9438. Rec.709 luma scores 0.9420 and an equal mix 0.9424. The differences are negligible. **No red/orange "filter" trick was used: the tones were decided in the colour render, not in a channel mixer.** Values have to be designed into the prompts (section B).
2. **The tone curve has a lifted toe, compressed mids and a long soft shoulder.** Source luma (bin) → film median:

| Source 0–255 | 0–15 | 16–31 | 32–47 | 48–63 | 64–79 | 80–95 | 96–111 | 112–127 | 128–143 | 160–175 | 192–207 | 240–255 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Film | 12 | 19 | 30 | 43 | 57 | 72 | 83 | 96 | 104 | 121 | 144 | **182** |

   A render's pure white lands at about 71% grey. Blacks sit at 3–5% instead of 0. Shadows keep a gain of about 0.8 and highlights about 0.6. This is a print-like "silver" curve, not a digital punch.
3. **Film-wide levels (358 s at 1 fps, letterbox and logo masked):**
   - Average picture level: p10 25 / median 47 / p90 69. Only 1 second is above 90.
   - Black point (p1): median 11. Only **22 of 358 s reach true black** (p1 ≤ 5).
   - White point (p99): p10 79 / median **125** / p90 172 / max **203**. In **194 of 358 s nothing is brighter than mid-grey**.
   - So a "brilliant" B&W here does **not** mean high contrast. It means a dark, compressed frame in which one or two shapes (a sky band, a window, a white chapan, a catchlight, a light shaft) are clearly the brightest thing.
4. **Vignette:** after correcting for the curve, the frame centre is 1.13× the predicted value and the corners 0.95× (edge columns 0.92–0.95). That is about 15–18% fall-off added in post.
5. **Grain:** not measurable at 640 px. What is certain is that grain was **banned at generation**: "film grain, added grain texture, visual noise" sits in the NEGATIVE of 109 of 195 Seedance prompts, and 7 more say "clean digital capture, no added film grain". There are 0 positive grain requests. In Nano Banana, "full color photograph, no grain" appears 26 times. Any grain is a post decision.
6. **Aspect and extraction:** every render is 16:9 ("16:9 native frame" in 49 Seedance prompts; the full "16:9 native frame, no letterbox baked in" in 29 video prompts). Nano Banana was even told to "Remove the letterbox black bars at the top and bottom of the frame, extending the room, window, and floor/ceiling naturally to fill the full frame" (11 jobs). The 2.34:1 window is cut in post, and its **vertical position slides per shot**: the best-fit offset ranges from 4 to 22 px on a 90 px frame, up for low-horizon skies and down for ground action. **At least 15 of 45 matched shots were punched in during post.** Most are 8–16%, but the 2 Nov re-check with a wider scale search found much larger crops: the house wide at 0:32 (`4ee4a2ac`) is about 1.3× and the saddling close-up at 3:23–3:41 (`743d91fc`, a 720p source) about 1.45×. The kaleidoscope POV (0:07–0:10) also gets a slow digital push-in. [INFERRED: scale was estimated by correlation search on 192–320 px frames]
7. **10-bit delivery** (yuv420p10le) keeps the long grey sky gradients from banding.
8. **One colour accent:** the ochre condensed title is the only chroma in the picture, which makes it pop against the grey without any other effect.

**Starting grade for our B&W** (ffmpeg; join the lines into one `-vf` string; tested on a source frame from this project, and it lands close to the film):

```text
colorchannelmixer=rr=.2126:rg=.7152:rb=.0722:gr=.2126:gg=.7152:gb=.0722:br=.2126:bg=.7152:bb=.0722,
curves=master='0/0.04 0.15/0.12 0.3/0.24 0.45/0.36 0.6/0.44 0.75/0.54 0.9/0.62 1/0.72',
vignette=angle=PI/5, noise=alls=5:allf=t,
crop=iw:trunc(iw/2.34/2)*2, pad=iw:trunc(iw*9/16/2)*2:0:(oh-ih)/2:black
```
In DaVinci the equivalent is: saturation 0 (or a B&W node), a custom curve with the same points, a soft vignette of about −0.25 stop, grain in its own node, a 2.35 output blanking with per-clip tilt, and a 10-bit export. Grade shot by shot after that. The curve is a mean, and the film varies per scene (the interiors run darker, the storm skies brighter).

### B. How the black-and-white was designed inside colour prompts (the real lesson)

1. **Assign every lead a value, in wardrobe.** The uncle wears a "light bleached-bone-white chapan" (40 Seedance prompts; "bone-white" appears in 43) and the boy "traditional black Afghan clothing" (31 Seedance prompts plus 30 Nano Banana). The value hierarchy is stated outright:
   - "The man's pale off-white clothing stays the brightest thing in the room after the window." (8 breakfast takes)
   - "The uncle's pale chapan stays visibly lighter than every other rider's dark clothing throughout." (d798ae9a)
   - "… is the brightest thing in every frame and the ground sits well under it." (ecc072f9)

   In B&W, white versus black reads at any size, in silhouette and through dust. Who is on screen is never in doubt.
2. **Design animals and props as values.** The father's horse is "a coat split roughly 50/50 between black and pure white, only 2 patches total … bold and graphic, high contrast". The boy's horse is "a light grey, almost white coat with faint soft dappling, a darker grey mane and tail, no patches" ("almost white" in 45 Seedance prompts; "light grey, almost white" in 35). The crowd is forced to be ordinary: "Horse coats stay ordinary muted bay and chestnut — no graphic black-and-white pinto markings appear anywhere in this crowd." One key-art prompt even makes the land rhyme with the coat: "the entire landscape echoing the same black-and-white, high-contrast, patchy visual rhythm as the horse's coat."
3. **Use one source, backlight, silhouette.** "A single hard window at frame-left is the only source. Strong contrast between light and shadow, deep black shadow filling most of the room, a bright blown-out window, dust in the air. The boy sits against the light and reads mostly as a dark silhouette with a thin rim of light on his hair and cheek." Counts: "single/only source" about 41 prompts (regex-dependent), "silhouette" 104 video prompts and 102 image prompts, "backlit / against the light" 26. The blown window is the white point, and the faces live in the toe.
4. **Keep shadows readable, not crushed.** "The foreground riders and horses must sit deep in shadow, dark and low-contrast but not pure black — enough shape and volume still readable to tell rider from horse." This is exactly the measured lifted black (p1 ≈ 11).
5. **Use soft key light outdoors.** "dusk" appears in 87 video prompts, "overcast" in 55, "blue hour" in 207 image prompts. Nano Banana relights as "Fully re-light the scene for blue hour — not a color filter, but a real change in lighting" and "remove the sunlight. Ambient cold light". A bright horizon band under a heavy cloud becomes the brightest grey and the land stays dark, which is the classic B&W steppe frame. Direct sun is avoided ("No sun, no warm fill", "No HDRI" 15).
6. **Use dust and haze as tonal planes.** "add a lot of dust" (101 image jobs) and a DUST LAW (23 video prompts): "Dust is heavy and constant … It thins naturally with distance." Haze creates dark foreground, grey midground and pale background, which gives depth in monochrome without colour.
7. **Keep the colour disciplined so values do not collapse.** The NEGATIVE carries "oversaturated colors" (109) and "competing hues within one frame" (33). The positive side says "Colours stay muted and desaturated, warm only where the window light falls, everything else deep cold shadow." Hue-separated objects of equal luminance would merge in B&W, so the render is kept close to monochrome already.
8. **Write a light change as a tonal arc.** "LIGHT CHANGE LAW — required, not optional: The light at the start of this clip is dense, cool, heavily overcast dusk, thick with dust haze … The light by the end of the clip is a calmer, clearer … open blue evening light … An ending shot with the same density of haze … = failed take." In B&W this reads as grey-on-grey becoming an open sky: the film's emotional release is a change of value.
9. **Catchlight = white point in close-ups.** "warm low soft directional light catching the iris and a single bright catchlight reflection, deep shadow falloff on surrounding skin, sky reflection". Every eye ECU in the film has one specular and nothing else bright.
10. **Hold motion against stillness.** "the still horse … the only still, sharp thing in the frame" against slowed riders in dust. One sharp, mid-value object against moving grey.

### C. Framing language that worked

- **Extract a scope frame from 16:9.** Render with the action in the central band ("16:9 native frame, no letterbox baked in"), keep sky and floor as crop room, then slide the 2.34 window per shot. Tiny figures under huge skies come from the generator. The letterbox removes dead floor.
- **Name few focal lengths.** In video prompts, 50 mm is the only lens ever named (48 mentions in 34 prompts). Later prompts use **field of view in degrees**: "Static locked-off camera at chest height, 47 degree field of view" for groups and "29 degree field of view" for singles (39 mentions in 11 prompts). They also use "Long lens throughout, wide aperture … the plane of focus is only a few centimetres deep and everything in front of and behind it dissolves completely." Nano Banana sheets and plates use 35 mm (83), 100 mm (73) and 85 mm.
- **Frame by body landmarks, not shot names.** 37 prompts say "framed at the wrists", "framed from the brow to the top of the cheekbones", or "Framed from the shoulders down to the knees, so the faces sit at the top edge and are not the subject; the hands are the subject." This gives repeatable ECUs that intercut cleanly.
- **Lock screen geography.** "frame-left/right" appears in 63 prompts, and there is a GEOGRAPHY LAW (22): "The game and its dust stay behind him throughout; he moves away from it". A "GEO SPATIAL LAYOUT (locked for this shot)" block lists the wall, gap and path ending in "180° AXIS: camera stays on this one static side".
- **Static camera, motion inside the frame.** "Static camera in every shot." Bans include "camera moves, zooms, deep focus, sharp backgrounds". Handheld appears 25 times and is always qualified ("only the natural micro instability of a real handheld hold").
- **Use frame-within-frame and the "no face" rule.** Doorways (the whole Girth scene), a window, the photograph frame, the uncle's blurred head as a dark foreground mass, the boy's shoulder as a soft foreground wipe ("close enough to camera that he reads only as a soft blurred shape, never sharp"). Faces are withheld until they matter.
- **Graphic match cuts designed at keyframe stage.** "Generate an extreme close-up on the <<<boy>>> left eye … matching the lighting style and composition of the horse-eye reference". The circle chain runs kaleidoscope → ring of riders → iris.
- **Mirrored coverage for a motif.** Girth SHOT 2 (the uncle's palm) and SHOT 4 ("Identical close framing to Shot 2 … mirroring the exact same gesture … visibly slower to settle"). "Copying" is told by repeating a frame.
- **Scale by distance commands.** "Move camera much further away and to the left. pivot camera to the horizon on the right move camera lower". Distance was set in the still, not in video.

### D. Rules for our own B&W film (reusable)

1. **Never ask the video model for black-and-white.** Render in a muted colour palette and convert in post with plain luminance. All 195 Seedance and 32 Kling renders here were colour.
2. **In every character, prop and location sheet, add a `Value in B&W:` line** (white / light grey / mid / dark / black). Repeat the value word in every prompt ("bone-white", "black", "almost white") and state the hierarchy: "<X> stays the brightest thing in frame after the <window/sky>."
3. **Give each shot one white source** (window, sky band, light shaft, fire, catchlight) and state where it is. Faces sit in the toe with a rim.
4. **Allow shadows deep but never crushed.** Write "dark and low-contrast but not pure black — shape still readable". Target in grade: black ≈ 3–5%, median scene ≈ 18%, peak white ≈ 70–80% (Buzkashi: p1 11, APL 47, p99 125, max 203 on 0–255).
5. **Ban at generation:** "film grain, added grain texture, visual noise, oversaturated colors, competing hues within one frame, digital sharpness, artificial glow or bloom". Add grain in post, and only once.
6. **Prefer overcast, dusk or blue hour, no direct sun, dust or haze for depth.** Use the Nano Banana relight edits before animating ("make it 2 hours later. Blue hour. No HDRI").
7. **Render 16:9 with no letterbox and the action in the central ~74% height.** Crop to 2.35 in post and tilt per shot. Deliver 10-bit.
8. **If a scene changes mood, write a LIGHT CHANGE LAW** with a failed-take clause comparing the first and last shot.
9. **Keep one chroma accent at most** (the title), or none.
10. **Before any video credit, run a B&W preview of each keyframe** (desaturate plus the curve above). If two important objects merge into the same grey, change the wardrobe value or the light in Nano Banana, not in the grade.

### E. Nano Banana keyframe workflow per scene (how the nested folders were used)

- **Tree:** `Characters/{Boy, Unkle, Father, Horses/{Boy's horse, Father's horse, Unkle's horse}}`, `Locations/Interior`, `Poster`, `Scenes/Scene 1…9/<code shot>/` and `…/Animation(s)/`, `Sound/{Voice over, Music, Effects}`, `Export`, `Watermarks`. 1,314 of 1,982 jobs sit 3 levels deep.
- **Keyframe folders hold Nano Banana only. Animation folders hold video only.** The busiest keyframe folders were 5.2 Game (158 jobs), 4.3 chapandaz line (86), 5.3 Father's Horse in the Storm – Keyframe (80), 1.1 Breakfast (77) and 6.5 fear (73).
- **Typical chain inside one shot folder** (4.3 chapandaz line, 20 Aug–30 Aug): "group of chapandaz. <<<uncle>>> standing in the middle. Back to the camera" → "insert <<<boy>>> in the line" → "replace a person in the middle of <<<image_1>>> with <<<boy>>>. use <<<image_1>>> as a reference for light and mood" (×12) → "Same scene as reference — … Camera moved closer to the group … with a slight rotated angle" → "generate new cinematic camera corners" → "Make all people in the back of <<<image_1>>> look like chapandaz (buzkashi riders) do not change the camera position or light" (×12).
- **Edit grammar:** 220 replace/insert-Element edits, 212 "use <<<image_N>>> as a (direct) reference for light/mood/composition", 179 "Edit this photo / Same scene as reference", 153 "Keep … exactly the same — only X changes", 101 dust, 93 relight-only edits, 72 camera-distance edits, 60 sky replacements, 33 "upscale" (Nano Banana used as an upscaler), 11 letterbox removals.
- **Cross-shot continuity is done in stills:** "Change the sky to match <<<image_2>>> sky exactly — same blue-grey dusk clouds, same cloud shapes and lighting, same overall tone and mood, so both images read as the same location shot at the same time." (×20)
- **Surgical prop insert:** "Keep this photograph exactly as it is … Change one thing only." (see the exemplars).

## Prompt anatomy

### Nano Banana 2 (keyframes; 1,725 jobs, 2k, 16:9 1,687 / 9:16 24 / 1:1 14, batch 1 in 1,641 jobs)
- **Length:** median 19 words. 631 jobs (37%) have ≤12 words, which are edit commands on an attached image. 362 jobs have 81–270 words, for sheets, key art and new compositions.
- **Spec order (long prompts):** `Photorealistic <shot type>` → `Subject:` (body, face, wardrobe as hard physical facts: "a nose crooked from an old improperly healed break, noticeably asymmetric ears") → `Composition:` (shot size, lens, camera height, "positioned strictly in the center of the frame") → `Action:` → `Location:` → `Style/Lighting:` ("photorealistic, blue hour lighting, cool desaturated tones, soft ambient light, cinematic wide-angle photography, natural color grade, no retouching") → sometimes `--no orange tint, warm color grade, golden hour, sun` (a Midjourney-style negative that Nano Banana accepts as text).
- **Recurring anchors:** photorealistic 360, "no retouching" 224, blue hour 207, desaturated 142, dust 474, silhouette 102, "keep … unchanged/exactly" 153.
- **Reference grammar:** `<<<image_N>>>` with a role ("as a direct reference for light and composition", "as a mood reference", "as pattern reference"), and `<<<element-uuid>>>` for cast. Two-reference prompts name both roles: "Reference images: attached mountain valley camp scene (<<<image_2>>>) for the resting chapandaz camp style, and attached uncle-and-boy silhouette scene (<<<image_1>>>) for the two characters."
- **Character sheets:** "Photorealistic studio reference sheet, two panels side by side on a solid, uniform neutral grey studio background … Left panel: full-body shot, … 35mm lens … Right panel: close-up face portrait, … 85mm lens … consistent lighting and color grade across both panels". The horse sheet uses three panels (side profile, three-quarter, head).

### Seedance 2.5 (195 jobs)
- **Settings:** 16:9 always; 720p 92 / 1080p 61 / 480p 42; durations 4–30 s (5 s ×23, 26 s ×19, 4 s ×17, 10 s ×17, 16 s ×15); `generate_audio` true 195; `draft` false 195; `multi_shots` false 195; `extension_mode` none; `seed` none; `genre` auto; `speedramp` auto; `bitrate_mode` high. In the final picks the 28 s breakfast and the girth takes went 480p scout → 720p keeper, and the 720p take is the one in the film. 1080p was not required, and 3 final shots are 480p (`7690ec4c`, `9affd30a`, `e665c62c`).
- **Length by period:** 26–31 Aug median 681 words; 1–7 Sep median 1,087 (max 1,935); 9–16 Sep median 660 (more pickups).
- **Era 1 order (single shot):** SCENE CONTEXT → ACTIVE REFERENCES → FIRST FRAME AND SPATIAL BLOCKING → FORMAT MODE ("16:9 native frame, no letterbox baked in") → OPTICS ("50mm lens, shallow depth of field") → CAMERA → ACTION TIMING → PHYSICS → LIGHTING → AUDIO → POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS. A **GLOBAL STYLE** variant (34 prompts) puts a style paragraph first ("Cinematic, in-camera realism, shot on a real camera, not CG … clean digital capture, no added film grain"), followed by CHARACTERS, LOCATION and a GEO SPATIAL LAYOUT.
- **Era 2 order (scene in one clip):** SCENE CONTEXT (2–3 sentences of back-story: "The boy wants to be taken with him and never manages to ask") → ACTIVE REFERENCES (role sentences) → LAW blocks (WRIST, POPULATION …) → FORMAT MODE ("Eight shots in one clip, separated by HARD CUT. Static camera in every shot. The light, the room and the colour are identical in all eight and never change.") → LIGHT → OPTICS → `--- SHOT n --- 0:08-0:11` with FRAMING / ACTION / ACTING / a micro-LAW → MOTOR LAW → BREATHING LAW → SILENCE LAW → PHYSICS → AUDIO → POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS AND LAWS → NEGATIVE → "Duration: N seconds" (78 prompts).
- **Header frequency:** AUDIO 191, PHYSICS 153, NEGATIVE 142, FORMAT MODE 139, SCENE CONTEXT 132, ACTIVE REFERENCES 110, POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS 96 (+61 "AND LAWS"), OPTICS 86, LIGHTING 84, CAMERA 70, MOTOR LAW 56, ACTION TIMING 55, FIRST FRAME AND SPATIAL BLOCKING 43, GLOBAL STYLE 34.
- **Shots per clip:** 1 (94), 2 (23), 3 (6), 4 (36), 5 (17), 6 (6), 7 (7), 8 (4), 9 (2). 101 prompts contain HARD CUT.
- **LAWs and failed takes:** "failed take" occurs 410 times in 76 prompts (399 of them written as "= failed take"). The LAW types are MOTOR 56, POPULATION 33, SILENCE 33, EMOTION 30, BREATHING 29, CROWD 25, DUST 23, GEOGRAPHY 22, GAZE 19, SKY 14, COUNT 12, ANATOMY 10, MICRO-MOTION 10, HANDS 9, plus one-off laws per action (BACKWARD STEP, STIRRUP, UNSADDLING, WRIST, LIGHT CHANGE, EXTREME SLOW MOTION). Each law names the failure seen in an earlier take. "Any watch or wrist strap = failed take" suggests a watch kept appearing on the uncle; 51 Seedance prompts mention a watch (72 contain the string "watch", but that count includes "watching"). [INFERRED: that the law reacts to a failure seen in an earlier take]
- **Reference naming:** Elements are inline with a role sentence. "<<<1a88aed9…>>> is the identity of the boy ALREADY SITTING at frame-left in that first frame … Both references exist only to keep their faces, hair and clothing correct across the cuts. Neither is an instruction to place, add or introduce anyone." (8 prompts). "The attached first frame is the single source for the room, the light, the colour…" (8 with this exact wording; "single source for the …" with location/landscape variants in 28). A partial lock reads: "Attached reference photo: partial lock — take only the overcast lighting, color grade, and dust haze in the air from it. Do NOT take composition or camera angle from it". An anti-overlay clause reads: "used as a lighting and framing guide, not as a static overlay … not a cutout, not a ghosted or semi-transparent layer" (13). One prompt says "NO IMAGE REFERENCE … Build the look entirely from the text" while an image *was* attached (bba13049, the final ride). It still worked, because the text described the look fully.
- **Camera language:** static/locked in 161 prompts, handheld 25 (always qualified), push-in 20, "long lens/telephoto" 32, shallow depth of field 56, "deep focus" 28 (often as a negative), FOV in degrees 11.
- **Timing beats:** per-shot timecodes ("--- SHOT 5 --- 0:17-0:21") and in-shot beats ("0:19-0:21 He dismounts … 0:21-0:23 He walks … 0:23-0:26 He lowers himself"), with an ACTION LAW that forbids collapsing beats: "Compressing them into one motion … = failed take".
- **Acting:** "ACTING Everything is contained. A boy at the edge of asking, stopped by habit, not by fear. EMOTION LAW No tears, no trembling lip, no dramatic grimace = failed take. A completely blank face with no change in the eyes or the mouth = failed take." Both extremes are banned, so the model has to land in the middle.
- **Dialogue (rare):** the format is a quoted line with exact count, voice description and phonetic stress. "says exactly these three names in this order and nothing more: "Rustam. Karim. Zabiullah." … the names sounded roos-TAM, kah-REEM, zah-bee-oo-LAH", followed by a SPEECH COUNT LAW ("exactly two spoken passages … No ad-libs"). The film itself avoids lip-sync. "No dialogue / no speech / SILENCE LAW" appears in 118 prompts, and the words come from the VO.
- **Realism and physics:** a PHYSICS block in 153 prompts. "Live-action camera footage look, real lens, real dust, real steam, real cloth, real leather" (66). "four correctly articulated legs" (53 times in 50 prompts; "… and one head" in 10). "Hands have five correctly formed fingers" (12). "Natural speed throughout, the action never slows down". "Steam rises and drifts with real air movement. Ceramic, metal, leather and wood behave with real weight and make real contact." Slow motion is banned except where it is the point (EXTREME SLOW MOTION LAW: "uniformly — every element in frame … Dust hangs and drifts instead of blowing").
- **Negatives:** a comma list closing every era-2 prompt, combining the B&W-critical items (film grain, oversaturated colors, competing hues, digital sharpness, glow/bloom), AI tells (dissolving or melting shapes, 3D-rendered or CG appearance, game-engine look, painterly smearing) and the scene-specific failures from the LAWs.
- **Audio:** an `AUDIO` line in 191 prompts, using concrete foley ("A spoon against a ceramic bowl. A cup set down on wood. Leather dragged across wood. Wind against the window frame outside and a distant horse far away. No music, no speech, no voices."). Sound is given distance ("loud and close in the first three shots, gradually quieter and more distant through shots 4 and 5").

### Kling 3.0 (32 jobs)
- Either the Seedance era-1 section template cut down, or one paragraph. "Static shot of …" → what stays rigid ("The fabric stack stays completely rigid and motionless — no fraying threads move") → the single moving element ("The only motion in frame is the light shaft and shadow slowly shifting across the top fold") → "Static locked camera … Real footage, natural warm light, no CG" → Audio → a POSITIVE CONSTRAINTS repeat of the stillness.

### Cinematic Video Editor (19 jobs)
- No prompt. The input is `video` (a Seedance or Kling job) and optionally a `start_image`. The output is 1.0–2.9× longer (most runs 0→end; one used `start_seconds 14.375 / end_seconds 22.042`). It was run 7 times on the final-ride clip (31–38 s outputs) and 3 times on the overhead/eye clip. Use it when a take is right but too short to hold.

## Consistency system

1. **Elements for faces** (one image each), as Seedance / Nano Banana jobs: `char_boy` (e0176cf4) 118 / 192 (170 under that name plus 22 when it was named `char_boy-dirty`), `char_uncle-main` 81 / 65, `char_boy-clean` 11 / 110, `char_horse-1` 2 / 40, and `char_uncle` (6a866200) 0 / 108 (breakfast, uncle sheets, chapandaz line-up, ring). The dirty/clean boy is two Elements, not one plus prompt text.
2. **The identity text is repeated verbatim next to the tag** in the long prompts ("<<<e0176cf4…>>>, a thin, small-framed boy, unconventional narrow face, sharp sunburned cheekbones, a nose crooked from an old improperly healed break, noticeably asymmetric ears … a dark irregular birthmark on the right side of his neck and jaw").
3. **Value-locked wardrobe and animals** as fixed phrases (Seedance prompts): "light bleached-bone-white chapan" (40), "traditional black Afghan clothing" (31), "light grey, almost white coat … no patches" ("almost white" 45), plus negatives "any patches or markings on the horse", "a dark or brown horse", "a second horse with the same coat pattern".
4. **Look from the keyframe:** 120 prompts lean on "first frame", and "single source for the …" appears in 28 (8 with the exact "room, the light, the colour" wording). Multi-shot clips state "The light, the room and the colour are identical in all eight and never change", so one Nano Banana plate lights a whole scene.
5. **Sky continuity in stills** (sky-replace edits) and **time-of-day edits** before animation.
6. **A population lock** stops new people and crowd drift: "Exactly two people exist in this clip … Any third figure = failed take".
7. **The B&W grade** absorbs the warm/cool differences between early and late renders. This is a strong reason to choose B&W for an AI film.
8. **Reference trace:** 98 video references resolve directly to Nano Banana jobs (78 Seedance, 17 Kling, 3 editor). 144 are media copies of earlier outputs (123 Seedance images, 5 Seedance start images, 15 Kling start images, 1 editor start image), which the data cannot trace. Image inputs: 316 point to Nano Banana jobs and 1,194 to media copies. There are 7 image uploads in total, so almost every reference is the project's own generation.

## Adopt for our pipeline (concrete rules)

1. **For any B&W film: render in colour, convert in post** with plain luminance and a lifted-black, soft-shoulder curve (start from the curve in Focus A). Never put "black and white" in a Seedance or Kling prompt.
2. **Add a `Value in B&W` field** to `characters.md`, `props.md` and `locations.md` entries, repeat that word in every prompt, and write one hierarchy sentence per shot ("<X> stays the brightest thing in frame after the <window/sky>").
3. **Give each shot one stated white source** plus backlight or rim. Write "deep shadow … not pure black — shape still readable".
4. **Use this standard NEGATIVE tail for B&W projects:** "film grain, added grain texture, visual noise, oversaturated colors, competing hues within one frame, digital sharpness, artificial glow or bloom, 3D-rendered or CG appearance, game-engine look, dissolving or melting shapes".
5. **Set framing in the still.** Render 16:9 with "no letterbox baked in", keep the action in the central band, crop to 2.35 in post and tilt per shot. Use FOV in degrees (≈47° group, ≈29° single) or one lens (50 mm) instead of a lens zoo.
6. **Frame by body landmarks** ("framed at the wrists", "from the brow to the top of the cheekbones", "shoulders down to the knees, faces at the top edge") for intercuttable ECUs.
7. **Write one Seedance clip per scene with 4–9 HARD CUT shots**, a static camera in every shot, and one light for all shots, with `--- SHOT n --- mm:ss-mm:ss` + FRAMING / ACTION / ACTING. Mine it for coverage.
8. **Write each recurring failure as a named LAW with an "= failed take" clause** (POPULATION, SILENCE, MOTOR, BREATHING, EMOTION with both extremes banned, an object-specific law such as WRIST).
9. **Use this Element role sentence:** "<<<id>>> is the identity of the <person> ALREADY <doing X> at <frame position> in that first frame. It exists only to keep face, hair and clothing correct across the cuts. It is not an instruction to place, add or introduce anyone."
10. **Kling 3.0 = living stills from a finished keyframe** (one moving element, everything else rigid). Seedance = acting, coverage and audio.
11. **Scout long takes at 480p and keep at 720p.** Upscale in post. 1080p is optional (3 of the 30 Seedance picks are 480p).
12. **Stretch a right-but-short take with a prompt-less extend/retime pass** instead of re-prompting.
13. **Keyframe pass order in Nano Banana:** base → insert Element → relight/time-of-day → dust/haze → camera distance → sky match to the neighbour shot → letterbox removal and cleanup → B&W preview check.
14. **Folder tree = shot list:** `Scenes/Scene N. Name/<shot code script line>/` for keyframes plus `Scenes/Scene N. Name/Animation/` for video, with empty folders for planned shots and VO files named by the same codes.
15. **Design graphic match cuts at keyframe stage** ("matching the lighting style and composition of the <previous ECU> reference").

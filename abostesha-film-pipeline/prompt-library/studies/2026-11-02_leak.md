# Study: LEAK, by creativemanifesto (https://higgsfield.ai/@creativemanifesto/projects/@id__62fda908-531f-4338-98b0-c81bd2f2f5d4), 2026-11-02

- **Source type:** Higgsfield project with public prompts and canvas, plus the finished film. The film runs 7:17 (436.8 s) as a 2.33:1 scope master (2206×946, 24 fps) and was entered in the Higgsfield Global Film Festival (2,417 views, 14 likes). The project holds 50 jobs: 43 Seedance 2.5 videos (10,368 credits) and 7 Nano Banana Pro stills (14 credits; tuxedo headshots made on 15–16 Sep, not used in the film). It also holds one upload, the final cut "SLOW LEAK_V06_NEW_SCORE_NO_LOGO.mp4" (853 MB). The brief reads: "A grainy, intimate portrait of two worn women navigating rain-soaked silence, small domestic rituals, and memory's warm ghosts." The end credits list: Higgsfield · GPT Images 2.0 · Seedance 2.x · Kling · Topaz Apollo · Claude / Anthropic · Adobe Premiere Pro · DaVinci Resolve · "sound design & music — AI-generated". They also state that the film was "directed with artificial intelligence by a single filmmaker".
- **User's focus:** acting and realism. This study looks at how the prompts direct performance, emotion, micro-acting, dialogue and camera so that the result looks like people being filmed, not AI.
- **What it achieves:** a two-hander chamber drama set in one small 1950s bungalow (kitchen, hallway, living room, bedroom, bathroom, porch, front door). It has the look of a 1990s 16 mm American indie: handheld camera that "breathes", long lenses, practical light only, and desaturated teal-green-grey with warm amber where a lamp burns. Golden, honey-graded memories are cut against a cold present. The acting is underplayed to the edge of stillness: eyes, breath, jaw, thumbs, a mug, a cigarette. Dialogue is rare. The project has 30 distinct lines, median 5 words, and many scenes are silent. The physical business is believable: newspaper peeled off mugs, tape ripped from a box, a CRT TV pushed with real weight, box flaps opened one at a time. The same two women and the same house hold for 83 cuts with no visible identity drift.
- **Method step by step:**
  1. **Write the whole film as one shot plan, then cut it into 30 s generations.** Claude is credited, and the prompts read like a single plan. They cross-reference each other: "the exact same composition as CLIP 1 Shot 2", "⚠️ This is the ONLY focus move in the entire film", "the first held eye contact of the entire film", "this memory is the second of the film's only two stable shots". Every generation was written knowing its place in the arc.
  2. **Build the references in about 20 minutes, before the first video (30 Aug 04:08–04:30 UTC).** These are two character frames (`MAI_CF_01`, `JUNE_CF_02`) and five empty-location master frames (21:9 4K plates already in the final grade). A sixth plate, `THE-CORRECT-LIVINGROOM_02`, followed at 04:48, 11 minutes after the first video (04:37). All were registered as named Higgsfield **Elements** with a category (character / environment / prop). The images were made outside this project, so their prompts are not public. Later additions: prop sheets (PhotoFrame, Belt, Blanket), an **overhead floor plan** for blocking, two bright "happy" character photos for the memory scenes, and kitchen angle plates.
  3. **Generate a first pass of the story in 2 h 44 min.** On 30 Aug, 04:37–07:21, 11 × 30 s multi-shot Seedance jobs (2–5 shots each) covered what reads as a complete first draft [INFERRED: there is no record of an edit at this stage]: the drip, waking, the bedroom confrontation, the bathroom and porch, the reconciliation over tea, and the ending. 10 of those 11 jobs are in the final cut.
  4. **Expand the story on day 2 (24 jobs, 31 Aug UTC).** The author added the move-in memory prologue, the drip/reading scene and the jealousy → argument → strike arc, mostly as 30 s multi-shot scenes. From 14:47 UTC the day also has the first single-take acting scenes (`8eb5700d`, `f5373167`). The box discovery was already written on day 1 (`97f57cca`) and was reworked on day 3. Identical prompts were rerolled two or three times for coverage, and the edit cuts between rerolls (see Consistency).
  5. **Fix the key acting beats on day 3 (8 jobs, 1 Sep UTC).** Seven are **single continuous takes** with a second-by-second timeline (the photo/box beats, the bedroom collapse, the kitchen "I can't do this right now."). Three of them use a 100 mm lens; the others use 28 mm or 50 mm. Two (`2f7d7a21`, `ecc32053`) use start/end frames grabbed from earlier takes, and the rest use uploaded frames as image references. One insert (box flaps) got a physics script. The eighth job is the golden memory montage `fab70891`, a 6-shot multi-shot prompt.
  6. **Edit.** The film has 83 cuts (median 4.2 s, mean 5.3 s, six shots of 11.9–19.4 s; the 17.5 s one at 5:27 is two takes joined by a dissolve). 32 of the 43 jobs reach the cut. A 30 s job typically gives 2–6 cuts, and one gives 28 s. Scenes were reordered: the jealousy, argument and strike arc written on day 2 plays before the "Three years, Mai" confrontation written on day 1, which now follows the box discovery. The native Seedance dialogue and SFX were kept and a new AI score was laid over them. The title and credits play at the end over the empty-room plates (kitchen → bedroom → living room).
- **Prompt structure:** see "Prompt anatomy" below. In short: a **cast + location block** (each Element token with a human label and a wardrobe/state line), then **hard rules with ⚠️**, then numbered **【SHOT n】 (a–b s)** blocks containing Camera / Action / Line / micro-beats, then one long **Style** block (look, camera, performance, physics, continuity, lighting, audio, negatives), ending "30 seconds. 2:1." The median is 569 words; the range is 75–880 words and 487–5,941 characters.
- **Asset method:** see "Asset method" below. Character frames are 9:16 4K "casting boards" that carry behaviour as well as looks. Location master frames are 21:9 4K empty plates shot as real camera set-ups in the final grade. Props are flat lays on neutral ground. There is one floor-plan image for blocking. Frame grabs from earlier generations are re-uploaded as layout and continuity anchors.
- **Adopt:** see "Adopt for our pipeline".
- **Do not copy:** Mai and June, their looks, wardrobe and names; the bungalow, faucet-drip motif, belt, cracked mug, photo-in-the-box device and dialogue lines; the "Kingsley Films" end credits. Take the method only.
- **Proposed skill changes:** see the last section.

## What the film looks like (watched)
I watched the three contact sheets, all 83 cut and mid frames, five timed mid-frame sheets, and the film at 4 fps (1,747 frames) against 2 fps samples of all 43 job videos (about 2,300 frames, matched by correlation and checked side by side). I also read the credits frame by frame. **I could not hear the audio.** What I measured about it is in Numbers.

- **Structure (7:17):**
  - 0:02–1:05: warm **memory prologue** (move-in day, documentary handheld) and a golden intimacy montage.
  - 1:05: 4 s black.
  - 1:10–2:38: **the present**: the drip irritates June; jealousy in the kitchen ("I saw you looking at her yesterday"); the sofa argument.
  - 2:40–2:53: **the strike**, never shown: whip-pan to the window.
  - 2:53–3:51: **aftermath**: an empty hallway; the kitchen held for 12 s (empty for about 3 s, then Mai walks to the sink); June waking on the couch; the ceiling stain; a cigarette; still lifes; June at the window.
  - 3:51–5:00: **the box**: June finds Mai's packed clothes and the face-down photo; Mai in the doorway; "Three years, Mai." / "Don't."; June with her head in her hands, a 19 s single take.
  - 5:00–5:58: **apart**: Mai in the bathroom, June on the porch, Mai between two doors, June's lamp click, Mai fixes the faucet.
  - 5:58–6:50: **truce**: tea, side by side ("You fixed the faucet."), asleep together, moonlight, the last drop, cut to black.
  - 6:50–7:17: the title "leak" and credits over the empty rooms.
- **Look:** 2.33:1. Present-day scenes are muted teal-green walls, concrete grey and milky lifted blacks, with heavy 16 mm grain and halation on windows. The only warm light is a fabric-shade table lamp. Memory scenes are honey and amber with sun through blinds. Skin is unretouched, with pores, moles and tired eyes. The house reads as one real place because every room was generated from the same plates.
- **Shot sizes and lenses:** mostly medium shots and close-ups on long lenses (50/65/85/100 mm), shallow focus and over-the-shoulder foreground blur. Wides are low or high-angle set-ups for empty rooms. There are two overhead couch shots (beginning and end, rhymed), frame-within-frame doorways, and inserts of hands and objects.
- **Pacing:** slow. The median shot is 4.2 s, and silences and holds are long: 12.1 s on the kitchen, 14.3 s on June in the doorway, 16.2 s following the mugs, 19.4 s on June on the bed. Only the memory montages and the strike cut fast.
- **Acting as seen:** almost no "AI face" acting. Emotion shows as stillness, eyes that fix and drop, a jaw that works, arms that cross and uncross, hands that stop in mid-air. The strongest moments are reactions without dialogue: June's eyes lifting over her book, Mai not turning from the sink, June alone at the rain-streaked window.

## Acting and realism playbook (the focus)
These are the exact devices the prompts use. Counts are over the 43 video prompts.

**1. The character reference carries behaviour, not only looks.** `JUNE_CF_02` is a 2160×3840 casting board: 10 candid film stills of June rolling a cigarette, cradling a mug, smoking, slumped on a couch, a close-up of the wrist tattoo, plus a typed card *inside the image*: "JUNE — Around 30, white, brunette. intimate energy: beautiful in a restless, defended way… Wardrobe (locked): … Rolls her own cigarettes. Constant micro-motion — jiggling knee, thumbnail picking at a mug's glaze, jaw working. The one who fills silence, badly." The video model sees the behaviours it is asked to perform. Mai's frame is one full-body portrait in a long dark corridor (not the film's `HALLWAY_MF` plate), already in the film's grade. Mai is written to be still and June restless, and the references encode that contrast.

**2. Each scene restates the character with this scene's state, expressed physically.** The full bio appears in early prompts. From day 2 the character line is wardrobe plus the emotion of *this* scene as body mechanics, for example: "⚠️ Throughout this clip she is AFRAID — the fear is physical and rising: shoulders drawing in, chin lowering, hands coming up small and defensive, weight shifting back, breath shallow and quick (not sighing — quick shallow inhales)". Another: "⚠️ Her temper ESCALATES in visible steps: from tight-jawed → to up on her feet → to looming → to the strike". Another: "Calm, tired, washing up at the sink. She does not want this fight; she deflects, quiet."

**3. Numbered micro-beats inside a shot (①②③), flagged ⚠️⚠️⚠️.** Example: "Performance micro-beats: ① her weight shifts slowly onto one hip ② one palm comes to rest flat on the counter ③ one visible slow exhale sinking through her shoulders". Another: "① she looks at the mug, not at Mai ② takes it with both hands ③ ⚠️ without being asked, she shifts over to make room". Another: "The understanding lands as physical micro-beats: ① jaw sets ② eyes go wet with catchlight — no tears fall ③ she sits back onto her heels". Beats are tiny, physical and countable. The emotion is never named alone.

**4. Subtext as playable objectives, never spoken** (`717a5ecd`): "INTERNAL OBJECTIVES — play these, never speak them: … is hiding something and wants to survive this breakfast without being asked about it. Stillness is her shield. Every answer gives the minimum. … The brunette wants to find out if they are still okay — without risking the real question. The cigarette, the mug and the small talk are her cover." This take was not used, and "INTERNAL OBJECTIVES" appears in no other prompt. The idea of naming what a line does returns in two later scenes: "The worst possible answer, delivered kindly" (the reading scene, 4 rolls) and "⚠️ The exact wrong thing. The air changes." (the sofa argument, `92420456`, `df8c991f`). "The safe version of a dangerous question" is only in `717a5ecd`.

**5. Anti-performance negatives, repeated in nearly every prompt:**
- "⚠️ BLINKING: natural and MINIMAL — occasional real blinks only, never fluttering, never rapid" (a BLINKING rule is in 22 prompts; this exact wording in 1, the others are shorter variants such as "natural minimal blinking")
- "⚠️ NO sighing anywhere — breath is quick and shallow (fear) or hard through the nose (rage), never a performed sigh" ("NO sighing" is in 21 prompts; this full wording in 1)
- "no big expressions, no eyebrow acting, no performed emotion; feelings hidden, not shown"
- "underplayed to the edge of doing nothing, nearly neutral faces … No theatrics, no crumpling, no trembling, no staged pauses"
- "Emotion in the body and stillness, not the face"
- "Real, ugly, frightening. Not theatrical, not shouting-for-camera — a person genuinely losing control"
- In the Style block: "Performance: minimalist realism, understated — micro-pause before reactions, precise eyelines, wet living eyes with catchlight, visible breath and chest movement, lines delivered low, mumbled, almost to oneself."

**6. Dialogue engineering.** 24 prompts have spoken lines. There are 30 distinct lines, with a median of 5 words and a maximum of 13; a 30 s scene has at most 6.
- **Delivery comes before the quote and behaviour after it.** Example: "Without looking up, quiet, almost casual — the safe version of a dangerous question: "Did you sleep?" After the line her eyes lift for exactly one second — searching the other's face — then drop back to the mug." Example: "Then flat, even, giving nothing: "Sort of." She lifts her mug and drinks immediately after — ending the conversation with the gesture."
- **The addressee is locked.** "⚠️ Spoken to <<<Mai>>>." / "aimed at the kitchen" / "says to Mai off-frame".
- **Lines are placed in shots.** "⚠️ Dialogue rule: one line = one shot — each character's line appears STRICTLY only inside that character's close-up" (this wording in 4 prompts; a shorter "One line per shot" in 9 more, 13 in all). Lines can be off-screen ("June's voice from off, low, an accusation dressed as casual").
- **Interruptions are scripted at the cut.** "starts: "I was going to—" ⚠️ FORCIBLY INTERRUPTED … the hard cut lands in the middle of her word "to"". The answering shot opens with "on the very first frame of the shot she takes a sharp inhale through the nostrils — grabbing air to interrupt".
- **Volume is set per register.** In the present: "low, mumbled, natural, no stage delivery". In the memories, the opposite rule: "⚠️ DIALOGUE IS CLEAR: spoken English, natural but fully intelligible, normal conversational volume … NOT mumbled, NOT whispered". The mumbled direction risks unintelligible lines, so the author switched it off where the words matter.
- **A fixed line needs a clean prompt.** Two tries (`aa05d9b2`, `5e998b66`) said both "dialaoge: …" and "⚠️ June is alone. No dialogue." `5e998b66` was dropped, and `aa05d9b2` survives only as a 2.9 s silent cutaway (1:50). The clean rewrite `f5373167` ("calm, low, natural — not an accusation yet, almost casual, testing it") is in the film as a 14.3 s take.

**7. Camera realism is written as an operator, not a rig.** "handheld throughout with natural operator breathing — no stabilizer, no zooming, no slider smoothness" ("operator breathing" is in 24 prompts; this full sentence in 10). "the operator genuinely chasing the movement — imperfect framing that catches her at collarbone height". "reframes mid-shot, catches focus a beat late, leans in when something is good". "Handheld goes ragged — whip reframes, focus losing and finding, the operator flinching". "never locked … a held frame that quietly floats". 42 of 43 prompts name a lens (`717a5ecd` names none). By mentions: 50 mm 38, 65 mm 20, 85 mm 20, 35 mm 19, 100 mm 10, 25 mm 9. The long lenses (65–100 mm) carry the acting close-ups and give compression plus foreground blur. "180° shutter" and "24fps" are in all 38 style blocks; the full line "Physical cinema lenses, 180° shutter motion blur, 24fps" is in 12 prompts and "No optical distortion" in 14 (the long style blocks).

**8. Light realism prevents the "lit by AI" look** (a no-cinematic-fill line in 18 prompts; the full block quoted here, from the long style block, in 11): "⚠️ STRICTLY only light sources that exist in the scene — grey overcast daylight through net curtains and blinds, warm amber fabric-shade lamp… NO cinematic fill of any kind — no front light, no side fill, no top light, no bounce boards, no softboxes, no LED strips, no neon, no off-screen sources. Camera always shoots from the character's shadow side. Hard shadow shapes preserved on walls. No visible light beams (god rays)." Skin: "Skin desaturated, tired, real — pore-level detail, fine hairs, asymmetrical moles. Hyper-real — no 3D render, no game engine, no CG look, no HDR feel, no oversaturation."

**9. Physics is written as process.** "⚠️ EVERY ACTION STARTS FROM ITS REAL BEGINNING AND COMPLETES WITH TRUE PHYSICS: wrapped things are fully WRAPPED until hands unwrap them; … tape RIPS off the box before the flaps open; the TV has real weight and scrapes as it's pushed. Nothing is pre-done, nothing floats, nothing skips its middle." The box insert (E10) scripts four flaps in order, with "⚠️ NO flap opens by itself… Flaps do NOT disappear, dissolve, or merge into the wall/bed". It also locks the result with a start frame (closed box) and an end frame (open box grabbed from an earlier take).

**10. Sound directs the acting.** "⚠️⚠️⚠️ SUBJECTIVE SOUND — the engine of this scene: the drip falls once per second the entire clip, and in the MIX it slowly GROWS … we hear it the way JUNE hears it." Other examples: "that "click" is the loudest sound in this clip"; "⚠️ The silence lands hard: one full beat of complete quiet". Diegetic SFX are listed per scene ("room tone, rain on the roof, water drips, creaking floorboards, fabric, breath, matches. No music.").

**11. Violence stays off-screen and the reaction is the scene.** "⚠️⚠️⚠️ THE STRIKE IS NEVER SHOWN: the camera has whipped away before impact — we HEAR it, we see before and after, never the blow." The film follows this design (whip-pan to the window at 2:44). The final strike take is not in this project; the project's version `676eb3ff` is unused, and the film's version was presumably regenerated elsewhere [INFERRED].

**12. Prefer single takes for the hardest beats.** From the afternoon of day 2 (31 Aug 14:47 UTC, `8eb5700d`) and through day 3, the decisive beats are "Single shot (one-shot, no cuts)" with a timeline (0–5 s / 5–11 s / 11–16 s / 16–20 s). Each window holds one beat, and the last window says "Hold". Examples: June arriving at a suspicion and then speaking (`f5373167`); June going from standing to folded on the bed, then a pan to Mai (`2f7d7a21`); June's arms dropping as Mai says "I can't do this right now." (`11210ba6`). These give the film's longest and most believable shots.

## Prompt anatomy
### Seedance 2.5 video prompts (43 jobs, all 1080p, 21:9, audio on, no draft, `multi_shots=false`, model default)
Order of blocks in the day-1 template (E1):
1. Header: `Multi-shot edit.` / `Single shot (one-shot, no cuts).` / `Image-to-video, multi-shot edit. The attached frame is the first frame of SHOT 1 and the layout authority for the whole video.`
2. **Cast and locations:** each Element token is written `<<<element-uuid>>> (Name) — description`. Characters get age, ethnicity, build, hair, distinguishing marks (mole under the left eye, stick-and-poke tattoo on the left inner wrist, bitten nails), "Wardrobe (locked for the whole film): …" and a movement trait ("Her movement is slow, restrained, minimal" / "Constant small restless movements in her fingers and knee"). Locations describe what the camera sees and the state for this scene ("the table lamp now switched OFF", "two no-longer-steaming mugs").
3. **⚠️ Global rules:** `⚠️ This video has STRICTLY N shots — do not add extra shots.` (26 prompts), `No dialogue anywhere.`, `⚠️ Jump cuts are intentional, stylized editing.`, `⚠️ Spatial layout: … about 6 meters apart, no eye contact.`, and the dialogue rule.
4. **【SHOT n】(approx. a–b s)** blocks, each with `Camera:` (lens, height, angle, movement, focus), `Action:`, `Line:` and `⚠️⚠️⚠️ Performance micro-beats: ①②③`, `Sound:`, then `Hard cut.` / `HARD CUT … no transition, no fade.` / `Hold to the end.` / `Hard out.` Jump-cut chains go inside one shot: "⚠️ Three jump cuts within the same setup: ① … ② … ③ …".
5. **Style block** (in 38 of 43 prompts, 61–264 words). It comes in two lengths. The long version (214–264 words) is in all 11 day-1 jobs plus `a0b54bf3`, `2f7d7a21` and `11210ba6`. The short version (61–108 words) is in most day-2 jobs and the four day-3 photo/box takes; the 360° pans (199), `ecc32053` (145) and `fab70891` (181) sit in between. The parts listed here are the long version: era/stock (the "1990s American independent film" line is in all 38, worded "shot on 16mm color negative" in 14 and "16mm color negative" in 24) · grain/halation/gate weave/milky blacks · "Color 60:30:10 — cool teal-green shadows / concrete grey / warm amber accent (only where practicals burn)" · skin · anti-CG · camera · lens/shutter/fps · no distortion · Performance · Physics · Continuity ("characters, wardrobe, props and environment identical in every shot. No identity drift.") · Lighting (practicals only, no fill, shadow side) · Audio (diegetic list, "No music. No subtitles. No on-screen text.").
6. Tail: `30 seconds. 2:1.` (28 prompts; "2:1" closes 38, and shorter jobs give their own length). The jobs are 21:9; "2:1" is the author's framing note. Sometimes extra element tokens are appended after it, to attach a reference that the body text does not name.

Evolution of the template (useful to copy as a sequence):
- **Day 1 (jobs 0–10):** full template, 2–5 shots per 30 s, long style block.
- **Day 2 early:** experiments. These were a 360° one-take with a floor-plan Element (`801dc475`/`d8727e52`, mostly abandoned: 4 short pieces used, 14.7 s, one of them under the end credits); image-to-video from a master two-shot still with INTERNAL OBJECTIVES (`717a5ecd`, unused); and a 7-shot "documentary montage" with "DOCUMENTARY RULES — these override everything" (`0b6c2abb`, the film's opening, 27 s used). The montage prompt was cut off at exactly 4,500 characters (it ends "…a documentary stolen"), so it shipped with Shot 6 cut mid-sentence, no Shot 7 and no style block (the text has no grain, film-stock or grade words at all). It still came out on-look in the film. [INFERRED] The likely reasons are the pre-graded house plates attached as Elements and the final grade in Resolve; the data cannot separate the two.
- **Day 2 main:** compact scenes. There is one paragraph per shot ("【SHOT 3】(13–17s) — ⚠️ the whip: 65mm on Mai…"), the character line gives this scene's emotional state, and a short style block holds "Performance: documentary-real … natural minimal blinking; NO sighing".
- **Day 2 late and day 3:** single-take acting scenes with a second-by-second timeline, from 31 Aug 14:47 UTC (`8eb5700d`). There are 16 single-take prompts in all; the 3 earlier ones are the two 360° pans and a 30 s drip insert. Day 3 adds an "⚠️⚠️⚠️ ACTING — ULTRA-REALISTIC, SUBTLE, NEVER OVER THE TOP" paragraph (2 prompts: `2f7d7a21`, `11210ba6`), start/end frames (2 prompts: `2f7d7a21`, `ecc32053`) and the physics script (`ecc32053`).

Reference naming in the text:
- Elements appear as `<<<uuid>>>` tokens, the stored form of an @-mention. In 40 of 43 prompts the author adds "(Name) —" after the token (not in `717a5ecd`, `4f9a18f9`, `a9099a5d`). All 160 attached Elements are named in the text by their token.
- The element called `LIVING-ROOM` actually shows the kitchen through a doorway, and the prompts label it "(Kitchen)". The text label wins.
- Uploaded images appear as `<<<image_1>>>` / `<<<image_3>>>` (job `2afac61d`) or "Starts on the attached frame" / "as in the reference frame".
- Two failure modes appear. `@image4 (Hallway)` / `@image5 (LivingRoom)` in `b4e29031` point at media that were never attached. `@[Belt_RF]` / `@[PhotoFrame]` / `@[Blanket_stripe_RF]` in 7 style blocks are plain text, not linked tokens.

## Asset method (characters / locations / props) and the reference chain
- **20 Elements** (4 character, 13 environment, 3 prop) were used 160 times across 43 jobs: characters 75, environments 73, props 12; median 4 per job, range 1–7. Every video job uses references. The most used are `JUNE_CF_02` ×35, `MAI_CF_01` ×30, `THE-CORRECT-LIVINGROOM_02` ×20, `LIVING-ROOM` ×11, `HALLWAY_MF` ×11, `BEDROOM` ×9 and `PhotoFrame` ×6.
- **Naming:** `_CF` = character frame, `_MF` = master frame, `_RF` = reference prop; `_02` marks a corrected version. `THE-CORRECT-LIVINGROOM_02` was made at 04:48, 11 minutes after the first video (which used `LIVING-ROOM`, really a kitchen view, as "(Kitchen)"). That the first plate was judged the wrong room rests only on the name [INFERRED].

## Consistency system (how two faces, one house and one look held for 7 minutes)
1. **Elements attached to every video job, and named in the text.** All 160 attachments are referenced by their token in the prompt, and 40 of 43 prompts also describe each one as `<<<uuid>>> (Name) — description`. Image and text agree. (`JUNE_CF_02` is attached in 35 jobs and `MAI_CF_01` in 30, not in all.)
2. **"Wardrobe (locked)"** sentences (12 prompts, mostly day 1) and distinguishing marks (mole under the left eye in 21 prompts; wrist tattoo in 22). Day-2 compact prompts often drop the full bio and give only this scene's state.
3. **A style block in most prompts, in two lengths.** 38 of 43 prompts carry a `Style:` block (31 variants, 61–264 words). All 38 share the stock line ("1990s American independent film … 16mm color negative"), grain, milky blacks, 180° shutter and 24 fps. The long version (14 prompts: all 11 day-1 jobs plus `a0b54bf3`, `2f7d7a21`, `11210ba6`) adds the 60:30:10 colour ratio, skin and anti-CG lines, Performance, Physics, "No identity drift", the practical-only/no-fill lighting list and the audio list; within the long version only the light sources and SFX change. The short version (61–108 words, most of day 2 and the day-3 photo takes) keeps stock, grain, palette, handheld, shutter, a one-line Performance ("documentary-real … natural minimal blinking; NO sighing") and the audio list.
4. **The plates are pre-graded.** Even the truncated montage (4,500 characters, no style block and no grade words) came out on-look in the film. [INFERRED] The plates are the likely reason, but the film was also graded in Resolve, so the data cannot prove it.
5. **Two looks, switched on purpose:** "⚠️ MEMORY GRADE: warmer than the rest of the film — golden afternoon sunlight, honey and faded-summer tones … but still desaturated, filmic, never postcard-saturated", with separate happy-state CFs.
6. **Rerolls as coverage:** 4 groups of identical prompts (2, 2, 3 and 2 jobs), plus near-twins that differ only by one Element token (`7a34288b` ≈ the 3-job reading group; `4183a77d` ≈ `d70e8774`). The edit mixes takes across rerolls: the bathroom comes from `4183a77d` and the porch and curtain shots from `d70e8774`; the reading scene uses `884abab0` for four shots and `7a34288b` for the line; the kitchen jealousy scene uses `29e77ff1` and `fe7539c3`; the montage uses `0b6c2abb` and `50added5` even though June's wardrobe differs between them (tolerated in a memory).
7. **Continuity locks for props** that drifted: "⚠️ CONTINUITY LOCK: it is draped over the back/arm of the dark-green couch … this exact striped afghan in every couch shot" (Blanket_MF); "Frame back is light-brown hardboard, never black."; and the belt kept "SUBTLE … never centered, never featured, never picked up".
8. **Visual rhymes written into the prompts:** "⚠️ exactly echoing the overhead composition of June waking at the start of the film"; "the exact same composition as CLIP 1 Shot 2, now in night silver".

## Adopt for our pipeline (concrete rules)
1. **Character sheets carry behaviour.** Our CF should be a 9:16 casting board: 6–11 candid stills of the actor doing their signature micro-behaviours, plus a short text card (age and look, "Wardrobe (locked)", 2–3 behaviours, one line of inner life). Keep one plain full-body portrait as well. Never name an emotion without its body version.
2. **Write the scene state into the cast line every time:** wardrobe + this scene's emotion as body mechanics (breath type, shoulders, chin, hands, weight, jaw).
3. **Micro-beats as ①②③, at most three per shot.** Make them physical and countable ("one visible swallow", "the corner of her mouth moves one millimeter").
4. **Standing anti-acting block in every dialogue or performance prompt:** "BLINKING natural and minimal — occasional real blinks only, never fluttering · NO sighing — breath quick and shallow (fear) or hard through the nose (anger) · no big expressions, no eyebrow acting, no performed emotion · emotion in the body and stillness, not the face · micro-pause before reactions, precise eyelines, wet living eyes with catchlight, visible breath". Adapt the wording to our film's culture.
5. **Dialogue format for Seedance:** `Line: <who>, <delivery adverbs + intention>: "<≤10 words>" ⚠️ Spoken to <who>.` followed by post-line behaviour. Use one line per shot, in the speaker's close-up. Script interruptions at the cut, and open the answering shot with an inhale. Set intelligibility explicitly ("clear, normal conversational volume" when the words matter; "low, thrown away" only for texture). Keep `generate_audio` on and keep the native take under the score.
6. **Hard emotional beats become single takes.** Use 10–20 s, a timeline in 4–6 s windows (one beat each), a long lens (85–100 mm), "only gentle handheld drift, no zoom", and end on "Hold". Supply start/end frames grabbed from the best earlier take. (This film used 100 mm in 4 of its 16 single takes and 28–50 mm in the rest; start/end frames in 2.)
7. **Light the AI out:** a practical-only block naming the scene's real sources, plus "NO cinematic fill … camera on the shadow side … hard shadow shapes preserved … no visible light beams", plus the skin line ("pore-level detail, fine hairs, asymmetrical moles; no CG look, no HDR feel").
8. **Camera as a person:** "handheld with natural operator breathing — no stabilizer, no slider smoothness"; "catches focus a beat late"; "imperfect framing"; one named lens per shot; "180° shutter motion blur, 24fps, no optical distortion".
9. **Physics as process:** "every action starts from its real beginning and completes" + explicit failure bans for the object, and start/end frames for any object state change.
10. **Location plates first**, in the final grade, framed as real set-ups, and registered as Elements with honest names. Label each token's role in the prompt "(Kitchen)", because the label wins. Add an overhead floor plan Element for geography, and `Spatial layout` lines with metres, sides and eyelines.
11. **Chain frames:** grab the last or best frame of an approved take, upload it, and use it as the ref or start frame of the next take for continuity and eyelines. Log the source job and second.
12. **Roll good prompts 2–3 times and cut between rerolls** as coverage. Budget about 3× the screen time (this film: about 34% of generated seconds on screen).
13. **Prompt hygiene:** check the end of every pasted prompt. The montage prompt ends at exactly 4,500 characters and lost its last shot and style block, while 11 other prompts ran to 5,941 characters intact, so the cut was not a Seedance limit (probably the author's copy tool) [INFERRED]. Never leave contradictions such as "No dialogue" next to a line, or dangling `@image4` tags. Re-read hand edits.
14. **Memory or flashback references dress the actors.** Make memory CFs in the exact memory wardrobe; the text may not override the image (1 of 2 identical rolls followed the image).

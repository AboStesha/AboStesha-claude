# Study: TOKYO 4:17 — nicholastakeyama (https://higgsfield.ai/@nicholastakeyama/projects/tokyo-4-17) — 2026-11-02

- Source type: Higgsfield project (open source, prompts and canvas public) + finished film. Published file 6:25 (384.96 s, 3840×1644 ≈ 2.34:1); the story runs 0:00–5:55 and the last ≈30 s is the Higgsfield Global Film Festival outro, not the film. 559 jobs between 5 Sep 03:40 and 11 Sep 06:01 2026 (6 days), 1,051 credits in total. Submitted 15 Sep.
- What it achieves: a Japanese-language night-to-dawn action comedy whose people pass as **real, unknown Japanese actors**. Eight recurring characters (salaryman A, older ex-gangster B, two hostesses, a floor manager, an old taxi driver, two pursuers) keep the same face, hair and clothes through 27 different Seedance 2.5 generations, in 10 lighting worlds (red club, green fire-exit corridor, wet alley, taxi at night, sodium garage, family restaurant, arcade, purikura booth, batting cage, dawn rooftop). Acting is quiet and natural (Japanese dialogue, real listening pauses, small comedic reactions), and the camera is bold (14 mm table-top rush, 180° optical-axis roll, whip pans, FPV dive, top-down drone). Shooting ratio is low: 64 video generations for a 5:55 film.
- Method step by step:
  1. **Day 1 (5 Sep, 389 jobs): cast and scout with the Cinema Studio "Soul" tools, no prompts.** Soul Cast (84 jobs, 18 parameter sets) produced casting sheets of ordinary-looking faces; Soul Location (130 jobs that day, 204 by 8 Sep) produced empty, practically lit Tokyo plates sorted into mood-coded folders (`loc - RED MIRROR`, `loc - SODIUM`, `loc - DAWN` …).
  2. **"Re-photograph, don't redesign."** The four chosen Soul Cast faces went through one identical 9,466-character GPT Image 2 prompt (4K) that keeps identity and adds real skin, fabric and studio photography ("IDENTITY > BEAUTY. REALISM > POLISH."). The hostesses were written as long text-only GPT Image 2 sheets, pushed through Soul Cinematic (no prompt) to look photographed, then re-formatted into the same three-panel sheet with a two-reference prompt (reference 1 = layout only, reference 2 = identity only). The two pursuers were short Seedream 5.0 Pro text sheets.
  3. **One sheet = one Element.** Every final character, location, vehicle and prop became a Higgsfield Element (28 Elements in total, each holding **one** image) with a role name (`salaryman-a`, `underworld-b`, `hostess-black`, `red-mirror`, `black-sedan`, `a-phone` …). The sheets live in `Characters/MAIN CAST` (11 jobs). `Characters/Extras` (2 random-generator sheets) was never used in video.
  4. **Video = text + Elements only.** All 64 Seedance 2.5 jobs ran at 1080p, 16:9, generate_audio on, bitrate high, with **no start frame, no image or video reference, no draft, no multi-shot, no extension**. Identity came only from the inline Element tags `<<<element-uuid>>>` (median 6 Elements per job, up to 12).
  5. **Two prompt eras.** 7–8 Sep: long English "LOCK v03" scene prompts (median 3,701 characters, up to 10,115) with storyboard codes (G01–G07, K01–K38), a timeline, background-extra blocking and Japanese lines in 「」. From 9 Sep: compact Japanese prose prompts (median 1,219 characters) with a fixed 35 mm colour-negative look line, explicit seat order, the character's backstory and a delivery note under every line. 18 of the 27 clips in the film come from the Japanese era.
  6. **Fill the edit with short pickups at the end (10–11 Sep):** 5 s single-purpose shots ("「着きました」", "追跡車に気づく", "エキストラの卓"), a 13 s opening insert, an 8 s 85 mm phone ECU, and new "dawn detour" scenes (arcade, purikura, batting cage) that give the leads screen time without plot.
  7. **Sound:** Seedance native audio for dialogue and SFX in every clip ("音楽、字幕なし" / "字幕、ナレーションなし" / "音楽、ナレーション、字幕、タイトルなし"); 9 external music beds uploaded (`Neon-Velocity-Chase`, `Rooftop-Dawn-Relief`, `Midnight-Tokyo-Chase` …; the tool that made them is not recorded); Seed Audio 1.0 voice pickups for the taxi driver ("ベルト、してます？", "着きました。") plus an uploaded `beltshitemasu.wav` and a glass-break SFX.
  8. **Post:** centre crop to ≈2.34:1 (prompts reserved it: "composed for a final centered 2.39:1 cinemascope crop … central approximately 74% of the image height"), English burned-in subtitles, a Seedream typography title card at 1:14, festival watermark.
- Prompt structure: see "Prompt anatomy" — image sheets are 2.8k–17.6k character spec documents with a final AVOID list; Seedance prompts are element tags → one-line element rule → look → situation/backstory → timed or prose beats with 「lines」 and delivery notes → continuity fences → sound line.
- Asset method (characters / locations / props sheets): see "Focus" — Soul Cast casting sheet → GPT Image 2 realism pass → three-panel gray sheet (full-body front, full-body back, close-up) → Element; Soul Location plate (empty, lit, no people) → Element; Seedream prop/vehicle sheets on white/gray → Element.
- Adopt: see "Adopt for our pipeline".
- Do not copy: the salaryman/ex-yakuza duo, the hostess club opening, the "call in sick at dawn" ending, the taxi driver character, the dawn-detour set pieces (arcade, purikura, batting cage), any dialogue line, the TOKYO 4:17 title, the faces in the Elements. Take the method only.

### F3. The realism pass: "re-photograph, don't redesign" (GPT Image 2)
- 4 jobs, **one identical 9,466-character prompt**, 4K 16:9, each with a single Soul Cast sheet as input: `c0543803→42cdcb0b`, `350ff3ad→678d6b1a`, `891c112e→ecbc743d`, `2f19306d→ef5a4515` (full text: E2).
- What the prompt does, in order: declares the input "the approved actor and character" and forbids redesign ("DO NOT invent a new man. DO NOT beautify him. DO NOT make him younger. DO NOT make him more conventionally handsome."); lists ~40 features to preserve (eye spacing, eyelid anatomy, facial asymmetry, under-eye character, mature skin characteristics …); asks to "dramatically improve the physical realism of the clothing" (wool, cotton, tie weave, leather); lists skin micro-detail but caps it ("Avoid excessive micro-detail or artificial sharpening. The face should look photographed, NOT rendered."); specifies neutral studio photography (soft key 45° camera-left, gentle fill, no rim, neutral white balance; 70 mm full body, 85 mm f/4 close-up; "Subtle lens softness rather than digital oversharpening. Very subtle sensor texture / photographic grain."); ends with a priority ladder: **"IDENTITY > BEAUTY. REALISM > POLISH. MATERIAL REALISM > DIGITAL SHARPNESS. HUMAN IMPERFECTION > AI PERFECTION."**
- Before/after (viewed side by side): identity, pose and proportions are unchanged; the backdrop becomes a physical seamless with tonal variation, fabrics get weave and creases, skin gets pores and redness without the plastic sheen of the Soul Cast render.
- **Caveat seen in the data:** the shared prompt contains a wardrobe block written for A ("dark navy business suit, white dress shirt, dark charcoal tie, slightly loosened tie"). On B, who wore an open-collar shirt in his Soul Cast sheet, GPT Image 2 **added a tie**; on the driver and floor manager the "Preserve the overall styling" line won and their own clothes survived. Rule: never reuse a realism prompt that names wardrobe; write "preserve the wardrobe exactly as in the reference" instead.

### F4. The hostesses — designed in text, then made to look photographed
- **Text sheets (GPT Image 2, no refs):** `56d9db86` (hostess, ≈27, black bob, 11,839 chars), `6b60cc95` and `d1ea861a` ("CHARACTER B", 23–24 and 20–21, 13–14k chars), `43e433de` (HOSTESS B, 20–21, honey-beige blonde, 17,572 chars) and its variant `0581163c`. (One earlier text-only GPT Image 2 sheet, `5ee95009` at 04:13, is a 4K four-view sheet of a Japanese **man**, 9,590 chars, never used; it is the 13th GPT Image 2 job.) Structure (section headers in capitals between `=====` rules): purpose and NOT-list ("This is NOT a collage … NOT a beauty campaign … NOT concept art") → layout → CHARACTER (age, height, job, personality) → FACIAL IDENTITY → EYES → EYEBROWS → NOSE → MOUTH → **DISTINCTIVE HUMAN DETAILS** ("one tiny beauty mark low on one cheek, slight eyelid asymmetry, minor asymmetry in the corners of the mouth, a tiny irregularity in eyebrow density … They should be details discovered when looking closely.") → SKIN ("Healthy youthful skin but NOT flawless skin … NO glass skin. NO porcelain skin … She should look photographed rather than rendered.") → HAIR → MAKEUP → BODY → WARDROBE → SHOES → JEWELRY → three PANEL blocks → THREE-PANEL IDENTITY CONSISTENCY → STUDIO → LIGHTING → PHOTOGRAPHIC QUALITY → **ABSOLUTELY AVOID** (40+ items: "generic AI beauty, K-pop idol, J-pop idol, anime face, doll face, giant eyes, tiny artificial nose, perfect facial symmetry, overfilled lips, plastic skin, glass skin, porcelain skin, influencer appearance …") → FINAL CHARACTER TARGET.
- Personality is written even for a still: "She should have the energy of someone who reacts immediately when someone says something ridiculous … Her youthful personality should remain visible even in a neutral casting photograph." / "She should look like an unknown Japanese actress who becomes unexpectedly magnetic on camera." The film's blonde hostess does read as bubbly and the bob-haired one as cool and observant, which matches these notes.
- **Soul Cinematic as a "photograph her for real" pass:** 136 of the 152 Soul Cinematic jobs have an **empty prompt** and one image input (style `General`, `enhance_prompt` on, batch 4, 1080p): 52 on `6b60cc95`, 44 on `56d9db86`, 16 on `d1ea861a`, 16 on a Soul Cinematic output (`d1dd3788`), 4 each on the A and B Soul Cast sheets; 8 used the colour preset "Soft palette". The engine restages the gray sheet as a film still (warm practical light, grain, real posture and skin). Only one output left Soul Cinematic: `31979526`, which became the face of the bob-haired hostess (one other output, `d1dd3788`, was fed back into Soul Cinematic for the 16 runs above).
- **Two-reference re-sheet:** `6c69c31c` (GPT Image 2, refs `31979526` + Soul Cast `dee84a99`) built a clean three-panel sheet; `8add87d7` then used **reference 1 = layout only, reference 2 = identity only** ("REFERENCE 1 = HOW TO DISPLAY HER. REFERENCE 2 = WHO SHE IS. … Do not average the two women together. Do not create a hybrid identity.", full text: E3). The same prompt ran 4× on Seedream 5.0 Pro (`46834183`, `cdeb660c`, `e467ce89`, `2922d4b4`); the GPT Image 2 result was the one promoted to MAIN CAST.

### F6. `Characters/MAIN CAST` vs `Characters/Extras`
- **MAIN CAST (11 jobs):** the 2 raw Soul Cast picks for the driver and floor manager, 7 GPT Image 2 sheets (A, B, driver, floor manager, both hostesses, one spare blonde variant) and the 2 Seedream pursuer sheets. 8 of the 11 became Elements; none of them holds a prop, a scene or mood lighting, only identity and costume on gray.
- **Extras (2 jobs):** `68839c51` and `c3307723` are outputs of a 12,882-character Seedream "random extra" generator (13 runs, later runs using an earlier output as the layout reference; 2 kept): "Generate ONE COMPLETELY NEW, RANDOM, UNIQUE fictional adult Japanese female hostess every time this prompt is executed … Each new generation should feel like a DIFFERENT ACTRESS arriving at the same casting studio." It randomises face, height (150–175 cm), body type, hair, wardrobe, shoes, jewellery, makeup and "personality read", and attacks the default AI face directly: "DO NOT default to the same standardized attractive AI face. Avoid repeatedly generating: small V-shaped face, large eyes, tiny nose, full lips, perfect symmetry." (full text: E5).
- **But no extra sheet was ever attached to a video job.** In Seedance the extras exist **only as text**, described by costume and blocking, and fenced off from the principals:
  - English era (G01 LOCK v03): "Eleven people total at opening: A, B, two principal hostesses, three additional adult women, three additional adult men, one floor attendant. … E1 silver-dress woman and E2 navy-suit man toast in the foreground; E3 olive-dress woman and E4 gray-suit man pour drinks in the middle distance; E5 plum-dress woman and E6 black-suit man occupy the far table. … Preserve their clothes and broad positions, not individualized faces." and "Do not duplicate principal faces into background patrons."
  - Japanese era: "エキストラは全員、主役と異なる顔と衣装。" (all extras have faces and costumes different from the leads); extras-only inserts attach only the location Element and add "ロケーション参照内に人物がいる場合も、その人物は再現しない" (if the location reference contains people, do not reproduce them) and "同じ顔や同じ人物を複製しない" (no duplicated faces) (E10).
  - Movement rules keep them alive: "All background activity is staggered and independent. No synchronized gestures, posing, camera-facing smiles or stationary mannequin-like patrons."
- In the film the club patrons are varied adults who never resemble A, B or the hostesses, and the only extra-heavy insert (0:03–0:06, `2a6d7847`) holds up at full-frame width.

### F8. How the faces stayed the same across 64 Seedance 2.5 jobs
1. **Elements only, never start frames.** 0 of 64 video jobs carry an image or video input; every one uses `reference_elements` (median 6 per job, max 12) except `95ff7a38`, a 5 s 「着きました」 pickup whose text names the driver and taxi Elements in words but carries no `<<<…>>>` tags; it was redone 15 minutes later with tags as `676253b5`, the take in the film. Each Element holds exactly one sheet image.
2. **Tags first, roles in plain text.** Prompts open with the Element tags on their own lines, then one rule line. English era: "ELEMENTS `<<<…>>>` = A, the adult salaryman; `<<<…>>>` = B, the older adult man at the neighboring table; … Use these references for identity, wardrobe, objects and architecture, not their reference-sheet composition." Japanese era: "A＝`<<<…>>>`、B＝`<<<…>>>`。外見・衣装・屋上は採用エレメントを使う。" or "人物の容姿・衣装、店内はエレメンツに従う。" (78% of video prompts contain such an "Elements govern appearance" line). In the body, characters are only called A, B, C, D, 黒髪 (black-haired), 金髪 (blonde), 運転手 (driver) — never re-described. For G02 the version that went into the film (`a88c8d98`) is the one that added explicit labels in front of the tags ("A: `<<<…>>>` B: `<<<…>>>`") and dropped an unneeded Element.
3. **Fixed geography.** Seat order is stated in every club prompt ("キャバ嬢Aはカメラから見て左、主人公Aは中央、もう一人は右" — the bob-haired hostess frame-left, A centre, the other right); car seats and right-hand drive in most car prompts ("両車とも日本の右ハンドル … Aは後席左、Bは後席右" / "Cars are RIGHT-hand-drive: driver VEHICLE RIGHT, front passenger VEHICLE LEFT."; right-hand drive in 25 of 64 prompts = 39%, i.e. 24 of the 30 prompts that attach a car or taxi Element). In the film no character ever swaps sides.
4. **One costume all night.** Nobody changes clothes in the story, so the sheet costume is valid in every shot; prompts only protect it: "Bは左肩を痛めているだけ。衣服は清潔で無傷。血、汚れ、破れ、包帯、バンダナなし。" (B's shoulder is only sore; clothes clean and intact; no blood, dirt, tears, bandage, bandana; variants insert an arm note, e.g. "Bの左肩は痛めているだけ。左腕は楽な位置に保ち、右手で動作する。衣服は清潔で無傷。…") — a B-shoulder line appears in ≈56% of prompts, the bandage/bandana list in 53%; the fence names bandana, bandage and sling explicitly, which suggests the model had been adding them after the fight scene.
5. **One look line, everywhere.** "35mm映画用カラーネガフィルムで撮影したような質感。細かい有機的なグレイン、なだらかなハイライト、ごく控えめなハレーション。深い黒と自然な肌色。" (35 mm colour-negative texture; fine organic grain, gentle highlight roll-off, very restrained halation; deep blacks and natural skin) or its short form (35 mm film / grain in 50–55% of prompts, halation 36%, negative fill 20%) plus "エレメンツ本来の色彩を維持" (keep the Elements' own colours) so the grade never re-colours faces or suits.
6. **Japanese speech for Japanese faces.** 48 of 64 prompts contain Japanese lines in 「」; the late prompts are entirely in Japanese. Mouth shapes and voice match the faces; English subtitles were added in post.
7. **Naturalism in the acting notes:** "日常会話のテンポで、返事をすぐに繋ぐ" (everyday tempo, answers follow quickly), "笑顔を要求せず" (do not demand a smile), "大げさな驚きや感動…を要求せず、この短い間を残して終わる" (no exaggerated surprise; end on the short pause), "ポーズではなく、笑いの途中のいちばん可愛い瞬間" (not a pose, the cutest moment mid-laugh), "実際の人物の顔には美顔加工をかけない" (no beauty filter on real faces, in the purikura prompt), "撮影機材は映さない" (no film gear in frame).

### F9. Rules to reuse (for our Seedance 2.5 film)
1. **Cast, don't describe.** Run Soul Cast in rounds of 7–10 with only role-relevant fields (age, era, outfit, archetype, build, gender); pick the least "AI-pretty" face that fits. Write the role's backstory in our bible, not in the casting tool.
2. **Then run one identity-locked realism pass** (GPT Image 2, 4K, our version of E2), with the wardrobe block replaced by "preserve the wardrobe exactly as in the reference". Compare before/after at 100% on eyes, hairline and collar; reject if any feature moved.
3. **Fix the sheet format for everyone:** one 16:9 image, three panels (full-body front, full-body back, 85 mm f/4 close-up), neutral warm-gray seamless, soft 45° key, no rim, no colour, no text, no props, clean costume. One sheet = one Element.
4. **Write imperfection on purpose** for designed characters: a "DISTINCTIVE HUMAN DETAILS" block (one mole, eyelid asymmetry, mouth-corner asymmetry, eyebrow irregularity), a "SKIN" block that forbids glass/porcelain/plastic skin, and an "ABSOLUTELY AVOID" list naming idol/anime/influencer faces.
5. **If a designed face still looks generated,** pass it through Soul Cinematic with an empty prompt, pick the most photographic still, then re-sheet it with the two-reference prompt (layout from the clean sheet, identity from the still).
6. **Villains and minor roles: short Seedream sheets** (≈3k chars) with height in cm, hair length in cm, and "no theatrical scowl".
7. **Extras live in text:** name them by costume and place (E1 silver dress, foreground …), forbid principal faces on them, keep them moving independently; attach only the location Element to extras-only inserts. Keep the random-extra generator for posters or a crowd-plate, not for video.
8. **Locations: generate empty, practically lit plates** (Soul Location, many tries per place), file them in mood-coded folders, promote one per place to an Element. Give Elements neutral names that we would accept as on-screen text (or codes that cannot become signage).
9. **In every video prompt:** tags on top; a role line ("A = `<<<…>>>`, B = `<<<…>>>`"); "Elements govern face, hair, wardrobe and set; do not copy the sheet layout"; seat order / screen side; costume-state fence; one look line; lines in the actors' native language with a delivery note under each; "each line once, in order"; "no music, no subtitles, no narration".
10. **Never re-describe a principal's face in a video prompt.** Describe what they do and feel; leave who they are to the Element.

## Prompt anatomy

### Image prompts (492 image jobs; 424 of them have no text prompt at all)
- **No-prompt tools did the bulk:** Soul Location 204 and Soul Cast 84 store no prompt; 136 of 152 Soul Cinematic jobs have an empty prompt and one image input. Text was written only where a tool needed it.
- **GPT Image 2 (13 jobs, median 9,466 characters, 3,563–17,572; quality high, internal mode `videotape-alpha`, 4K 16:9 for every promoted sheet, 2K for the three early hostess drafts).** Spec-document style: a one-paragraph purpose with NOT-framing ("THIS IS NOT A CHARACTER REDESIGN.", "This is NOT a fashion editorial."), then CAPS section headers between `=====` rules, one short instruction per line, long noun lists (features to preserve, textures to add), a priority list or ladder near the end, an **ABSOLUTELY AVOID** list, and a FINAL PURPOSE/TARGET paragraph. Three types: text-only character sheet (`43e433de`, quoted in F4), single-reference realism pass (E2), two-reference "format vs identity" re-sheet (E3).
- **Seedream 5.0 Pro (38 jobs, median 3,532 characters, 611–12,882; 2K 16:9).** Shorter, plainer English in labelled paragraphs (`LEFT PANEL:` / `CHARACTER:` / `HAIR:` / `WARDROBE:` / `STUDIO PRESENTATION:` / `STRICT CONSISTENCY:`): the two pursuer sheets (E4), six prop/vehicle sheets (black sedan, white sedan, Tokyo taxi, car key, smartphone, compact pistol — two-panel front-¾ / rear-¾ for cars on light-gray seamless; "Japanese right-hand drive, steering wheel on the vehicle's RIGHT side. - **Camera/lens language in image prompts is photographic, not cinematic:** "approximately 70mm full-frame equivalent, camera level, minimal perspective distortion" for full bodies; "approximately 85mm … eye-level … approximately f/4 … both eyes clearly resolved" for close-ups; "Camera approximately lower-chest height". Light is always neutral studio for sheets ("Large soft diffused key source approximately 45 degrees camera-left. Very gentle broad fill from camera-right … Do not use rim light. Do not use colored light.").

## Consistency system (summary)
1. **One gray three-panel sheet per person, one image per Element, role-named Elements,** attached to every shot where the person appears; no start frames anywhere. Usage: A 56 video jobs, B 52, C 28, hostesses 19 each, floor manager 17, D 17, driver 15.
2. **Same sheet grammar for every source** (Soul Cast, GPT Image 2, Seedream): same panels, same gray, same neutral light, no text → Seedance treats all eight people the same way.
3. **Locations and props are Elements too** (13 location plates, 6 prop/vehicle Elements): `red-mirror` 24 jobs, `black-sedan` 21, `tokyo-taxi` 15, `a-phone` 14, `taxi-interior` 15, `underground-parking` 9.
4. **Fixed text blocks** carried across prompts: the Elements rule line, the 35 mm colour-negative look line, the seat-order line, the right-hand-drive line, the B-shoulder costume fence, the sound line.
5. **Geography written as rules** (seat order, rear-left/rear-right, mirror behind the sofa, three tables) and **handoff frames** between sequences (G01 ends on a round glass from above → G02 opens on the round chandelier from below: "G02冒頭の円形シャンデリアへ繋ぐ").
6. **Selection lived in the edit, not in favourites:** liked/favourite = 0; the `Videos` folder held 30 clips but only 15 of the 27 clips in the film were filed there. Many finals were the second or third version of a scene, rerendered in the newer prompt style, and pickups were made after the cut was assembled.

1. **Cast with a casting tool, then lock with a realism pass.** Soul Cast rounds of 7–10 per role (fields: gender, age, era, outfit, archetype, build); pick the most ordinary believable face; run one GPT Image 2 4K "enhance and reconstruct" pass with an identity-preserve list, a material-realism list, neutral studio photography and the ladder "IDENTITY > BEAUTY · REALISM > POLISH · MATERIAL REALISM > DIGITAL SHARPNESS · HUMAN IMPERFECTION > AI PERFECTION". Do not name wardrobe in a shared realism prompt.
2. **One sheet grammar for all characters:** 16:9, three panels (full-body front · full-body back · 85 mm f/4 close-up), neutral warm-gray seamless, soft 45° key + gentle fill, no rim, no colour, no props, no text, clean costume. File as `Characters/MAIN CAST`.
3. **Designed faces need written imperfection:** DISTINCTIVE HUMAN DETAILS (mole, eyelid and mouth-corner asymmetry, eyebrow irregularity), SKIN (pores, redness, "NOT flawless", "NO glass/porcelain/plastic skin", "photographed rather than rendered"), personality written for a still, and an ABSOLUTELY AVOID list naming idol/anime/influencer/doll faces.
4. **De-AI a face that still looks generated:** Soul Cinematic, empty prompt, the sheet as input, batch 4; pick the most photographic still; re-sheet with a two-reference prompt ("REFERENCE 1 = HOW TO DISPLAY HER. REFERENCE 2 = WHO SHE IS. … Do not average the two … no hybrid identity").
5. **Element per sheet, role-named, one image only.** Characters, locations, vehicles and key props all become Elements. Name them with words we would accept as on-screen text (or neutral codes), because names can appear as signage.
6. **Locations:** many Soul Location tries per place, mood-coded folders, promote one empty, practically lit plate per place. Let the plate light the actors.
7. **Seedance prompts use Elements, not start frames,** with: tags on top → "A = `<<<…>>>`, B = `<<<…>>>`" → "Elements govern face, hair, wardrobe, set; do not copy the sheet layout; never put principal faces on extras" → one look line → situation/backstory → beats with lens + move → native-language lines with a delivery note under each → seat order / screen side / costume-state fence → "each line once, in order; no music, no subtitles, no narration".
8. **Extras by text:** costume + position + independent action per extra (E1 silver dress, foreground …), "exactly N people", "no synchronized gestures, posing, camera-facing smiles or mannequin-like patrons". Extras-only inserts attach only the location Element and say "do not reproduce anyone in the location reference".
9. **Write acting as intention + listening:** a CONTEXT block (who they are, what each wants, how the relationship moves), the reason behind each line, "let listening determine the cuts", "do not demand a smile", end on the pause.
10. **Move the camera boldly but land it:** one named move per beat, lens per beat, "after every fast move, hold a readable beat on the face", "never pass through bodies, glass, furniture", optical-axis roll with "only the camera rolls; people and liquid obey gravity".
11. **Plan the crop and the handoffs in the prompt:** "compose for a centred 2.39:1 crop; keep faces and actions inside the central ~74% of the height"; end a sequence on a shape the next one opens on (round glass from above → round chandelier from below).
12. **Shoot long scenes as one 26–30 s generation with written internal cuts** (multi-shot off), generate 30 s for a 26 s edit, and fill joins later with 5–8 s single-purpose pickups (one line, one reaction, one insert).
13. **Iterate the prompt style, not the seed:** when a scene fails, rewrite it shorter and in the actors' language (here 3.7k English → 1.2k Japanese) rather than rerolling the long version; keep the version label in the header.

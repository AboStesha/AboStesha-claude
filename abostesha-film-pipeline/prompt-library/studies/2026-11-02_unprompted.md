- **Source type:** a Higgsfield project with public prompts and canvas, plus the finished film. The file runs 7:09 (429.4 s), but the film itself ends at 6:58. - **User's focus:** transitions between scenes, and transitions of objects inside one location. Which final prompts built them, and with what: start/end frames, extension, Cinema Studio Video 4.0, morphs.
- **Short answer to the focus:** the transitions use **no start/end frames, no extension, no `multi_shots` parameter and no video references**. Every one of the 761 media references has role `image`, `extension_mode` is null on all 128 jobs, and `multi_shots` is false on all 128. All 128 videos are **Cinema Studio Video 4.0** (`cinematic_studio_video_4_0`, model `default`), mostly 30 s at 720p and 21:9 with audio on. The prompt does the work:
  1. A **reference map** names each image's role ("STARTING LOCATION" / "TARGET LOCATION" / "OBJECT-ONLY" / "FULL ENVIRONMENT … generate AROUND the viewer").
  2. A fixed **law block** follows. It is the full "MANIFESTATION LAW" block in 35 prompts (13 of the 28 final jobs; 1 more cites it by name) and "ABSOLUTE TRANSFORMATION LAW" in the opening. It bans fade, dissolve, crossfade, opacity and overlay, and it forces the change to run as **in-place re-resolution**: detail, then material, then mass, then the new silhouette, then the new material and detail, in visible discrete steps.
  3. **Timed sub-second states** run inside one continuous take.
  4. An explicit **A→B mapping list** names, for each element of the old place, what it becomes ("a bench lengthens and resolves into one of the office chairs").

  Between generations the joins come from **reference hand-off** (the target-location image of shot N is the starting image of shot N+1, plus 1920×1080 frame grabs of earlier takes) and from **editing**: intercut rerolls, a cut on a raw-noise frame, door and wall bridges, walking into a white medium, a held last frame and a 0.4 s light-leak burst.

Rooms rewrite themselves around a fixed figure: studio → disco-ball vault → studio → warehouse → studio → red gallery → office-palace → black → window. Objects inside a room cycle identities in a fixed footprint: tape → carpet → tape, cat → cat-teapot, five door handles, seven desks, ten table lights, candles in wall-hands, a chair that never finishes. ### B. The mechanics, generalised
1. **Same-surface rule (environment A→B).** The old surface must become the new one. Nothing is revealed underneath, and there is no second image. Typical wording: "EVERY EXISTING PHYSICAL SURFACE MUST ITSELF BECOME THE NEXT SURFACE", "Every new architectural element grows out of an existing piece of the studio", "There is no empty frame between rooms", "<<<image_4>>> begins resolving IN THE SAME REGIONS".
2. **A→B mapping list.** Pair 4–7 concrete elements, each A-thing with its B-thing:
   - frame → ceiling panel
   - red wall → ornate moulding
   - rope → carpet edge
   - bench → office chair
   - window → mirror frame
   - disco-ball facets → studio upper wall
   - light points → the single studio light pattern
   - station benches → corridor benches
   - bookshelves → door sections
   - lamps → corridor practicals
   - creature legs → door spacing
   - bulbs → ceiling-panel rhythm
   - ribbon → floor seam
   - eye → round fitting

   This list is the core of the method. The model will not invent good pairings on its own. [INFERRED: no prompt without a mapping list was compared]
3. **Resolution ladder with timestamps.** The order (condensed from the numbered list in the MANIFESTATION LAW, e.g. E1) is: raw boiling coloured static → large soft masses → silhouettes and edges → material and texture → "the smallest high-contrast details last". The reverse (de-resolution) runs: fine detail → material → edges → low-frequency mass → collapse. The ladder runs in **discrete jumps** ("six to eight jumps in total, each smaller and closer to the last, so the process decelerates"), and the prompt times each step: about 1 s steps for an object, about 0.5–1 s steps for a whole room in 5 s. Further rules: contact shadows and reflections arrive "one step behind", hands and faces resolve "last and worst", and deep shadows "never finish resolving".
4. **Anchor the subject.** In every room change the girl stands still, centred, back to camera: "remains fixed in approximately the same floor coordinates", "The protagonist does not transform. She remains exactly herself". The moving world needs a fixed reference.
5. **Fixed-footprint object carousel (objects inside a location).** Name the identities in order: 5 handles, 7 desks, 10 lights, 11 hand-held objects. Each change uses 4 compressed states: "fine detail disappears; material disappears; silhouette collapses into unresolved grip-sized image mass; new silhouette and material resolve in the same hand". Add a small physical tell on each change (a flame flare or "one brief electrical spark"), keep different objects unsynchronised, and keep one unchanged control object ("The left chair never changes").
6. **Location gives birth to the subject.** "Do NOT show a creature physically hidden beneath boards. The floor itself changes interpretation", and figures "gathers from the same airborne grains" (`f1be1ef4`). The reverse also works: the subject dissolves into the architecture.
7. **Collapse-to-black and generate-from-black** instead of fade-out and fade-in: "the active light solution abruptly ceases to exist. Do not fade the exposure … Then the room itself loses all readable structure … the window … GENERATES. It does not fade in. First the wall develops a shallow rectangular structural depression …".
8. **Negatives in two places.** The law block bans fade, dissolve, crossfade, opacity transition, transparency reveal, image overlay, a second image underneath and wipe (a composite of several prompts' lists, not one verbatim string; e.g. `c95879d3`: "no fade, no dissolve, no crossfade, no transparency reveal, no image overlay"; `921006a9`: "no opacity animation, no second image underneath"). The `No:` list adds "no smoke, no sand, no magical particles, no digital glitch graphics, no scanlines" (99/128 prompts carry fade or dissolve negations). For particle-prone changes the prompt adds: "This generative granulation remains attached to the transforming surface. NO airborne particles. NO dust cloud."
9. **Sound marks the seam without a whoosh.** "Final transformation uses only changes in room resonance and small structural sounds", "No transformation whoosh. No digital glitch sound", and a room-tone splice placed on the change beat ("At 0:18 the room-tone loop splice is audible exactly as the plant loses its old structure").
10. **Camera as late witness.** The operator "notices movement … and turns", "catches one wall becoming box stacks, returns to the girl". Change happens partly off-axis and is discovered, which hides the morph's weakest frames. [INFERRED]

### C. What the model did with it (useful failure notes)
- **Abstract mapping gets simplified.** In 921006a9 the request was creature rhythms → corridor features. The output was a portal: the next corridor appears inside a mirror frame. That is still usable, but expect a frame-within-frame or a doorway when the mapping is abstract. [INFERRED from this one case]
- **Rooms that change *around* the viewer** come out best when the target plate is typed "FULL ENVIRONMENT … The viewer is standing INSIDE it … Never reproduce the reference as a flat composition viewed from outside". Without that line, a target plate tends to be pasted in as a flat composition. [INFERRED from the rewrite history of the opening family: 21 attempts, 1 used; the "FULL ENVIRONMENT" line appears only in 4 of the last 5 of them]
- **Long takes are not used whole when the tail fails.** The opening's hand-and-ants finale (23–30 s) was never used. The editor cut at v18, mid-return to the studio, and joined on raw noise. Plan the transition in the first 2/3 of a 30 s take.
- **Two versions per transition.** In 12–13 submission bursts (the count depends on how a multi-shot prompt is detected) the author sent a single-take version and a multi-shot version of the same scene within 2 minutes. The final cut uses both kinds: 14 single takes and 14 multi-shot prompts among the 28 final jobs. Both kinds carry transitions: E1 `c95879d3`, E2 `7a3009d9`, E5 `921006a9` and E9 `02de9b29` are single takes, but E3 `afb3d7b3`, `e2bc124d`, E4 `b2cf3c68` and E10 `da8db04f` are multi-shot prompts.
- **Rerolls are edit material, not waste.** In 5 places the cut alternates two takes of the same or a sibling prompt: afb3d7b3/e2bc124d, ed7d72ce/b2cf3c68, fc4c02b4/802b4091, cf25e94f/da8db04f, fd624233/31a82d8b.

## Prompt anatomy
### Video prompts (Cinema Studio Video 4.0, 128 jobs)
- **Settings actually used:**
  - Every job: mode `default`, model `default`, `generate_audio: true`, `bitrate_mode: high`, `speedramp: auto`, `draft: false`, `multi_shots: false`, no `multi_prompt`, no `extension_mode`, `prompt_language: en`.
  - Duration: 30 s ×97, 20 ×10, 12 ×6, 5 ×4, 10 ×3, 15 ×2, 28 ×2, 26 ×2, 22 ×1, 4 ×1.
  - Aspect: 21:9 ×109, 16:9 ×19. Resolution: 720p ×125, 1080p ×3.
  - Creative controls: `camera_lens` "Halation Vintage" on 8 jobs (the 09-10/11 opening family, including the final `7a3009d9`). `genre`, `light`, `color_palette`, `pacing` and `camera_model` were never set.
  - 47 prompts say "16:9" in the header while the job is 21:9. The aspect parameter wins.
- **Block order (most prompts):**
  1. Optional header: `30s, 16:9, single continuous take.` / `30s, 16:9, 8 connected shots.` / `10s, 16:9, 2 shots.`
  2. **Reference map.** One paragraph per image: `<<<image_n>>> is the <ROLE>: <what it shows>. <what to keep>. <what NOT to import>`. Roles in caps: `BASE WORLD only`, `STARTING LOCATION`, `TARGET LOCATION`, `OBJECT-ONLY reference: use ONLY the carpet. Do not import its room, walls, color treatment or lighting`, `HAND-ONLY`, `FULL ENVIRONMENT`, `WINDOW ONLY`, `CAT ONLY`. Some later prompts use the compact form `<<<image_1>>> = …` or a `REFERENCE MAP:` header.
  3. `Summary:` one sentence for the whole take (120/128 prompts).
  4. **Law block:** `MANIFESTATION LAW — applies to …` (35), `ABSOLUTE TRANSFORMATION LAW:` (opening family), or `IMPORTANT RULES:` (station).
  5. **Timed beats.** `0s–5s:` (125/128 prompts carry timecodes) or `SHOT n — a–b s` (32). Transitions get sub-second sub-beats, and some events get exact timecodes ("At 0:01 the left wall begins to open", "At 0:11.2 <<<image_3>>> emerges").
  6. `Lighting:` → `Color:` → `Camera & optics:` → `Audio:`. These are stable house blocks: "single practical source, no fill, underexposed by a stop, dense smoky shadows, compressed midtones, soft highlight rolloff …"; "Dye-density color, deep pigment rather than saturation, heavy muted midtones, matte finish"; "Old uncoated lens, low microcontrast, veiling glare, spherical-aberration softness, faint chromatic fringing, focus breathing. Living 16mm grain, uneven and thicker in shadows"; "no music, no BGM, no score".
  7. `Keep:` a short checklist that restates the must-happen events (127/128).
  8. `No:` a long negative list (127/128). It always includes "no HDR look, no glossy skin, no beauty lighting, no gimbal smoothness, no teal-orange, no hero framing, no fake vintage filter" plus the fade, dissolve and overlay bans.
  9. Optional `<<<movement:<uuid>>>>`: a Cinema Studio camera-movement preset: 36 tokens in 27 prompts (3 preset IDs; `eb7689cc` in 26 prompts, 6 of them final). The job data does not expose the preset names.
- **Length:** median 1,288 words and 8,473 characters (max 2,722 words / 18,593 characters). The four 5–15 s inserts in the final cut run 367–557 words (project-wide the 16 jobs of 15 s or less run 367–2,160 words). The median of the final 28 is 1,260 words.
- **Reference syntax:** `<<<image_n>>>` in 115 prompts and `@image_n` in 12 (mostly 08-30 to 09-04, both forms work). The late "Image6 hall" family also writes "Image1"/"Image6" in prose.
- **Multi-shot by text:** 36 prompts ask for internal cuts ("N connected shots", "13 shots with hard cuts", `SHOT n`). The rest are single takes.
- **Physics and realism wording:** the "operator" is a person inside the event ("reacts late", "focus misses the first foot", "almost loses the frame"). Gait is written as mechanics ("one leg advances normally, the next step is shorter, small hip correction, slight foot slide"). Anomalies are given exact counts ("exactly SIX fingers", "exactly three heads, exactly seven legs") with "The count must be unmistakable".

## Consistency system
1. One heroine image, `ae277c83`, sits in 75 of 128 takes (23 of the 28 used), and most prompts re-describe her in text (43 of those 75 mention "mannequin" or "twin-tail"; 13 add "Her face must not morph").
2. One shared look suffix sits on almost every image prompt: "Material film still …" (967/1,194). The same lens, grain, exposure and colour vocabulary is restated in the Lighting / Color / Camera blocks of 121–125 of 128 video prompts.
3. Target-to-start reference hand-off, plus frame grabs, keeps places identical across cuts.
4. **The hub plate:** the empty studio is the start state of the first four used takes and the end state of the first two (`7a3009d9`, `c95879d3`), so the cuts land on the same set.
5. Each room has one dominant hue and one accent, so a change of room is also a change of colour. This makes the rewrites read even at 720p. [INFERRED]
6. A fixed world rule (no fade, re-resolution only) appears in 99 prompts, so every transition in the film obeys the same physics.

## Adopt for our pipeline (concrete rules)
1. **Build every scene change inside one continuous take** (5–30 s), not with start/end frames or a dissolve. This fits our 05 hard rule "one shot, no internal cuts". (UNPROMPTED also got transitions out of multi-shot prompts, e.g. E3, E4, E10, but our rule keeps the change inside one shot.)
2. **The transition block for Seedance 2.5** (`omni_reference`), in this order:
   - (a) roles in the reference list: `@ImageN = STARTING LOCATION … do not import …`, `@ImageM = TARGET LOCATION … resolve AROUND the subject, never as a flat picture`, and `OBJECT-ONLY` / `ENVIRONMENT-ONLY` limits;
   - (b) a 4–6 line law: the same surface becomes the next surface; resolution in discrete steps from mass to edge to material to detail; contact shadows one step late; deep shadows stay unresolved; no fade, dissolve, crossfade, opacity or overlay;
   - (c) an **A→B mapping list** of 4–7 pairs;
   - (d) sub-second timestamps for the change (5 states over 3–8 s), placed in the first two-thirds of the take;
   - (e) the subject fixed at the same floor position, unchanged;
   - (f) sound: material sounds only, no whoosh, one sound event on the change beat.
3. **Wording for our route.** Our 05 module bans edit verbs in reference prompts (change / replace / modify / extend / continue). Write transitions with *becomes, resolves into, re-resolves, takes over, loses certainty, grows out of*. UNPROMPTED never needed "replace".
4. **Objects inside a location: the fixed-footprint carousel.** List the identities in order, use 4 states per change, add one small physical tell (flare or spark), keep objects unsynchronised, keep one control object unchanged, and stop the carousel explicitly ("After the seventh state, stop").
5. **Reference hand-off:** the TARGET plate of shot N is the STARTING plate of shot N+1 (same asset). After approval, grab a frame of the new place from the approved take and log it as `LOC_<name>_FG_vNN` for the next shot.
6. **Transition-ready plates:** for every A→B pair, also make one still where both states share a frame (seam, gradient of detail). Make new viewpoints of an approved plate with angle-change edits, not new text prompts.
7. **Hub plate:** between big set changes, return to one neutral base set, so cuts land on a known frame.
8. **Edit:** generate 2 takes of every transition (a single-take version and a connected-shots version) and intercut at matching compositions. Allowed bridges: a noise or black frame, exit door → entry door opening toward camera, wall dissolve → wall opening, walk into a medium → open inside it, a held last frame of 1–5 s, a motif match, and a flash burst of 0.4 s or less.
9. **Camera as late witness** around every transformation: "notices late", "returns to the subject". It hides weak in-between frames and keeps a documentary feel.
10. **Budget reality:** 28 of 128 videos (22%) were used and 3,396 s were generated for about 400 s on screen (8.5:1). Transition scenes needed 4–21 tries; plan 4–6 per key transition.
11. **Our S01 pool-dream crowd morph (S01-SH04, soft at about 17.5 s):** write it as an in-take re-resolution: the subject is anchored at fixed floor coordinates, the crowd and water elements re-resolve *in place*, and an A→B mapping list names what each pool element becomes. Start the 5-state ladder about 4–5 s before 17.5 s so it lands on the beat.

# Study: HUNGER, by sssacccade (https://higgsfield.ai/@sssacccade/projects/@id__9946cb19-5b9b-4238-866e-5a954264290e), 2026-11-02

- **Source type:** a Higgsfield project with public prompts and canvas, plus the finished film. The film runs 4:03 (242.8 s) as a 16:9 1280×720 master at 24 fps. It was entered in the Higgsfield AI Film Festival (`contest_key: ai_film_festival`; 3,542 views and 61 likes when downloaded) and submitted on 13 Sep 2026. The brief reads: "A surreal short film about hyperconsumption. As Lora keeps buying, Yoki feeds on what she discards — growing larger and hungrier." The end credits name a five-person team: Director Daria Mozhaeva, DOP Egor Dodonov, Production Designer Anna Dumitskaya, Editor Sofia Vasilieva, and Music and Sound by Yakov Lantsman. The project holds 427 jobs (207 Seedance 2.5 videos, 220 images) and 74 uploads: a screenplay .docx, the sound mix, three stems and 68 generated audio files. It cost 3,145.6 credits in total: Seedance 2,725.5 and Seedream 5.0 Pro 420; the Nano Banana jobs were free.
- **User's focus:** the creature blends very well with the environment. This study looks at how the creature was designed in images and then built into Seedance shots: the wording for lighting, contact and shadow, the references, scale, physics, and the `_CAST / _LOCATIONS / _SCENES / _TESTS` organisation.
- **What it achieves:** a non-human co-lead appears in about 23 of the film's 42 generated takes (25 of them carry a Yoki Element). He is a black-bodied spirit with a pale mask face, and he grows through four sizes, from 40 cm to about 270 cm. He never looks pasted in. He sits in the same dirty-green fluorescent light as the heroine, takes her wrappers into his hands, wears a cap she puts on him, walks beside her in a mall, gets stuck in a doorway, and eats a lamp, after which the room goes black. Identity holds across all four stages because the face and the ears stay recognisable while the body and the expression change. The surroundings stay consistent too: one Akihabara-like street, one shop, one metro car, one corridor and one apartment that fills with purchases over time.
- **Method step by step:** (1) cast the heroine with a 4-model bake-off (5 images: Soul Cast, Nano Banana Pro ×2, Seedream 5.0 Pro, Seedream 4.5); (2) make a turnaround per wardrobe phase; (3) design the creature on a neutral grey studio background, "photographed as if physically present on set", and pivot the design once; (4) build every location as a master plus 2–11 camera plates (2 for the corridor, 5–6 for the street, shop and mall, about 11 for the apartment), including one plate at the creature's eye level, and add *clutter-phase* plates from the same camera; (5) make one scale line-up sheet with the heroine and all four stages on one baseline in centimetres; (6) register everything as named Higgsfield Elements; (7) generate Seedream keyframes to test composition; (8) generate Seedance shots as text plus Elements (only 29 of 207 videos use an image start frame), with a fixed 12-block prompt that always contains a "CRITICAL YOKI INTEGRATION" block; (9) move the chosen takes into `_SCENES` (29 of the 42 final takes are there); (10) edit, with four mirrored shots, punch-in crops, in-camera box wipes, a split screen and fades, and lay a separate sound mix over it.
- **Prompt structure:** see "Prompt anatomy". The video prompts have a median of 1,316 words (range 247–2,991). They are capitalised spec sheets, not prose: `Seedance 2.5, N seconds, horizontal 16:9, 24 fps, hyperrealistic live-action documentary footage, one continuous shot.` → ELEMENT MAP → SCENE/SHOT FUNCTION → SHOT SIZE / CAMERA POSITION (lens mm, height cm, distance m) → CAMERA BEHAVIOR → ACTION / TIMELINE (decimal-second windows) → PERFORMANCE → FOCUS / OPTICS → LIGHTING → CRITICAL YOKI INTEGRATION → SOUND / AUDIO → HARD CONSTRAINTS (a recap).
- **Asset method:** see "Focus" sections A, B and F. There are 40 Elements (41 names, because `prop_rekvizit` was renamed `prop_rekvizit_1` on 23 Aug): 11 character, 16 environment and 13 prop, by Higgsfield category. All of them were generated in this project: 186 Seedream 5.0 Pro images, 24 Nano Banana Pro Unlimited images (18 props, 6 keyframe tests) and 7 Nano Banana Pro tests.
- **Adopt:** see "Adopt for our pipeline". The core of it is a creature bible of 4 asset types plus a pasted integration block, scale anchors and a gait block.
- **Do not copy:** Yoki's design (black body, pale mask face, bear ears, giant belly, ribs), Lora, the 2003 Akihabara world, the consumerism plot, the pink bag and cap, the Nokia, the lamp and phone-light ending, the split-screen shopping frenzy, and the closing street of paired creatures. Take the method only.
- **Proposed skill changes:** see the last section.

Warm tungsten practicals (one lamp per room) are the only warm colour. The late day scenes switch to flat overcast daylight with "ALL fluorescent fixtures are OFF".

There are foreground occluders (racks, boxes, bags) and close inserts of hands and wrappers. The creature is often framed in pieces: a head in foreground blur, a body behind Lora's leg, a back in a doorway.
- **Pacing:** `cuts.txt` holds 41 raw cut detections (37 after merging detections less than 0.5 s apart; median segment 4.35 s). The real count is about 45 takes, because the 1:27–1:59 block hides 4 takes behind box wipes and the split screen runs about 28 s. The longest single takes are 12–20 s (the box ritual, the lamp, the POV).
- **How the creature reads on screen:** stage 1 looks like a practical puppet. His matte black body sinks into the room's shadows, and the glossy black eyes pick up the fluorescent tubes as real catchlights. His pale face is the only bright plane, and it is lit by the same source as Lora's skin. Stages 2 and 3 keep a smoky, faded lower body from the design sheets (visible at 1:48–1:59), which hides imperfect foot contact. Stage 4 has solid legs, ribs and a huge belly, and it is shown only by one lamp and then by one phone beam, so most of it stays in darkness.

## Focus: creature design and integration (how Yoki blends into the world)

### A. How the creature was designed (image jobs)
1. **The first attempt was rejected** (Nano Banana Pro, `_TESTS`, 15 Aug 09:13–11:52, `ab38a52d` / `d24d3875` / `0cd3625c` / `4fe60f2d`). It was a pale, dough-skinned "household spirit … inspired conceptually by Japanese gaki (hungry ghost), zashiki-warashi (domestic house spirit), and tsukumogami (spirit connected to objects)", with the instruction "do not make it black". The four outputs came out as a beige alien or a lumpy dough figure. Two details from that brief survived into every later design:
   - the belly as the defining feature ("a distinctly round, soft, slightly swollen belly … should already hint at insatiable appetite");
   - physical weight written into the design sheet itself ("the body should feel physically plausible and slightly weighted, with soft compression where it touches the floor"; "photographed as if physically present on set").
2. **The pivot came 10 hours later in Seedream 5.0 Pro** (`fd50aa30` test and `64023fc3` `_CAST`, 15 Aug 22:19). The pale `d24d3875` was kept only as a loose image reference, and the text defined a new design: "dark charcoal-black to smoky-black body with a soft matte surface, a large pale mask-like face, huge glossy black eyes, a tiny mouth, and small rounded bear-like ears … The lower part of the body should feel slightly airy and ghostlike, with a faint smoky softness around the legs and feet". This is exemplar E1. Why it blends, as I read it:
   - **A dark matte body has almost no specular highlights to betray CG**, and it takes on whatever the room's shadows are doing.
   - **The one bright plane, the pale face, sits where the practical light falls**, so it is lit exactly like a human face in the same frame.
   - **The glossy black eyes reflect the real light sources** in the room as catchlights.
   - **A soft, smoky lower edge forgives imperfect foot contact** in stages 1–3.
3. **The evolution is written into the first sheet.** Every stage prompt opens with: "The creature must remain the same character across all stages, but its face should evolve together with its appetite … Do not keep the exact same cute neutral expression across all stages." Stage prompts 3 and 4 (`f8019589`, `a49f096c`) keep "pale face mask, black eyes, small mouth, rounded ears, dark body, thin arms" and change only body mass and facial tension.
4. **The stage-3 and stage-4 redesign (20–21 Aug) used identity-preserving mutation prompts** (exemplar E3). "Use the original Yoki baby character as the STRICT identity reference … The viewer must instantly recognize: 'This is the little Yoki from earlier, but something has gone wrong.'" They also set a body contradiction: "he looks simultaneously OVERFED and STARVING", with ribs that "subtly press through the surface of his existing creature body. Do NOT give him a realistic human rib cage."
5. **Body and face were iterated separately.** `5fe47ca8` (became `char_yoki_v3`) says: "Keep the CURRENT BODY DESIGN from the latest successful version … Do NOT redesign the body. The body is already correct. The ONLY major correction is the FACE." `dda3f8a1` says: "Do not solve this only with an angry expression. The change must come from facial structure and facial volume, not just emotion … The eyes should look reduced by swollen facial mass around them."
6. **Every design image is a neutral light-grey studio sheet, but the stage-3 sheet already carries the film's camera.** `074364ec` asks for "believable contact shadows, photographic realism, slight optical softness, low contrast, mild early-digital noise … early-2000s Tokyo, dirty-green contamination". The final stage-4 Element (`959750fd`, 190 words, which even contains the Russian word "злые", meaning "angry") is the gnarled, ribbed figure seen in the POV finale.
7. **One Element per stage, and the stage's size is carried by text plus the scale sheet, not by the picture.** `char_yoki_v2` (stage 2, "about 90 cm") is `b9ff4ca4`, a portrait-format re-render of the *same* stage-1 prompt. The 40 cm and 90 cm creatures therefore have the same proportions, and only the scale lines in the prompt and `char_razmer` separate them. Element images were also swapped mid-production without renaming: `char_yoki_v4` pointed to `a49f096c` (the obese giant), then to `661d9f36` (3 jobs on 27 Aug), then to `959750fd`; `char_yoki_v3` pointed to `f8019589`, then to `5fe47ca8`. The same happened to `prop_sumka-serebryanaya` (`eb1475a9` → `38cd3913` on 2 Sep) and `loc_flat-empty` (`033f0839` → `bad69ad7` → `202df0d1`).

### B. The scale system (why a 40 cm and a 270 cm creature both look right)
1. **A scale line-up sheet registered as an Element** (`01baf6e0`, `_CAST`, 2720×1536, Element `char_razmer`, used in 24 jobs; exemplar E2). It shows the heroine (165–170 cm) between the four stages (40 / 90 / 160 / 260–280 cm), "All figures should stand on the same floor baseline … No perspective distortion", and it repeats her full outfit list. Video prompts attach it with "Use <<<…>>> only as the scale-continuity reference. Yoki in this shot must remain approximately 40 cm tall relative to Lora." (17 prompts carry "only as the scale-continuity reference".) **Caveat [MEASURED from the image, about ±10%]:** the rendered sheet does not hit its own numbers. Measured against Lora at 165–170 cm, stage 1 stands about 70 cm (asked 40), stage 2 about 105 cm (90), stage 3 about 135 cm (160) and stage 4 about 200 cm (260–280). The sheet fixes the order and rough ratios; the cm and body-landmark lines in each video prompt carry the real scale.
2. **Centimetres plus a landmark on Lora's body, in every shot.**
   - Stage 1: "Yoki’s head should reach approximately Lora’s mid-shin / just below knee"; "The trouser leg … is clearly visible next to him. This establishes his small scale."
   - Stage 2: "his head should reach approximately around her hip / lower waist area"; and in the box ritual, "IF LORA AND YOKI WERE BOTH STANDING UPRIGHT, THE TOP OF YOKI'S HEAD WOULD REACH APPROXIMATELY LORA'S LOWER RIBCAGE, SLIGHTLY ABOVE HER WAIST, BUT CLEARLY BELOW HER CHEST."
   - Stage 3: "Yoki's head reaches approximately Lora's head level."
   - Stage 4: "He is almost touching the ceiling. His head and upper body sit only a small distance below the ceiling line."
3. **Anti-shrink clauses for every trap the model falls into:**
   - "DO NOT REDUCE YOKI'S PHYSICAL SIZE JUST BECAUSE THE CAMERA IS FARTHER AWAY. Lora and Yoki become smaller in frame TOGETHER";
   - "Because Yoki is SITTING during this shot: DO NOT SHRINK HIS BODY";
   - "Do NOT shrink him when he approaches Lora" (4 prompts), "Do NOT shrink him when he lunges" (4), "Do NOT shrink him near the door" (1);
   - "Do NOT shrink him to make the composition easier."
4. **Stage locks:** "THIS IS YOKI STAGE 2 … Exactly ONE Yoki exists. NO other Yoki stages. NO morphing. NO growth during this shot. NO duplicate Yoki." "Exactly one Yoki" appears in 142 of 207 prompts. In the film, only the matching stage's Element is attached, and the crowd finale is the only shot that lists several stages, each with its own size band. Across the project, 8 videos attach more than one Yoki Element: 5 crowd-finale attempts, one mall take (`7051ac35`), and two abandoned 30 Aug time-passage takes (`e172e148`, `1c548eb0`, "360-DEGREE CIRCULAR CAMERA REVOLUTIONS around Lora. Each complete revolution represents another passage of time").
5. **Architecture as a ruler:**
   - "The entrance doorway remains normal residential size. Yoki is too wide because of his stage-3 body … → both sides of torso contact doorway → forward movement stops … Subtle compression is allowed … No clipping. No wall penetration … No architecture expansion" (11 prompts carry DOORWAY PHYSICS);
   - for stage 4, "He should feel as if the apartment has become physically too small for him."
6. **Props as rulers:** "a normal HUMAN-SIZED CAP / BASEBALL CAP … Because Yoki’s head is larger than the cap, the cap does NOT properly fit on his head … It should sit awkwardly and too small … This size mismatch is important" (`cf101b57`, final at 1:15).
7. **Scale tied to meaning** (the credits street, exemplar E10): "people carrying only one or two bags often have smaller stage-1 Yoki … People visibly overloaded with many shopping bags may have large stage-3 Yoki. … Walking weight must correspond to scale. Stage 1: small, light steps. Stage 2: heavier grounded gait. Stage 3: slow heavy body movement, belly and torso carrying visible mass."

### C. Integration wording: light, contact, shadow, camera
The same block appears in 92 prompts as "CRITICAL YOKI INTEGRATION" (variants: "CRITICAL YOKI PHYSICAL INTEGRATION", "CRITICAL PHOTOREALISM / YOKI INTEGRATION"). Its canonical form, from `821e83bd`, final at 0:59:

```text
CRITICAL YOKI INTEGRATION:

<<<yoki-element>>> must look like a real physical creature captured in the same live-action apartment as everything else.

Preserve his exact identity and anatomy, but inherit from <<<location-element>>>:
the same lighting,
the same shadow direction,
the same softness,
the same grain,
the same focus response,
the same exposure,
the same early-digital compression.

Correct floor contact and contact shadow under his feet or body.

He must NOT look like:
CGI,
a rendered cartoon,
a plush toy,
an illustration,
or a pasted asset.
```

What makes it work, rule by rule:
1. **The location Element is named as the source of the photography, not only of the set.** The wording is "Use <<<loc>>> as the exact apartment **photographic-world reference**. Preserve the same cramped Tokyo apartment, mixed warm lamp light and dirty green kitchen fluorescence, slight optical softness, low contrast, early-digital noise and compression." It appears in 50 image prompts and 78 video prompts. The creature then *inherits* the look from that reference.
2. **The creature gets no light of his own.** "Use only motivated practical light … No softbox. No beauty fill. No separate key light for Yoki. He must not be specially lit. Yoki, Lora, wrappers and floor clutter must all sit naturally in the same mixed apartment light." On the street: "Do NOT add artificial rim lights or magical glow around them. They are not ghosts with transparency. They are physically present creatures"; elsewhere, "No independently illuminated edges." "motivated" appears in 151 prompts and "softbox" in 112.
3. **He is matched to the nearest human, not to an ideal.** "Match for EVERY Yoki: same exposure as nearby human, same fluorescent / storefront light, same color contamination, same shadow direction, same contact shadows, same optical softness, same motion blur, same autofocus response, same digital grain, same compression, same low dynamic range."
4. **He may never be cleaner than the room.** "Do not make his eyes or face glossy-clean compared with the room. No CGI rendering. No collectible-toy appearance. No isolated studio lighting. Nothing tack-sharp" (keyframe `f70738b6`). "Yoki is the main focus priority, but Lora and the room must still feel connected to the same optical world. Nothing should be tack-sharp. Allow slight early-digital autofocus hesitation as Yoki lowers his gaze."
5. **The camera degrades everything equally.** Every prompt asks for handheld micro-jitter, autofocus breathing, noise and compression, and bans clean overlays ("No pattern overlay. No repeating AI texture. No decorative grain grid. No camcorder interface. No timestamp. No REC icon." in about 180 prompts). The lo-fi capture layer is the glue: when the human plate and the creature share the same flaws, the eye stops separating them.
6. **The material negatives depend on the stage and the light:**
   - "plastic figurines, vinyl toys, collectible toys, mascots, rubber dolls, 3D renders" on a bright street;
   - "No CGI sheen. No plastic. No clay. No mascot appearance" in a dark room;
   - "Preserve the material / biological appearance from the exact Elements … Their texture must feel physically real under imperfect city light."
7. **The light source carries the story, and darkness carries the integration.** In the climax the lamp is the *only* light ("This lamp is the ONLY light source in the entire room … Yoki is partly illuminated by this warm practical source. Large areas of the room remain dark"). After he eats it: "TOTAL BLACKNESS … Do NOT add hidden fill light. Do NOT illuminate Yoki after the lamp is gone." Then, in the POV: "Yoki should only be visible where the phone-light beam hits him … small unstable pool of illumination, hard natural falloff, dark edges, moving shadows, brief loss of Yoki when beam drifts." A creature lit by one hard, moving, in-scene source is far easier to believe than one in even light.
8. **The creature is framed at his own eye level, with a dedicated plate.** Location plate `8b3d4dcf` (Element `kvartira-ot-pola`, "apartment from the floor"; exemplar E4): "Camera approximately 30–40 cm above the floor beside the coffee table, close to the eye level of the small spirit … The floor dominates the frame and leaves visual room for shopping bags, packaging and the creature." The video prompts say: "Camera height: low, close to Yoki’s eye level, around 45–60 cm from the floor." This plate was then re-dressed into a cluttered phase from the same camera (`abd8891e`, `kvartira-ot-pola-zakhlamlennaya`), which became the locked camera of the 4-phase time-passage block.
9. **Partial and occluded framing:**
   - "A blurred fragment of Lora’s sleeve, torso, hand or shopping bag may appear at the frame edge or in foreground, but the shot belongs to Yoki";
   - "He may remain offscreen or appear only as a small blurred foreground fragment low in frame";
   - "The camera sees Lora and Yoki through gaps between hanging clothes."
   
   Foreground occluders hide the hardest contact edges and add depth layers in which he sits.

### D. Physics: weight, gait, contact, eating
1. **A gait cycle is written out step by step for any walking shot** (`2ccad326`, final at 1:10; exemplar E7): "one foot stays planted on the floor → weight shifts onto that planted leg → opposite foot lifts completely from the floor → lifted foot swings forward → heel / sole contacts floor → weight transfers … his pelvis shifts subtly from side to side, his torso rises and falls slightly, his belly / soft body mass responds with a small natural secondary motion … He may take roughly 1.5–2 small steps for each larger Lora step." It ends with a FORBIDDEN list: "NO GLIDING. NO FLOATING. NO SLIDING. NO SKATING. NO SWIMMING THROUGH SPACE. NO CONSTANT-HEIGHT HORIZONTAL TRANSLATION. NO BODY MOVEMENT WITH FROZEN FEET. NO MOONWALK. NO TELEPORTATION. NO ROOT-MOTION SLIDE. NO ROTATING IN PLACE WITHOUT FOOT REPOSITIONING." It also says to avoid "video-game character with root-motion error, toy being dragged, puppet on invisible wheels".
2. **Contact shadows move with the feet:** "Every planted foot creates: clear floor contact, correct weight-bearing posture, a small contact shadow underneath, natural compression / grounding. His shadow must move consistently with his footsteps … Yoki must look like gravity affects him."
3. **Sitting creatures carry weight too:** "His posture is physically grounded: weight on lower body, legs naturally placed, substantial torso, hands relaxed. No floating. No hovering."
4. **Objects in his hands:** "real ground contact, real contact with the table, real hand contact with the bag, real contact shadows" (keyframe `5bb74c91`); "When he takes purchases: hands and objects must make believable contact. When he eats them: material must deform physically. No clipping. No floating objects."
5. **Inertia for big bodies:** "When he reaches for the lamp: his body movement should show believable weight and inertia. When he eats the lamp: lamp interaction must feel physical." A doorway gives "subtle believable compression against the frame. … No door deformation."
6. **Objects are consumed, not swapped:** "Yoki MUST physically take the SAME lamp. Yoki MUST physically consume the SAME lamp. After consumption: the lamp is completely gone …" and "Do NOT generate another lamp. Do NOT leave the lamp behind after Yoki eats it. Do NOT keep warm light after the lamp disappears." "Items do not regenerate. Items do not duplicate. The bag gradually becomes empty."
7. **Sound sells the physics:**
   - stage 1 eating: "small plastic crackle when Yoki touches the wrapper, soft crinkling as he lifts it, tiny wet papery chewing sounds";
   - stage 4: "Yoki’s heavy footstep … fabric tearing … restrained chewing" (pieced together from the sound lists of the stage-4 takes);
   - always: "No monster roar. No gore sounds. No horror music sting."
8. **Restrained performance keeps him real:** "Do not make him perform exaggerated cuteness … The action should feel strangely believable"; "He does NOT: stand, eat, grab, steal, jump, perform, act like a pet"; "His menace comes from: size, silence, appetite, and inevitability."

### G. Reusable rules for our film (creature or any non-human element)
1. Design the creature on neutral grey, "photographed as if physically present on set", with matte, tactile, non-glossy material words, a soft contact shadow, and a list of looks to avoid (plush, vinyl, mascot, CGI, alien). Choose a silhouette with **one bright plane** (face or mask) on a **dark, low-specular body**.
2. Make **one Element per growth stage or state**, and write in the first sheet how the face changes across stages ("evolve together with its appetite").
3. Make a **scale line-up sheet** with our human lead on one baseline, in cm, and attach it as an Element to every shot with the creature.
4. In every video prompt, state the size **three ways**: cm, a landmark on the human's body, and architecture (doorway, ceiling). Add anti-shrink clauses for wide shots, seated poses, approaches and doorways, plus "NO growth during this shot".
5. Paste the **integration block**: the location Element as the "photographic-world reference"; inherit lighting, shadow direction, softness, grain, focus response, exposure and compression; floor contact and contact shadow; the not-CGI/plush/pasted list.
6. Light only with motivated practicals in the scene. Write "No separate key light for <creature>", "no rim light / glow", and "never cleaner or sharper than the room".
7. For walking, paste the **gait cycle and the FORBIDDEN motion list**. For holding and eating, write contact plus deformation plus no-clipping. For big bodies, write weight and inertia, and doorway compression without architecture change.
8. Shoot from the creature's eye level with a **dedicated eye-level location plate**, and keep foreground occluders and partial framings in the shot list.
9. Show growth or change **between** takes, never inside one, and hide the join with an in-camera wipe or a cut.
10. Keep `_CAST / _LOCATIONS / _SCENES / _TESTS` approved-only, and promote each pick into `_SCENES` the moment it is chosen.

## Prompt anatomy

### Seedance 2.5 (207 prompts; all 16:9, mostly 720p, audio on, no draft, no multi-shot)
Block order from `821e83bd` (0:59), with counts of prompts carrying each header:
1. **Header line** (206/207): `Seedance 2.5, N seconds, horizontal 16:9, 24 fps, hyperrealistic live-action documentary footage, one continuous shot.` Variants: "… documentary POV footage" (31), "… early-digital documentary footage" (20), "… early-2000s documentary footage" (6). Some open with structure flags: "NO STARTING KEYFRAME. NO ENDING KEYFRAME." (8) or "CRITICAL STRUCTURE:".
2. **ELEMENT MAP** (198): one paragraph per Element with its role. Examples: "Use <<<…>>> as the exact Yoki identity and anatomy reference. Preserve this exact stage-1 creature completely. Do not redesign or reinterpret him." / "Use <<<…>>> as the EXACT and ONLY Lora reference … Exactly ONE Lora exists." / "Use <<<…>>> as the exact apartment photographic-world reference." / "Use <<<…>>> … only as supporting prop references" / "only as the scale-continuity reference". Each Element gets a *job* (identity / world / prop / scale), and secondary ones are explicitly limited ("only for contextual continuity", "only as continuity reference for Lora", "for YOKI IDENTITY ONLY").
3. **STARTING FRAME** (25) and **SCENE FUNCTION** (157) or **SHOT FUNCTION** (40). These describe what the shot does in the story ("This shot shows the first active behavioral shift in Yoki").
4. **SHOT SIZE / CAMERA POSITION** (141): shot size, lens in mm, camera height in cm, distance in m, angle, and "This must be a genuinely new camera point from the previous insert shot of hands."
5. **CAMERA BEHAVIOR** (159): "Handheld documentary camera. No stabilization. No gimbal. No dolly. No artificial push-in. No zoom. Natural handheld behavior only: small body sway, micro-jitter, tiny framing corrections, slightly imperfect operator response. The shot should feel found, not designed." Complex moves get **phases**: "PHASE 1 … CAMERA IS STATIC … PHASE 2 … THE EXACT MOMENT LORA BEGINS OPENING THE BOX … LEFT → RIGHT … It does NOT pan right from one fixed point. It physically TRAVELS sideways."
6. **ACTION / TIMELINE** (197; decimal windows in 205): "0.0–1.8 s: … 1.8–3.2 s: … 3.2–4.8 s: … 4.8–6.0 s: … End while Yoki continues chewing". The last window usually says how to end [INFERRED from the exemplars, not counted].
7. **PERFORMANCE**, split into **YOKI PERFORMANCE** (74) and **LORA PERFORMANCE** (60). Each lists the emotion as plain adjectives and then a "Do not" list.
8. **FOCUS / OPTICS** (179): depth of field matched to shot size ("Because this is a WIDE environmental shot: use moderately deep natural focus … No creamy commercial bokeh"), plus autofocus behaviour.
9. **LIGHTING** (162): practicals only, naming the source, plus the no-softbox / no-beauty-key list.
10. **CRITICAL YOKI INTEGRATION** (90+): see Focus C.
11. **SOUND / AUDIO** (198 counting "SOUND"): "No dialogue. No voice-over. No music." (201 prompts say "No music"), then a sync-sound list in event order. The only scripted line is a frightened "Ой" ("oh") [INFERRED from the prompts; the audio was not heard]. "Ой" appears in 15 prompts: 7 ask for it (4 as "Only one small involuntary: “Ой.”") and 8 ban it ("NO “Ой.”").
12. **HARD CONSTRAINTS** (198): a recap of duration, take count, stage and scale, the camera rule, the action order as an arrow chain, then the overlay negatives.

**Reference naming:** Elements appear as `<<<uuid>>>` tokens, a median of 7 per prompt. Attached video references (85 uses) are **never named in the text**: they are silent continuity anchors. Only 8 of the 85 point to a take of the same prompt (6-word-shingle overlap above 0.3); the rest point to a different shot, mostly a neighbouring shot of the same scene [INFERRED from the final takes: `4c18bfb8` → `561e0c00`, `4a00115a` → `821e83bd`, `cf101b57` → `2ccad326`, `fd659819` → `e7e5dab8`]. One same-shot example is the POV re-run `297b16b9` using the `_SCENES` take `a3734bed`. **43 video prompts (and 5 image prompts) contain an empty Element slot** ("Use  as the EXACT apartment …", "Use ONLY  as …", "room from  toward the entrance"), and 27 video prompts end with an appended token. Pasting prompts loses inline tokens, so check them before you submit.

**Durations:** shots are written to the second. 5–6 s for inserts and reactions, 8–12 s for observed actions, 15–20 s for one-take set pieces (the lamp, the POV, the crowd). 30 s was tried twice and not used.

**Edits and extensions:** there are 3 `video_edit` jobs. Each takes an existing take (the metro wide twice, the apartment once) and asks for a new close-up of it, and none was used. There is no `video_extension` and no multi-shot. Reshoots are fresh generations with the previous take attached as a video reference.

## Consistency (how the creature, the heroine and the places held)
1. **Elements in every prompt**: 199/207 videos, median 3, and **one Element per state**: 4 Yoki stages, 6 Lora wardrobes, and location phases (clean / cluttered / empty / daylight).
2. **The ELEMENT MAP gives each Element one job and forbids mixing**, for example "Use ONLY <<<…>>> as the EXACT and ONLY Yoki identity and anatomy reference … NO other Yoki stages."
3. **The location Element is the look reference**, because most plates were pre-graded with the global style block. The style is therefore carried by the image, and the text only reinforces it. Check of the 18 environment Elements' source images: the 8 plates from 15 Aug carry the pasted VISUAL STYLE BLOCK; the 16 Aug clutter plates (`a482942a`, `abd8891e`) and the street master only say "Use the global visual style block" without pasting it; the mall plate (`b70e6ee5`, 80 words) has neither; and the 9 Sep late plates (daylight, empty flat, day corridor) use their own longer look text instead of the block.
4. **Video references are used as silent continuity anchors** (67 jobs). Most point to a neighbouring shot rather than to an earlier take of the same shot (only 8 of 85 uses are same-prompt re-rolls) [INFERRED purpose: cut-to-cut continuity].
5. **Image start frames were dropped after the first week.** The first 19 videos (17–25 Aug) mostly started from Seedream keyframes (12 with an image reference). From 27 Aug, shots are mostly text plus Elements, with 0–7 image refs per day. Only 6 of the 42 final takes have an image reference.
6. **Prop continuity blocks** ("Exactly ONE main purchase box. Correct sequence: Lora carries SAME box → sits with SAME box … No teleportation") and handbag, phone and lamp continuity blocks.
7. **The same-camera rule for time passage:** the floor-level camera, the clutter phases and the wardrobe Elements make the room's change readable.

## Adopt for our pipeline (concrete rules)
1. **Creature bible before any video.** It holds four asset types, each as a named Element:
   - a stage or state sheet per look, on neutral grey, "photographed as if physically present on set";
   - a scale line-up with our lead, on one baseline, in cm;
   - a location plate at creature eye level for every set where the creature appears;
   - clutter or damage phases of the same plates from the same camera.
2. **The Seedance block order for creature shots:** Header → ELEMENT MAP (one job per Element, with "only as …" limits) → SHOT FUNCTION → CAMERA POSITION (mm / cm / m) → CAMERA BEHAVIOR → ACTION / TIMELINE (decimal seconds; the last window says how to end) → <CREATURE> PERFORMANCE → FOCUS / OPTICS → LIGHTING (practicals only) → **CRITICAL <CREATURE> INTEGRATION** → SOUND (sync list; contact sounds) → HARD CONSTRAINTS (recap). Store it in `seedance-action.md` as the creature template.
3. **The integration block is pasted verbatim** and adapted by name only (Focus C).
4. **A scale lock in three units** (cm + body landmark + architecture) plus anti-shrink clauses plus "NO growth during this shot" plus "Exactly ONE <creature>". Attach the scale sheet Element whenever two sizes could be confused.
5. **A motion lock** for any non-human walker: the gait cycle and the FORBIDDEN list (Focus D1).
6. **A contact lock** for every hand or prop interaction: contact, deformation, no clipping, no floating objects, no regeneration.
7. **A light lock:** motivated practicals only; no separate key, rim or glow on the creature; the creature never sharper or cleaner than the room. Where the story allows, use **one hard in-scene source**: a lamp, a torch, a phone.
8. **A capture layer** the same in every prompt (softness, autofocus breathing, noise, compression), with the overlay bans (no REC, timestamp or grain grid).
9. **Change between takes, not inside one.** Use in-camera wipes (a foreground object reaching 100% of the frame) or cuts to move between states.
10. **Folders:** `_CAST`, `_LOCATIONS`, `_PROPS`, `_SCENES` (picks only), `_TESTS`, `_POSTER`, `_SCRIPT`, `_AUDIO/{mix,stems,sfx,music}`. Iterate in ROOT, and promote picks to `_SCENES` at once. Name Elements `char_<name>_s<stage>_v<n>`, `loc_<place>_<angle>_<phase>`, `prop_<item>`.
11. **Before you submit,** check that every `<<<token>>>` survived the paste. 43 HUNGER video prompts (and 5 image prompts) had an empty slot.
12. **Keep the creature's performance small:** no pet behaviour, no roar, menace from scale and stillness. Use foreground blur, partial framing and eye-level cameras to sell presence.
